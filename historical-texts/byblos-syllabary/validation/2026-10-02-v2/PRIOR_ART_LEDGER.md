| A11b | "The later OCBI working inventories **independently** move in exactly the direction demanded by the external control" | **HUB, UNSUPPORTED** / VERIF | The upstream README says the opposite about what these objects are: "To offer room for alternative decodings, we avoid prematurely mapping the glyphs … Only in a second step these variants are grouped together. **This allows quickly changing the grouping to test out hypotheses.**" Its status table marks Byblos *Grouping* as "**(✓)**" — the only parenthesised entry among seven scripts. `Syl1`–`Syl9` are therefore switchable hypothesis sets, not a progression of improving editions; `syl5` is even *named* "Syl4", a duplicate that fits scratch data and not an edition sequence. And since GEAS published `E4AF` = ATON in 2021 and Schmutz & Mäder assign `E416` = ʕ, the most economical explanation of the `Syl6`+ split is that it **encodes the same name alignment** — in which case "independent convergence" is the same evidence counted twice. The Hub's argument needs the word "independently" and has not earned it. |
| A12 | "Vita & Zamora (2018) were right to call the non-Dunand material heterogeneous and problematic" | PUB, **UNDERSTATED** / VERIF | V&Z p. 89 actually write that the Colless/Garbini texts are "a much more heterogeneous and problematic ensemble", and go further: "Even if we were to accept their **very dubious identification as Byblos script**, the differentiation and configuration of each of its signs, and their alleged relationship with the graphic material extracted from the Byblos corpus, seem quite often — and at the very least — **highly speculative**." They also date the seal to the **mid-14th century BC** on Garbini's own account and say documents from outside Byblos proposed as Byblos script "are few and **highly dubious**". The Hub's paraphrase drops the specific judgment on the specific object everything rests on. |
| A13 | "The name alignment makes that classification much stronger" | **HUB, UNSUPPORTED** | The alignment is evidence that *if* these signs are Byblos script they encode those names. V&Z's objection is a graphic one — whether these sign forms belong to the Byblos signary at all — and a successful name fit does not address it. Schmutz & Mäder's own §1 exo-/endo-referential test (GEAS guidelines) is the standard here, and strengthening a premise with the fit it licenses is the endo-referential move that paper rejects. |
| A14 | The corpus holds "18 `BYBL` and 14 `BYBL?`" witnesses | **WRONG** / VERIF | Upstream has 43 fragment entries: 29 `BYBL` + 14 `BYBL?` by entry, **23 + 13** distinct objects. | B6 | **The two-phase diachronic model: older core + later linearized terminal phase, replaced by Phoenician; one date must not be assigned to the whole script** | **PUB** | **The claim's conceptual core is V&Z 2018's published synthesis.** They write: "the attested variants seem to indicate some **diachronic development**"; "the noticeable graphic variants … and the existence of documents that might be evidence of **later stages or derivations** of the script (such as … 'Linear Pseudo-Hieroglyphic') suggest a **relatively long period of use**"; "the Byblos script could have preceded the Phoenician until the Phoenician eventually prevailed"; and — nearly the Hub's Phase A bullet verbatim — "the Byblos script may well have been used for a significantly long period of time, mainly for **practical purposes on probably perishable material, which would have left few traces**". The Hub cites V&Z and does not hide this, but "Before this pass, the Hub problem framed the chronology largely as an open choice … The better framing is now" describes the Hub's own earlier framing, not the field's. |
| B7 | V&Z already make the Hub's KAI 3 argument | PUB / VERIF | V&Z p. 90: "it is not clear whether they were both simultaneously in use or whether the latter script was used on this object, when its surface was no longer legible … it is not easily deduced how long before it had been engraved in the Byblos script". |
| B8 | Using the early royal Byblian sequence to resist Sass's lower dating | PUB | Rollston 2008 is titled *"The Dating of the Early Royal Byblian Phoenician Inscriptions: A Response to Benjamin Sass"*; Lemaire 2006 argued the 10th-century placement of Abibaal and Elibaal. | B9 | "Sass's overlaps are evidence for lateness of some forms, not necessarily lateness of the whole script" | **HUB** | The sharpest formulation in either file, and I did not find it put this way in V&Z, Rollston or Lemaire. | B10 | The phase-stratified sign-form test that would promote or kill the model | **HUB, NOT RUN** | Proposed in §5 of CHRONOLOGY and re-proposed in HANDOVER as "best next experiment". Never executed, here or anywhere in the folder. |
| B11 | The cylinder seal's bearing on the chronology | **ABSENT** | CHRONOLOGY.md never mentions the cylinder, and KERNEL never mentions the chronology. The Hub's two standing advances are unintegrated on exactly the parameter criterion 3 scores, and the anchor object has no phase assigned in the model. |
