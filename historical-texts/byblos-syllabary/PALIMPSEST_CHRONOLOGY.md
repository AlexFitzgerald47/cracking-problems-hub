# Byblos Script Palimpsest Chronology

## Result in one sentence

The apparent choice between a **Middle-Bronze Byblos script** and a **ninth-century invention** is probably a false one: the surviving evidence is better explained by a **diachronic tradition with an older pictographic/logosyllabic core and a later, increasingly linear terminal phase that was physically superseded by alphabetic Phoenician**.

This does **not** decipher the script and does **not** prove that every core inscription belongs to 1900–1600 BCE. It does, however, make a ca. 900 BCE *invention* of the whole system substantially harder to sustain without redating the early royal Phoenician sequence and discounting several independent-looking pieces of chronological structure at once.

---

## 1. The crucial observation: stop assigning one date to the whole script

The dating literature often treats “the Byblos script” as if the extant documents were a single synchronic corpus. The material itself warns against that assumption.

Vita & Zamora (2018) explicitly note:

- the texts may have been produced in different periods;
- graphic variants may reflect chronological development as well as medium/scribe;
- the so-called **Linear Pseudo-Hieroglyphic** inscription was already understood by Dunand as an *evolved and simplified* form closer to the Phoenician linear tradition;
- variants and possible derived forms suggest a relatively long period of use;
- the palimpsests show the Byblos script being physically replaced by Phoenician.

The simplest synthesis is therefore not “1900 BCE or 900 BCE?” but:

> **Phase A — core tradition:** the fuller pictographic/sign-rich system, plausibly developed in the Bronze-Age Egyptian–Byblian scribal environment.
>
> **Phase B — terminal tradition:** linearized/derived variants surviving later, close enough in time and graphic environment to early Phoenician for some sign-form convergence or borrowing.
>
> **Replacement:** alphabetic Phoenician becomes the preferred monumental script; old Byblos-script surfaces are erased/reused.

This is a hypothesis, but it has the important advantage of making different classes of evidence point in the same direction instead of forcing one class to invalidate all the others.

---

## 2. What the palimpsests actually prove

### Strong physical result

Martin's detailed readings of erased texts were criticized by Sass (2005), who could not reproduce all of them. That criticism matters. But the modern reinspection by Vita & Zamora is more discriminating: they judge the existence of Byblos-script signs **clear at least beneath the Yehimilk inscription and on one side of the Azarbaal spatula**. They conclude that, at minimum, one script succeeded the other at Byblos.

Thus the safe chronological relation is:

`Byblos-script layer  ->  erasure/reworking  ->  Phoenician overtext`

What is *not* proved is how many years separate the layers or whether both scripts remained readable simultaneously.

### Current catalogue dates for the overtexts

The current DEAPS catalogue assigns:

| Object | Relationship to Byblos script | Current palaeographic date |
|---|---|---:|
| KAI 3, Azarbaal bronze spatula | Phoenician written over an older pseudohieroglyphic text | 1000–975 BCE |
| KAI 4, Yehimilk | Phoenician cut over a faded pseudohieroglyphic inscription | 950–920 BCE |
| KAI 5, Abibaal | Early royal Phoenician on statue base of Sheshonq I | 940–930 BCE |
| KAI 6, Elibaal | Early royal Phoenician on bust of Osorkon; Elibaal is son of Yehimilk | 920–900 BCE |

These dates are not four independent radiometric anchors; KAI 3–6 are partly tied together by palaeographic reasoning. They therefore must not be multiplied as independent evidence. But the sequence has more structure than a free-floating typology:

1. **KAI 5 and KAI 6 are carried by monuments of successive Egyptian pharaohs, Sheshonq I and Osorkon I.**
2. DEAPS notes that the two Byblian reigns should therefore be chronologically proximate if the monuments were contemporary acquisitions.
3. The KAI 6 wording says Elibaal had the Osorkon statue made/commissioned, which weakens (without eliminating) the escape route that the Egyptian object was merely an ancient heirloom.
4. KAI 6 names Elibaal as the son of Yehimilk, linking the Egyptian-object sequence back to the king whose inscription physically overwrites Byblos script.
5. Lemaire's 2006 re-examination was prompted specifically by Sass's lower dating and argued for a tenth-century placement of Abibaal and Elibaal rather than the ninth/eighth-century reduction.

So the early royal sequence is a **linked chronological chain**, not just four similar letterforms.

---

## 3. Why Sass's ca. 900 BCE invention is the part that should move

Sass (2019) makes a clean, falsifiable proposal: the pseudo-hieroglyphic script was devised around **900 BCE**, used through roughly the first two thirds of the ninth century, and replaced by monumental alphabetic inscriptions around **830 BCE**. The explicit positive support he states is only a small number of sign-form overlaps with the ninth-century Byblos alphabet, reinforced by the absence of securely historically dated monumental West-Semitic alphabetic inscriptions before the late ninth century.

The palimpsest chain exposes an asymmetry in that argument.

### If the conventional/DEAPS royal chronology is approximately right

Then a Byblos-script layer demonstrably beneath KAI 3 or KAI 4 must predate the tenth-century Phoenician overtext. A script *invented around 900* is impossible under that chronology.

### To preserve a ca. 900 invention

One must lower-date the early royal Phoenician series substantially **and** explain the pharaonic statuary relationships **and** the Yehimilk–Elibaal genealogy **and** the physical palimpsest order. None of those moves is individually impossible. The problem is cumulative: the late-origin model spends several auxiliary assumptions to save one narrow date.

### The sign-overlap evidence has a cheaper explanation

If the Byblos script had a long life, **late variants are exactly where Phoenician-looking forms should appear**. Similarity to early Phoenician can therefore date a *terminal phase* without dating the *invention* of the system.

This is the key collapse of the dispute:

> **Sass's overlaps are evidence for lateness of some forms, not necessarily lateness of the whole script.**

That distinction is especially important because the corpus already contains a candidate “Linear Pseudo-Hieroglyphic” stage described as morphologically evolved, simplified and closer to the Phoenician linear tradition.

---

## 4. Why the old 1900–1600 BCE dating should not simply be declared victorious

The opposite overreach is just as tempting. The palimpsests provide **termini ante quos**, not origin dates.

Dunand's 1900–1600 BCE horizon rests on old excavation/stratigraphic judgments; Posener and Hoch used Egyptian/hieratic morphology. Vita & Zamora favour the second millennium because of archaeological context and Egyptian graphic relationships, but explicitly say the precise date of creation and duration cannot be fixed.

Therefore this session does **not** claim:

- every known inscription is Middle Bronze;
- the sign inventory was stable for centuries;
- the late forms descend linearly from every early form;
- Phoenician itself derives from the Byblos script.

The stronger, narrower conclusion is that **a single-date model is methodologically wrong** and that the late-origin hypothesis confuses evidence for a terminal stage with evidence for genesis.

---

## 5. A falsifiable two-phase model

### Model

**A. Formation / core use**
- Bronze-Age Byblos scribal environment.
- Larger pictorial repertoire, probably syllabic or logosyllabic.
- Egyptian graphic influence strongest here.
- Everyday use may have been predominantly on perishable material, explaining sparse survival.

**B. Linearization / terminal use**
- A reduced or increasingly linearized descendant tradition persists later.
- Some sign forms converge with or influence/is influenced by the local alphabetic tradition.
- Old Byblos-script texts remain physically present on reusable objects and monuments.

**C. Replacement**
- Phoenician alphabetic writing becomes dominant in monumental use.
- At least KAI 3 and KAI 4 preserve literal stratigraphic replacement at the inscription surface.

### Prediction that can kill the model

Classify signs *without assigning phonetic values* into (i) core fourteen texts, (ii) Linear Pseudo-Hieroglyphic, and (iii) secure palimpsest traces. Then compare only diagnostic morphology.

The model predicts:

1. Egyptian/hieratic-looking forms should be proportionally strongest in the core corpus.
2. Phoenician-like linear forms should be enriched in the Linear/PALIMPSEST group.
3. The late group should preferentially use simplified variants of core signs rather than introducing an unrelated signary.

If the Phoenician-like forms are distributed uniformly across the entire core corpus, or if the alleged late forms cannot be mapped to the core sign system without subjective cherry-picking, the two-phase model weakens sharply.

This is a much higher-value next test than another attempted translation.

---

## 6. The tempting “Gubal Rosetta” claim: do not cash it yet

Martin's 1961 work is often repeated online as identifying a recurring four-sign Byblos sequence beneath/alongside later occurrences of Phoenician **GBL** (“Byblos”), potentially giving the script a rare semantic anchor.

That would be transformative if independently verified. It is **not yet safe to use as a phonetic decipherment anchor**.

Why:

- Sass could not reproduce all of Martin's erased sign sequences from the originals/photos.
- Vita & Zamora's modern photographs support the existence of the older layers, but they do not endorse Martin's full underlying transcription or the exact four-sign = GBL alignment.
- Reused surfaces create a severe confirmation-bias trap: once an investigator expects GBL, fragmentary strokes can be grouped into a desired sign sequence.

Status:

- **Older Byblos-script layer under Yehimilk / on Azarbaal spatula:** strong.
- **Martin's exact erased texts:** mixed / needs modern imaging.
- **Four-sign sequence = GBL as a semantic or phonetic anchor:** promising but unverified.

A single multispectral/RTI reimaging campaign on KAI 3 and KAI 4 could therefore be disproportionately valuable. It should test a preregistered stroke map of Martin's claimed sequence, not hunt freely for readable signs after seeing the Phoenician text.

---

## 7. Session verdict

### What changed

Before this pass, the Hub problem framed the chronology largely as an open choice between conventional Bronze-Age dating and Sass's much later proposal.

The better framing is now:

> **The date of the script's genesis, the date of its late derivatives, and the date of its replacement are three different parameters.**

The evidence already permits a directional conclusion:

- **High confidence:** Byblos-script writing physically precedes alphabetic Phoenician on at least Yehimilk and the Azarbaal spatula.
- **Moderate confidence:** the current early-royal Phoenician chronology places that replacement process in or before the tenth century BCE, not after ca. 900.
- **Moderate confidence / model:** some Phoenician-looking Byblos forms belong to a late linearized stage and should not be used to date the invention of the whole script.
- **Low-to-moderate confidence:** the core tradition began in the second millennium BCE; plausible and better contextualized than a ninth-century invention, but still not fixed to 1900–1600 by secure absolute dating.

### The leap

**Treat the Byblos signary as diachronic.** The next sign inventory should carry a *phase/medium witness label for every form* instead of merging all variants into one timeless catalogue. That one methodological change can adjudicate the dating question and simultaneously reduce false decipherment matches.

## Epistemic note

This is a **chronological model and evidence audit**, not a decipherment. The most aggressive claim here — that the ninth-century sign overlaps are late-phase evidence rather than origin evidence — is deliberately stated as a falsifiable model. It should be promoted or killed by a phase-stratified sign-form audit, not by adding another speculative translation to the literature.
