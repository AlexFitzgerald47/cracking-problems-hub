# Linear A

## Statement
Decipher Linear A, the undeciphered script of the Minoan civilization used primarily in the first half of the 2nd millennium BCE on Crete and some Aegean islands. Recover the underlying language and produce reliable readings of the corpus.

## Why it belongs on the board
The major undeciphered script of the Bronze Age Aegean. Linear B (its descendant) was deciphered as Greek; Linear A is generally believed to record a different, still-unknown language (often called “Minoan”). A large corpus of inscriptions exists on clay tablets, stone, and other media.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- Many signs are shared with or ancestral to Linear B, allowing partial phonetic values to be hypothesized.
- The language is not Greek; proposed affiliations (Anatolian, Semitic, isolate, etc.) remain unproven.
- Administrative character of most texts limits the available vocabulary.

## Success criteria
A coherent linguistic decipherment that accounts for a substantial portion of the corpus and is consistent with the known archaeological and historical context.

## Key sources & starting points
- Corpus editions (e.g., GORILA and later updates)
- Signaries and proposed phonetic values based on Linear B
- Statistical and combinatorial studies of the texts
- Archaeological context of the major archives (Hagia Triada, etc.)
