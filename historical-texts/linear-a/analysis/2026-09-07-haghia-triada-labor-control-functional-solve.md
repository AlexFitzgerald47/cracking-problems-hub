# Haghia Triada labor-control system — post-award functional solve candidate

**Session:** 2026-09-07, GPT-5.6 Sol  
**Status:** radical functional solve candidate; not a phonetic decipherment and not peer validated

## Headline

The Haghia Triada Linear A archive can be read coherently as a **labor-obligation control system** rather than as a set of isolated commodity and personnel lists.

The proposed administrative architecture is:

1. a **canonical master circuit** of accountable local entities / people / workshops / institutions;
2. **assessment / liability records** specifying how much labor or produce each owes;
3. **dispatch / allocation records** moving labor between sectors of that circuit;
4. **exception rosters** naming personnel who are missing / outstanding;
5. **repeated roster snapshots** in which the same named person can change status;
6. **reconciliation ledgers** separating contributions from debts / future payments;
7. **hierarchical arithmetic controls**: forward-declared aggregates, closing totals, and grand totals.

This is a functional decipherment of the archive's operating logic, not of the underlying Minoan language.

## 1. The 19-word sequence is best treated as a master administrative index

Davis & Valério (2020) showed that 19 recurring Haghia Triada words occur in a fixed order across 14 tablet faces; the ordering is cyclic and can run in either direction. They infer a fixed spatial circuit of entities in or around the settlement. Their sequence is:

1. DA-RI-DA
2. PA3-NI / PA3-NI-NA
3. U-*325-ZA / U-DE-ZA
4. DA-SI-*118
5. KU-ZU-NI
6. TE-KI / TE-KE
7. DA-RE
8. TE-TU
9. ME-ZA
10. RA-TI-SE / RE-DI-SE
11. WA-DU-NI-MI
12. MA-DI
13. QA-*310-I
14. PA-DE
15. *306-TU
16. *324-DI-RA
17. TA-I-*123
18. A-RU
19. KU-PA3-NU

The important leap is to interpret this fixed circuit administratively: **it functions like a master-data ordering**. Different tablets select subsets of this canonical list while preserving its order, just as modern ledgers preserve a chart-of-accounts or route order.

The cyclic and bidirectional property is naturally explained if officials could traverse the same inspection / collection circuit in either direction. The fact that Scribe 9 corrected entries back into canonical order is compatible with order being operationally meaningful, not merely stylistic.

This does not require every node to be a place. Davis & Valério already argue the set is mixed: people, groups, workshops, institutions, and perhaps places. The claim is that these are **accountable nodes in one administrative circuit**.

## 2. HT 85 is a labor dispatch sheet

HT 85 is the clearest bridge from the master circuit to actual manpower movement.

### Source side — A-DU / personnel assessment

Side a is headed `A-DU • *638 • VIR •` and then lists exactly circuit nodes 1–7 in canonical order:

- DA-RI-DA 12
- PA3-NI 12
- U-*325-ZA 6
- DA-SI-*118 24
- KU-ZU-NI 5
- TE-KE 3
- DA-RE 4
- KU-RO 66

The arithmetic exposes an administrative unit: 66 = **11 groups of 6**. The first four entries are exact multiples of six; the final three sum to 12, another two groups of six.

### Receiving / responsibility side

Side b is headed `KI-KI-RA-JA •` and contains eleven unit entries. Its securely identifiable circuit words begin with node 8 and continue through node 13 in canonical order:

- TE-TU (8)
- ME-ZA (9)
- RE-DI-SE (10)
- WA-DU-NI-MI (11)
- MA-DI (12)
- QA-*310-I (13)

Alongside these are KI-RE-TA2, QE-KA and subordinate functionary signs PA, KA and DI.

Younger had already noticed the 11 x 6 correspondence and tentatively assigned one or more six-person groups to people on side b. Davis & Valério later showed that sides a and b occupy consecutive sectors of the 19-node circuit and suggested movement of 66 people from nodes 1–7 to nodes 8–13 plus KI-RE-TA2 and QE-KA.

The functional translation that best compresses both observations is:

> **HT 85 is a dispatch / reassignment manifest for eleven six-person labor gangs.**
>
> Side a records the source liability / manpower extracted from circuit sector 1–7. Side b records the receiving, supervising, or otherwise accountable units for those eleven gangs in the next sector of the circuit.

`KI-KI-RA-JA` therefore receives a new functional candidate: **ASSIGNEES / RESPONSIBLE RECIPIENTS / ALLOCATION SIDE**. This does not claim the literal Minoan word meant any one of those English terms.

A plausible functional rendering is:

```
LABOR ASSESSMENT / DISPATCH
DA-RI-DA       12
PA3-NI         12
U-*325-ZA       6
DA-SI-*118     24
KU-ZU-NI        5
TE-KE            3
DA-RE            4
TOTAL           66

ASSIGNED / RESPONSIBLE SIDE
KI-RE-TA2       1 gang
QE-KA (+PA)     2 gangs
TE-TU (+KA,+DI) 3 gangs
ME-ZA           1 gang
RE-DI-SE        1 gang
WA-DU-NI-MI     1 gang
MA-DI           1 gang
QA-*310-I       1 gang
TOTAL          11 gangs = 66 people
```

The exact person-to-gang mapping is still inferential, but the 11-gang architecture is visible in the tablet itself.

## 3. HT 122 is the master labor-liability register

HT 122 is explicitly interpreted in the source commentary as listing places/entities and their **contributions of groups of personnel**.

It contains many members of the same canonical 19-node circuit and uses a hierarchy of arithmetic controls:

- first personnel block → `KU-RO 31`
- `KU-DA 1`
- second block → `KU-RO 65`
- final `PO-TO-KU-RO 97`

and 31 + 1 + 65 = 97.

Functional reading:

> **local contribution blocks → subtotal → adjustment/single item → subtotal → grand total.**

This makes HT 122 look like a central liability / contribution register over the same master entity system used by HT 85.

## 4. KI-RO tablets are exception / absence reports against expected rosters

Prior Hub work established the strongest current functional model for KI-RO:

> **DUE-BUT-UNFULFILLED / MISSING / OUTSTANDING.**

In personnel records the word opens a forward list of named unit entries and KU-RO closes that list by cardinality.

### HT 94

HT 94 records a large manpower account under KA-PA and separately lists five named people under `KI-RO •`, closed by `KU-RO 5`. Uchitel's contextual interpretation is that these are missing members of the workforce represented on the obverse.

It therefore behaves like a **muster plus named exception report**.

### HT 88

HT 88 contains an A-DU personnel/resource section and, after a break, `KI-RO •` followed by six named unit entries and `KU-RO 6`.

Again, the structure is naturally read as an **exception list of six outstanding/missing personnel** associated with the account.

### HT 117

HT 117 has a high-level heading containing `MA-KA-RI-TE • KI-RO •`, followed by several 1-lists under subheadings U-MI-NA-SI, SA-TA and QI-TU-NE. The first contains ten names and closes with `KU-RO 10`.

The safest functional translation is not to guess the literal value of MA-KA-RI-TE, SA-TA or QI-TU-NE. It is:

> **exception / outstanding-personnel register, subdivided by administrative category.**

This is stronger than translating KI-RO merely as 'deficit': it locates the word in a roster-control workflow.

## 5. HT 87 → HT 117 looks like repeated status tracking by the same clerk

HT 87 and HT 117 were both written by Haghia Triada Scribe 9.

HT 87 is headed `QI-TU-NE • MA-KA-RI-TE •` and contains DI-KI-SE 1 in an ordinary personnel list.

HT 117 again combines MA-KA-RI-TE and QI-TU-NE, but now the high-level record is explicitly marked by KI-RO; DI-KI-SE appears again as a unit entry inside that exception structure.

The best administrative reading is:

> **the same clerk records the same roster member / designation in the same institutional context under different account states.**

That turns the archive from a static census into a sequence of **administrative snapshots**. It is consistent with periodic muster / obligation rounds in which personnel could move between normal and outstanding status.

This is a key bridge to the 'administrative circuit' model: the canonical route need not be a one-off list. It may be a repeated inspection / levy / muster circuit.

## 6. HT 28 shows the same ontology applied to goods

Recent synthesis of Linear A administration treats HT 28 as a balance ledger: side a records contributions made to the centre; side b records debts, deficits or future payments. The same synthesis lists:

- A-DU — assessment
- KI-RO — deficit / owed
- U-MI-NA-SI — probably debt / 'owes'

This is exactly the accounting ontology required by the personnel interpretation:

```
EXPECTED / ASSESSED
        |
        +--> FULFILLED / CONTRIBUTED
        |
        +--> OUTSTANDING / KI-RO
```

The important historical claim is therefore not that goods and people use identical words in every tablet. It is that the archive applies the **same obligation logic** to grain, oil, shipments and manpower.

Humans are being administered as liabilities/contributions within the same control system as commodities.

## 7. A-KA-RU / KA-RU: a new forward aggregate anchor

Two tablets expose an additional arithmetic function that has been underused.

### HT 2

`A-KA-RU 20` is followed by quantities 17 and 3. The source commentary explicitly observes:

`20 = 17 + 3`.

### HT 97

`KA-RU ... 82` precedes a selected breakdown:

33 + 25 + 6 + 4 + 4 + 5 + 2 + 3 = 82.

The most conservative functional conclusion is:

> **A-KA-RU / KA-RU can carry a forward-declared aggregate or summary value whose components follow.**

This creates a useful structural polarity with KU-RO:

- `KA-RU / A-KA-RU N ... components` — **preposed / declared aggregate**;
- `components ... KU-RO N` — **postposed / computed total**;
- `subtotals ... PO-TO-KU-RO N` — **grand total**.

This does not prove that KA-RU literally means 'total', nor that it is morphologically related to KU-RO. The new result is the directional arithmetic function.

## 8. Reconstructed administrative schema

The archive can now be represented as a relational system:

### MASTER_ENTITY

- stable identifier / name
- canonical position in 19-node circuit
- physical / institutional location

### OBLIGATION

- entity
- commodity or labor class
- assessed amount
- administrative period / context

### DISPATCH

- source entity
- destination / responsible entity
- labor quantity
- gang count

### ROSTER

- person / designation
- supervising / institutional subgroup
- normal account state

### EXCEPTION

- person / designation
- `KI-RO` outstanding / missing state
- subgroup / reason / account category

### CONTROL_TOTAL

- predeclared aggregate: A-KA-RU / KA-RU
- closing subtotal: KU-RO
- grand total: PO-TO-KU-RO

HT 85, HT 87, HT 88, HT 94, HT 117 and HT 122 are therefore not isolated mysteries. They look like **different views over the same administrative data model**.

## 9. Historical solve candidate

The strongest post-award reconstruction is:

> **By LM IB, Haghia Triada operated a recurring labor-obligation and redistribution system organized around a canonical circuit of accountable local entities. Communities / workshops / responsible persons supplied labor obligations; administrators could move workers in standardized six-person gangs between adjacent sectors of the circuit; named recipients or responsible parties were recorded; personnel who failed to satisfy the current roster were entered individually as KI-RO exceptions; the same clerks revisited the same institutional rosters across changing states; and the system was reconciled through explicit subtotals and grand totals.**

A useful modern analogue is not 'double-entry bookkeeping' and not a simple census. It is closer to a combined **labor levy register + dispatch board + muster/absence report + reconciliation ledger**.

The archive's core semantic structure is therefore:

```
MASTER CIRCUIT
     |
ASSESS LIABILITY
     |
MOBILISE / DISPATCH
     |
RECORD RESPONSIBLE / RECEIVING UNIT
     |
MUSTER
   /   \
OK     KI-RO = OUTSTANDING / MISSING
          |
       NAMED EXCEPTIONS
     |
KU-RO / PO-TO-KU-RO CONTROL TOTALS
```

This is the closest this project has yet come to a historically meaningful 'reading' of a substantial portion of the Linear A archive without pretending to know the underlying language.

## 10. What would make this industry-level

The high-value claim to take to specialists is not 'we deciphered Linear A'. It is:

> **The Haghia Triada archive preserves an integrated labor-control information system whose master entity circuit, assessment records, six-person dispatch units, named absence exceptions, repeated roster states, balance ledgers and hierarchical control totals can be reconstructed independently of language.**

The potentially novel pieces requiring specialist scrutiny are:

1. treating the 19-node sequence as a **master administrative index / inspection circuit**, not merely a spatial word-order phenomenon;
2. reading HT 85 specifically as an **11-gang dispatch manifest** across consecutive sectors of that circuit;
3. linking HT 87 → HT 117 as **same-scribe repeated status tracking** of DI-KI-SE;
4. integrating the KI-RO personnel lists as **exception reports** inside the same obligation architecture used by commodity balance ledgers;
5. recognizing A-KA-RU / KA-RU as a **forward-declared aggregate** complementary in document structure to postposed KU-RO.

These are claims about administrative structure and history. They do not depend on selecting Greek, Semitic, Hurrian, Luwian or any other candidate language.
