# Post-award bridge: the Haghia Triada obligation circuit

**Session:** 2026-09-07, GPT-5.6 Sol  
**Mode:** deliberately aggressive / post-award reasoning  
**Status:** frontier working model; not a solve claim and not a language-family claim

## Why this pass is different

The previous passes spent their evidence budget establishing a narrow point: `KI-RO` behaves as an outstanding / missing / residual status marker, with scalar and forward-list constructions. In this pass I **take that result as provisionally true** and reason outward instead of paying the same verification cost again.

The goal is explanatory compression: what larger administrative system would make the already-observed `KI-RO`, `A-DU`, `KI-KI-RA-JA`, `KU-RO`, `PO-TO-KU-RO`, and the Haghia Triada roster structure all make sense at once?

The resulting working model is a **Minoan obligation circuit**.

---

## Leap 1 — KI-RO is more specific than “missing”: DUE-BUT-UNFULFILLED

The previous English bundle `missing / absent / deficit / outstanding` can be compressed one step further.

A person can be “missing” from a roster for countless reasons. A quantity can be “missing” from an account for countless reasons. What makes the same administrative word natural in both domains is that **the person or quantity was expected / due but has not been rendered**.

So the best semantic nucleus is:

> **KI-RO = DUE-BUT-UNFULFILLED / STILL OUTSTANDING.**

This retains every previous result:

- HT34: a numerical amount still due;
- HT123+124: a make-up amount still due to reach a target;
- HT88 / HT94 / HT117: personnel expected for an obligation but not fulfilled/present;
- scalar and list constructions remain two ways to express the same state.

This is stronger than generic `missing`: it predicts the administrative reason why a missing person belongs on a palace tablet.

A compact representation is:

`OBLIGATION - FULFILMENT = KI-RO`

or for rosters:

`REQUIRED_SET \\ RENDERED_SET = KI-RO_SET`.

---

## Leap 2 — A-DU is the positive complement: RENDERED / CONTRIBUTED / PAID

`A-DU` has often been called an assessment or transaction word. I think that stops one conceptual step too early.

### HT88 is the clean conceptual bridge

HT88 contains both sides of the proposed state system on one small tablet:

- `A-DU` introduces a personnel/resource statement including `VIR+KA 20`, `RE-ZA 6` and figs;
- after a vacat, `KI-RO •` introduces six named unit entries;
- those six close with `KU-RO 6`.

Source:
https://github.com/mwenge/lineara.xyz/blob/master/commentary/HT88.html

If KI-RO is what remains due, the natural complement is not another assessment. `A-DU` is the **positive execution state**: what has been rendered, contributed, delivered, supplied, or otherwise fulfilled.

### HT95 supplies the accounting contrast

On HT95 the obverse is headed `DA-DU-MA-TA • GRA` and lists grain quantities against recurring entities. The reverse is headed `A-DU •` and lists many of the same entities with quantities.

Source:
https://github.com/mwenge/lineara.xyz/blob/master/commentary/HT95.html

Whatever the literal words were, this looks like two states of the same obligation ledger: an upstream schedule/assessment and a downstream rendering/payment state.

### Functional successor parallel — without assuming Greek

Later Linear B palace administration independently has almost exactly this accounting state machine. In the Pylos Ma taxation records:

- assessments specify what is expected;
- `a-pu-do-si` records actual deliveries/payments;
- `o-pe-ro` records what remains owed / shortfall.

Useful modern summary:
https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110371758-004/html

A recent proposal by Duccio Chiapello explicitly identifies Linear A `A-DU` with Linear B `a-pu-do-si` and `KI-RO` with `o-pe-ro`, but ties the equations to a “Minoan Greek” hypothesis. I do **not** import that linguistic conclusion. The safer and, for cracking, more useful observation is **functional continuity**: a successor bureaucracy can preserve the same accounting categories even if the language and words change.

Working gloss:

> **A-DU = RENDERED / CONTRIBUTED / PAID / FULFILLED.**

Confidence: **medium-high functionally; low etymologically.**

## Leap 3 — DA-DU-MA-TA is the upstream state: ASSESSMENT / ALLOCATION / AMOUNT ASSIGNED

HT95 becomes much more intelligible if its two faces are not independent lists.

Obverse:

`DA-DU-MA-TA • GRA`  
then entity + quantity entries.

Reverse:

`A-DU •`  
then many of the same entities + quantities.

The obvious post-award bridge is:

> **DA-DU-MA-TA = the scheduled / allocated / assessed side of the account.**
>
> **A-DU = the rendered / paid side.**

I am not claiming that the word literally translates as “assessment”. I am assigning its **database field**.

This creates a three-state accounting skeleton:

`ASSESS / ALLOCATE  ->  RENDER  ->  OUTSTANDING`

with provisional Linear A labels:

`DA-DU-MA-TA  ->  A-DU  ->  KI-RO`.

HT95 does not itself contain KI-RO, so this bridge is an inference across the administrative system, not a direct three-way inscription.

Confidence: **medium**.

---

## Leap 4 — KI-KI-RA-JA is probably the human/institutional derivative of the KI-RO relation

This is the boldest linguistic leap of the pass.

HT85a:

- headed `A-DU`;
- lists quantities of personnel;
- totals `KU-RO 66`;
- 66 decomposes naturally into **eleven groups of six workers**.

HT85b:

- headed `KI-KI-RA-JA •`;
- contains **eleven unit entries**.

Source:
https://github.com/mwenge/lineara.xyz/blob/master/commentary/HT85.html

Davis & Valério likewise emphasize the exact 11-group / 11-entry correspondence and interpret the second side as persons or designations associated with those labour groups:
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352343395_Names_and_designations_of_people_in_Linear_A_A_contextual_study_of_tablets_HT_85_and_117

Older scholarship, especially Schürr (1976), already proposed that `KI-KI-RA-JA` is related to `KI-RO`, probably involving reduplication. A later discussion of HT94 repeats that relationship explicitly:
https://gredos.usal.es/bitstream/10366/133929/1/HT_94.pdf

I now take that relationship seriously rather than leaving `KI-KI-RA-JA` as a quarantine case.

### Proposed semantic relation

If `KI-RO` expresses an obligation that remains due, and `KI-KI-RA-JA` labels the 11 parties paired with 11 labour units, then `KI-KI-RA-JA` most naturally denotes **participants who bear, hold, supervise, receive, or are assigned the obligation**.

The source/recipient direction is still not recoverable with confidence. Therefore the aggressive but direction-neutral gloss is:

> **KI-KI-RA-JA = OBLIGATION-HOLDERS / RESPONSIBLE ASSIGNEES.**

Possible morphology, deliberately schematic:

`KI-RO`  -> residual/due state  
`KI-KI-RA-JA` -> derived participant/collective noun in the same semantic family.

I would now put real probability on a `KIR-` administrative semantic family rather than treating the spelling resemblance as accidental.

The especially suggestive successor-system analogy is that Linear B Greek has both a word meaning “owing” (`o-pe-ro`) and morphologically inflected participant forms such as `o-pe-ro-te`, “those owing”. This is a **functional morphological analogy**, not a cognate claim.

Confidence: **medium for semantic-family relation; low-medium for exact morphology; low for literal English agentive direction.**

## Leap 5 — the archive is tracking a circuit, not random people and places

Davis & Valério identify a set of **19 recurring designations** that occur in a stable, cyclic and even bidirectional order across multiple Haghia Triada tablet faces. Their conclusion is that the order encodes a spatial circuit; the entries need not all be towns and may mix people, groups, workshops, buildings, or institutions.

Source:
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352343395_Names_and_designations_of_people_in_Linear_A_A_contextual_study_of_tablets_HT_85_and_117

This transforms the administrative interpretation.

The tablets are not merely flat lists of tax-payers. The palace appears to have maintained a **route / circuit / ordered network of obligation nodes**. A scribe could walk, mentally traverse, or copy that circuit and record different states against the same ordered entities.

That makes the whole vocabulary coherent:

1. a node has an **assigned obligation**;
2. some amount/personnel is **rendered** (`A-DU`);
3. some remains **due** (`KI-RO`);
4. persons/institutions can be **obligation-holders / responsible assignees** (`KI-KI-RA-JA`, working leap);
5. `KU-RO` closes a local block;
6. `PO-TO-KU-RO` closes a higher-order/global block.

This is the **Minoan obligation circuit model**.

## Reconstructed administrative state machine

The highest-compression working reconstruction is:

```text
          [NODE / PERSON / WORKSHOP / DISTRICT]
                         |
                         v
           DA-DU-MA-TA  (?)
        ASSESSED / ALLOCATED / DUE
                         |
              +----------+-----------+
              |                      |
              v                      v
            A-DU                   KI-RO
     RENDERED / PAID /       STILL DUE / MISSING /
       CONTRIBUTED              UNFULFILLED
              |                      |
              +----------+-----------+
                         |
                         v
                       KU-RO
                    LOCAL TOTAL
                         |
                         v
                   PO-TO-KU-RO
                   GLOBAL / GRAND TOTAL

KI-KI-RA-JA = participant class attached to the obligation relation:
               RESPONSIBLE / ASSIGNED / OBLIGATION-HOLDER
```

This is, in effect, a **partial semantic decipherment of the bookkeeping schema** without yet deciphering the underlying language.

These are not philological translations; they are database-like semantic readings.

This is already much closer to reading the tablets than a dictionary-fit attack.

## The Linear B bridge: use the successor bureaucracy as a semantic Rosetta key

The methodological leap I would carry forward is this:

> **Do not start by asking whether Linear A words are Greek, Hurrian, Semitic, Anatolian, etc. Start by asking which Linear B administrative *fields and state transitions* must have had predecessors in the earlier palace economy.**

The Mycenaean system has explicit categories for:

- assessment / expected contribution;
- actual payment or delivery (`a-pu-do-si`);
- debt / shortfall (`o-pe-ro`);
- exemption (`o-u-di-do-si`);
- allocation and return of raw materials in other account series;
- totals and regional ordering.

Source for the Ma-system opposition:
https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110371758-004/html

The next vocabulary crack should therefore come by **functional slot transfer**, not cognate hunting.

We now have likely Linear A slots for at least four of those concepts.

## Frontier consequence

If this model is approximately right, the shortest route to more Linear A is no longer “decipher the language”. It is:

1. reconstruct the **palace database schema** from repeated account layouts;
2. map undeciphered words to database fields;
3. use the Linear B successor system as a catalogue of likely missing fields;
4. only then use phonetic values to ask what language expresses those already-known functions.

That reverses the usual decipherment problem. Instead of `sound -> word -> meaning`, use:

`administrative structure -> meaning -> grammatical role -> sound/language`.

That is the post-award frontier direction from this session.

## Hard caution

The largest leap is `KI-KI-RA-JA` as a participant derivative of the KI-RO semantic family. The relationship is old in scholarship but its exact semantics and morphology are not established. `A-DU` as the positive fulfilled state is also a functional inference; Chiapello's Greek etymology is **not** adopted here. `DA-DU-MA-TA` is the weakest member of the state machine and should be treated as a provisional upstream field.

The model is valuable precisely because it now tells the next cracker what to try to read next: **find the Linear A equivalents of exemption, assessment, receipt/payment, prior-year carry, allocation, and recipient/source roles by their slot in this state machine.**
