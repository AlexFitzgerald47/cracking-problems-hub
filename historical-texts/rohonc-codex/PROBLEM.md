# Rohonc Codex

## Statement
Decipher the Rohonc Codex: identify the script, the underlying language (or encoding), and produce a coherent reading of the text. Explain the relationship between the text and the accompanying illustrations.

## Why it belongs on the board
A genuine historical codex written in an unknown script. Contains religious and military illustrations. Has resisted decipherment despite multiple serious attempts. Provenance is Hungarian / Central European.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- Various claims of solution (Hungarian, Romanian, artificial language, cipher, etc.) none of which has gained broad acceptance.
- The script has a large character set.
- Codicological and art-historical studies provide chronological and cultural constraints.

## Success criteria
A reproducible decipherment that yields meaningful, consistent text across multiple pages and aligns with the illustrations and historical context.

## Key sources & starting points
- High-quality images of the codex
- Existing character inventories and statistical studies
- Scholarly literature on its provenance and previous decipherment attempts

## Restatement (appended 2026-09-27)

This is not a blank-slate unknown-script problem. Levente Zoltán Király and Gábor Tokai,
“Cracking the code of the Rohonc Codex,” *Cryptologia* 42(4), 285–315 (2018), DOI
`10.1080/01611194.2018.1449147`, argues that the manuscript uses a code system rather than a
substitution alphabet and presents multiple interlinear readings. The article explicitly leaves
advanced morphology, syntax, the author's possible language or languages, fuller contents and a
glossary for later work. Benedek Láng's 2021 monograph provides the larger historical and
cryptological context.

The Hub therefore treats Rohonc as a **published partial decipherment requiring independent
replication and scope adjudication**, not as solved and not as never studied. Its success criterion
still requires consistent coverage across multiple unseen pages plus illustration and historical
alignment. The first Hub task is to reproduce the Király–Tokai segmentation and codebook on a
declared training subset, then score held-out pages and illustration links against negative
controls. Building a fresh transcription or running undirected frequency statistics comes later
only if that replication identifies a concrete defect.
