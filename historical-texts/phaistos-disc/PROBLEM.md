# Phaistos Disc

## Statement
Decipher the Phaistos Disc: determine the nature of the script (syllabic, logosyllabic, etc.), the underlying language, and produce a reading of the text on both sides.

## Why it belongs on the board
Unique clay disc from Minoan Crete (Middle or Late Bronze Age) stamped with a spiral of undeciphered symbols. One of the most famous undeciphered artefacts in archaeology. No other example of the script is known.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- 241 tokens using 45 distinct signs.
- Numerous proposed decipherments exist; none has achieved consensus.
- Archaeological context provides chronological and cultural boundaries.
- The uniqueness of the object makes statistical approaches difficult.

## Success criteria
A decipherment that is internally consistent, respects the physical layout, and is plausible within the known linguistic landscape of Bronze Age Crete and the Aegean.

## Key sources & starting points
- High-resolution images and drawings of both sides
- Sign lists and previous transcriptions
- Archaeological literature on the disc’s discovery and context
- Comparative work on Linear A and Cretan hieroglyphic
