## 1. Prospective predictions about corpora not yet seen (P1–P3)

The Disc has 45 signs, which is in the syllabary range (Cypriot ~55, Linear B ~87), and the
standard inference is that it is a syllabary. Its 61 groups are 2–7 signs long, mean 3.967,
with **zero one-sign groups**. If the groups are words of a natural language written
syllabically, that is anomalous, because function words are short in every natural language.

- **P1.** In a real Bronze/Iron Age Aegean syllabic corpus (Linear B and/or Linear A word
  tokens, syllabograms per word), **≥ 5 % of word tokens will be 1 syllabogram long.**
  *Failure condition:* < 5 %. If it fails, the "zero monosyllables" observation carries no
  weight and I will say so.
- **P2.** The Disc's mean group length (3.967) will **exceed the comparison corpus's mean
  word length by ≥ 1.0 signs.** *Failure condition:* gap < 1.0.
- **P3.** Drawing 10,000 size-matched samples of 61 word tokens from the comparison corpus,
  **fewer than 1 % of samples will contain zero 1-sign words**, and fewer than 1 % will have
  a mean length ≥ 3.967. *Failure condition:* either rate ≥ 1 %.

If P1–P3 hold, the consequence I commit to drawing is narrow and it is **not** a
decipherment: the Disc's groups **are not single words of a natural language written in this
syllabary**. They are multi-sign units — phrases, name+title compounds, or something not
lexical at all. That falsifies, quantitatively rather than aesthetically, every published
reading that maps one group to one word of running prose.

If P1–P3 fail I will report the genre test as dead and keep only the flatness result.

## 2. Exploratory hypothesis, with its failure condition fixed in advance (H1)

**H1 — the oblique stroke marks a section end, and the section-final slot is formulaic:**
words carrying the oblique stroke repeat among themselves far more than unmarked words do.

Statistic: `S = (marked tokens) − (distinct marked types)`, the duplicate excess in the
marked set. Null: permute *which words carry the oblique stroke*, holding the word sequence
and the word multiset fixed — permuting exactly one thing, per `board/PRACTICES.md`.

Two nulls, both to be reported:
- **N1** free label permutation (18 of 61);
- **N2** label permutation **stratified by group length**, because short groups repeat more
  easily by chance and marked groups are slightly shorter. N2 is the one that counts.

**Failure condition, fixed now: if p > 0.01 under N2, H1 is not established and I will
report it as not established**, whatever N1 says and whatever the pattern looks like by eye.

## 3. Ablation predicted in advance (A1)

The sources print an oblique stroke on **A24** which high-resolution images show to be a
crack (noted in the Wikipedia transcription preamble; I have not seen the images). A24 is
`06-18-17-19`, a non-repeating group. **Prediction:** dropping A24 from the marked set leaves
S unchanged at its current value and *raises* the marked-set redundancy rate, so the
conclusion must not depend on A24. *Failure condition:* if removing A24 weakens the N2
p-value past 0.01, the result rests on a crack and I will report that instead.

## 4. Search budget for the exploratory part

Hypotheses of H1's shape that I could have tested on this corpus, enumerated before testing:
the label is one of {oblique-marked, side, spiral-turn, paragraph-initial,
paragraph-final-without-oblique}; the response is one of {duplicate excess, longest repeated
n-gram, type/token ratio, initial-sign identity, final-sign identity}. That is **5 × 5 = 25**
combinations, and I tested the one that looked best by eye. A Bonferroni-style correction at
25 means **p < 0.0004 is needed for H1 to clear the budget at a nominal 0.01**. I commit to
reporting both the raw and the budget-charged figure, and to calling H1 *suggestive but not
established* if it lands between them.
