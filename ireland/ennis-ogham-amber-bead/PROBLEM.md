# Ennis ogham amber bead (I-CLA-003 / CIIC 53)

## Object

A perforated amber bead, c. 2.1 cm in diameter, traditionally associated with Ennis, Co. Clare and now in the British Museum (1888,0719.119). It was documented in 1856 as an inherited amulet used for sore eyes and associated with safe childbirth. The date of the bead and the date of inscription are both unknown.

## Crack target

Establish, from the physical marks before language-fitting:

1. what strokes and stemline branches are actually intentional;
2. which reading path and direction are physically justified;
3. which signs are ordinary ogham, forfeda, damage, decoration, or non-phonetic marks;
4. only then, whether the resulting sequence encodes a name, ordinary Irish, a learned/cryptic ogham convention, an amuletic formula, or no recoverable linguistic text.

A successful crack must explain the anomalous terminal structures and the fork around the perforation, not merely produce a plausible word.

## Competing inherited readings

- Older literature reported the Ennis sequence as `MTBCML`; reversed, this was compared with the Glenfahan magical inscription `LMCBDV` and prompted a proposed *rúnogam* interpretation.
- Macalister (1945) instead read `ATUCMLU`, explicitly admitting that his identification of the two V-shaped anomalous marks as the U-forfid had "little justification".
- Direct OG(H)AM examination at the British Museum in December 2023 gives the tentative reading `?DMVA?VA`, with the clear middle group possibly `DMLO` rather than `DMVA`. The team reports that the stemline appears to split around the perforation and that the initial `<`-like shape is not a known ogham character.

These are not minor spelling variants; they imply different segmentation, direction, character identity, and even inscription topology.
