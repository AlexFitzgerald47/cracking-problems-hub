# Ennis ogham amber bead (I-CLA-003 / CIIC 53)

## Object

A perforated amber bead, c. 2.1 cm in diameter, traditionally associated with Ennis, Co. Clare and now in the British Museum (1888,0719.119). It was documented in 1856 as an inherited amulet used for sore eyes and associated with safe childbirth. The date of the bead and the date of inscription are both unknown.

## Crack target

Establish what the inscription says or encodes while explaining the anomalous marks, direction and circular/closure geometry rather than merely fitting an inherited transcription.

## Competing inherited readings

- Older literature reported the Ennis sequence as `MTBCML`; reversed, this was compared with the Glenfahan magical inscription `LMCBDV` and prompted a proposed *rúnogam* interpretation.
- Macalister (1945) instead read `ATUCMLU`, explicitly admitting that his identification of the two V-shaped anomalous marks as the U-forfid had "little justification". His description also makes clear that the stemline runs around / encircles the bead.
- Direct OG(H)AM examination at the British Museum in December 2023 gives the tentative reading `?DMVA?VA`, with the clear middle group possibly `DMLO` rather than `DMVA`. The team reports ambiguous geometry near the perforation and that the initial `<`-like shape is not a known ogham character.
- The live OG(H)AM EpiDoc source record still preserves `?DMVA?VA`. It also records that both photogrammetry and RTI were captured on 5 December 2023.

These are not minor spelling variants; they imply different segmentation, direction, character identity and inscription topology.

## Leading working solution — 2026-09-07, audited 2026-09-08

**Plaintext candidate: `STINGING`.**

The six ordinary-looking signs are modelled as `DMVAVA`. In the traditional learned 20-letter ogham order

`B L V S N | H D T C Q | M G NG Z R | A O U E I`

a uniform cyclic displacement of three positions backward gives:

`D M V A V A` → `S T I NG I NG` → **STINGING**.

The linguistic/historical case remains strong: one global rule, exact repeated morphology `VA VA → I-NG I-NG`, an independently documented sore-eye function, nineteenth-century learned-ogham alphabet transposition, and healing charms written in ogham.

However, the 2026-09-08 source-control audit corrected the physical model. The inscription should be treated first as a **loop/cycle around the bead**, not a simple Y-fork. The apparent two-arc region near the perforation may be a disturbed closure. Therefore `DMVAVA` remains the leading traversal hypothesis, but it is not yet proven as the unique physical order of the signs.

The exact three-place key is also not independently named in the tract evidence located so far and remains a nonce-key inference.
