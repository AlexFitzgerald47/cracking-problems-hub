# Sources — Ennis ogham amber bead

## Primary / object-level

1. **OG(H)AM, University of Glasgow, Og(h)am of the Month, January 2024 (Nora White).** Direct examination and photographs of British Museum 1888,0719.119 in December 2023. Records the anomalous `<`-shape, possible `DMVA` / `DMLO` core, split stemline, branch geometry and tentative `?DMVA?VA` reading.
   - https://ogham.glasgow.ac.uk/index.php/ogham-of-the-month/

2. **British Museum, 1888,0719.119.** Ennis amber bead / amulet catalogue object.
   - https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1888-0719-119

3. **Proceedings and Papers (1856), Journal of the Kilkenny and South-East of Ireland Archaeological Society, new series 1(1), pp. 147–169, esp. 149–150.** Earliest published description located in this session; gives provenance, amuletic use and woodcut, but explicitly does not attempt an interpretation. Records that it had been in the O'Connor family for "many generations" and was used for sore eyes and safe childbirth.
   - https://www.jstor.org/stable/25502504
   - NLI bibliographic record: https://sources.nli.ie/Record/PS_UR_044045

## Historical readings

4. **R. A. S. Macalister, Corpus Inscriptionum Insularum Celticarum, vol. 1 (1945), no. 53, pp. 57–58.** Gives `ATUCMLU`, while expressly conceding "little justification" for treating the anomalous V-shaped marks as the U-forfid.
   - https://dokumen.pub/corpus-inscriptionum-insularum-celticarum-vol-1-1.html

5. **Helmut Arntz, “Das Ogom.”** Reports Glenfahan `LMCBDV` and Ennis `MTBCML`; notes that reverse Ennis nearly coincides with Glenfahan. Reports Marstrander's *rúnogam* proposal inserting vowels in alphabetic order to yield `LaMoCuBeDVi`, but judges the result interpretable yet unconvincing.
   - https://libarch.nmu.org.ua/bitstream/handle/GenofondUA/15160/8ac43168d66889635ee85be10eff6cfe.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1

6. **Thomas Johnson Westropp, “A Folklore Survey of County Clare” (1911), Folklore 22(1).** The Clare Libraries transcription assigns `L.M.C.B.D.V.` to the Ennis bead and `L.M.C.B.T.M.` to the Fahan comparator. This conflicts with Arntz, Macalister's later corpus, and the modern corpus assignment of Glenfahan=`LMCBDV`; treat the Westropp assignment as a probable object/transcription swap until checked against the printed plate/footnote.
   - https://clarelibraries.ie/localstudies/folklore/a-folklore-survey-of-county-clare-by-thomas-johnson-westropp/charms-amulets-and-magical-sites/

## Corpus controls

7. **OG(H)AM data v1, `og_readings.csv` / `og_words.csv`.** Machine-readable corpus used only as a control for inherited readings and standard ogham lexical/formulaic inventory. The dataset predates the December 2023 Ennis re-examination, so absence of the new reading is expected rather than evidential.
   - https://github.com/ogi-ogham/ogham-datav1

## Learned-ogham cryptography

8. **National Library of Ireland MS G 163, Peadar Ó Longáin, 1831.** Dedicated ogham cryptography manuscript. The catalogue records a "Topsy Turvey Cryptography" described as "only a transposition of the letters", a "secret Military Cryptography commonly called Ogam róinn na bhFíann", discussion of where Irish alphabets begin, and several other cryptographic alphabets. This establishes alphabet transposition/re-indexing as a live learned-ogham operation in nineteenth-century Ireland; it does **not** establish the exact Ennis −3 key.
   - https://www.celt.dias.ie/publications/online/nli/5/NLI163.html

9. **OG(H)AM, Og(h)am of the Month: February 2025, Cambridge University Library MS Additional 4437.** Another nineteenth-century Peadar Ó Longáin manuscript explaining ogham and cryptographic methods, including an alphabet key and an encoded passage. Useful as a direct comparator for learned nineteenth-century ogham practice and scribal imperfections.
   - https://ogham.glasgow.ac.uk/index.php/ogham-of-the-month/

## Healing ogham

10. **OG(H)AM, “Ogam Script in Irish Medical Tradition” (2023).** Documents NLS Advocates' MS 50.3.11, the Minchin Manuscript, a 66-page notebook probably written in Kerry c.1849 containing healing charms and prayers written entirely in ogham.
   - https://ogham.glasgow.ac.uk/index.php/2023/03/24/ogam-script-in-irish-medical-tradition/

11. **Deborah Hayden & David Stifter, “Ogam, cryptography and healing charms in the nineteenth century: observations on ‘The Minchin Manuscript’” (2025).** Peer-reviewed treatment of the manuscript and the continued interaction of ogham, cryptography and healing into the nineteenth century.
   - https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/19488/

## Amber and eye healing

12. **National Museums Scotland, “Amber charms for eye conditions.”** Records traditional use of amber beads/charms rubbed on eyelids to treat failing eyesight, including MacDonald of Glencoe examples. This matters because it shows that the Ennis object's eye-healing function can arise from the material/object tradition independently of the exact age of its inscription.
   - https://www.nms.ac.uk/discover-catalogue/from-amulets-to-elf-bolts-10-scottish-charms

13. **Marion Dowd, “Bewitched by an Elf Dart: Fairy Archaeology, Folk Magic and Traditional Medicine in Ireland,” Cambridge Archaeological Journal.** Discusses the Ennis bead as an unusual reused ancient object in folk medicine and explicitly notes that bead and inscription need not be the same age.
   - https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cambridge-archaeological-journal/article/bewitched-by-an-elf-dart-fairy-archaeology-folk-magic-and-traditional-medicine-in-ireland/7EF2D9BD63A34CAA405A42E120C4D421

14. **Timothy F. Allen, The Encyclopedia of Pure Materia Medica (1874–79).** Period English medical vocabulary includes descriptions such as frequent "stinging" in both eyes and "stinging in the eyes". Used only to establish that `STINGING` is historically natural eye-symptom language, not a modern semantic gloss.
   - Bibliographic record: https://wellcomecollection.org/works/bcw4ts9b
   - Searchable transcription example: https://homeoint.org/allen/s/sars.htm

## Source cautions

- Do not treat Westropp's `LMCBDV` assignment to Ennis as an independent witness: the cross-source object labels conflict.
- Do not treat a modern serialized string such as `?DMVA?VA` as proof that the physical inscription is linear; the source itself reports a fork in the stemline.
- The object's chronology is not fixed by the age of amber or by the 1856 folklore of its use.
- NLI G 163 establishes the **class** of alphabet-transposition cryptography, not the exact three-place Ennis key.
- The `STINGING` solution specifically belongs to the later learned-ogham value system because it uses nGétal as `NG`.
