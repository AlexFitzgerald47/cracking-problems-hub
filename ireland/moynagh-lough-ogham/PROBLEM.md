# Moynagh Lough ogham antler tine — I-MEA-003

## Named unknown

What do the two ogham inscriptions on the worked red-deer-antler tine from Moynagh Lough, Co. Meath (NMI RIA 1889:13; OG(H)AM I-MEA-003) actually say or represent?

The current specialist reading is approximately:

- side 1: `COLORRS` (less-favoured reverse: `CRRODOS`)
- side 2: `PIBANSNAVQE`, with live alternatives for the initial forfid/marker and damaged final letter

The meaning remains unresolved.

## Why this is crackable

The object is physically secure enough to be meaningful, its archaeological horizon is constrained to roughly AD 625–810, modern RTI/photogrammetry-derived imagery exists, and the inscription contains multiple independently testable constraints: direction, sign identity, word division, chronology, Old Irish orthography, portable-object formulae, and the newly identified function of the tine as a leather-working edge beveller/burnisher.

## Crack standard

A successful result must do more than make a plausible word. It should:

1. freeze the visual/sign uncertainties rather than silently resolve them;
2. explain both the orthography and the object/date context;
3. beat serious competitor readings and a short-string null;
4. distinguish whether the two sides are one text, two texts, or separate carving episodes;
5. produce falsifiable predictions for higher-resolution RTI/3D or physical inspection.
