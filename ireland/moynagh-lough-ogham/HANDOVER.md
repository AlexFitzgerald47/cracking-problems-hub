## Strongest result A — `PIBAN` can be a personal name

Fáilbe, eighth abbot of Iona (d. 679), is independently recorded as **son of Pípán** / *mac Pipan*. This is virtually contemporary with Moynagh’s AD 625–810 horizon.

This creates a serious alternative to Stifter’s common-noun *pípán* “small tube”. On a portable craft object, an owner/maker/name reading is structurally plausible.

### Do not overstate

- `SNAVQE` is still unread.
- Initial side-2 sign might be a start marker or another forfid rather than P.
- `PIBAN` is therefore a **component identification**, not a solved sentence.

### Next experiment

Search a pinned early-Irish onomastic corpus for:

- Pípán/Pipan/Píobán and variants;
- names/bynames beginning `SNAV-`, `SNAM-`, `SNAB-`, `SNAQ-`, `SNAC-`;
- genealogical constructions in which a personal name is followed by an unmarked second name or qualifier on portable inscriptions.

Do not fuzzy-match the entire medieval lexicon. Score only physically surviving branches and compare against a matched name-corpus null.

## Strongest result B — test `COLOR | RS` as two carving phases

Stifter reports that the final two signs of side 1 appear cut with a finer blade, perhaps the same tool used on side 2. This physical observation independently places a possible phase boundary after sign 5:

`COLOR | RS`

The first phase is exactly Latin *color*.

### Why this matters

Unlike arbitrary word segmentation, the endpoint is proposed from groove morphology. If the cut-phase difference is real, the lexical coincidence deserves much more weight.

### Exact physical test

Obtain the highest-resolution RTI/photogrammetry or arrange microscope inspection. For every sign measure:

- groove width at comparable depth;
- maximum depth;
- V/U cross-section profile;
- cutting direction / burr or shoulder asymmetry if preserved;
- start/stop morphology;
- overlap/intersection sequencing;
- surface/patina contrast;
- spacing and pressure variation.

Cluster **side1 signs 1–5**, **side1 signs 6–7**, and **all side2 signs** blindly by groove/tool features.

Predictions:

- If signs 6–7 cluster with side2 and away from signs 1–5, `COLOR | RS` gains strong independent support.
- If all side1 signs form one homogeneous cutting episode, `COLOR | RS` should be heavily downgraded.
- If side2 is mechanically distinct from all side1 signs, treat the two sides as independent texts by default.
