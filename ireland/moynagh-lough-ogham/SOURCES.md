# Evidence register

## Direct specialist/object evidence

### OG(H)AM — April 2025
https://ogham.glasgow.ac.uk/index.php/ogham-of-the-month/

**Seen in full HTML.** Records the object as worked red-deer antler, c. 63.5 × 19 mm, now NMI RIA 1889:13. The OG(H)AM team examined/recorded it in November 2023 and publishes RTI/3D-derived views. Identifies the object as a leather-working beveller/burnisher after recognition in 2025. Gives Moynagh crannog horizon c. 625–800 and notes extensive high-status craft activity.

Important inscription facts reported there:
- two sides, one using a natural ridge, one a drawn stem-line;
- cuts differ in quality and may be different tools/people/times;
- order/continuity of the two sides is unknown;
- reformed-ogham-like long vowel strokes;
- side 2 initial sign may be X-forfid, iphín (`P/PH`), or a start marker;
- final side-2 sign is damaged and may be `I`;
- readings `COLORRS` and `XIBANSNAVQẸ` / `PIBANSNAVQẸ`.

### David Stifter — 2026 preprint
https://www.academia.edu/171047177/The_ogam_inscription_on_an_antler_tine_from_Moynagh_Lough_I_MEA_003_pre_print_

**Seen in full HTML transcription.** Adds detailed sign/spacing discussion and argues for left-to-right direction on each side because spacing narrows toward the right. Gives reverse side-1 `CRRODOS`; reverse side-2 `ENTAQCQAHIP`. Notes side-1 fifth `R` is poorly executed and sixth `R` may be an improvement/correction. Tentatively segments side 2 `PIBAN SNAVQE` and proposes `pípán` 'small pipe/tube' + hypothetical genitive `*snamchae` from a bark/cork word, hence 'a tube of bark', while explicitly stating that this does not explain the solid-antler object.

Stifter dates the object no earlier than c. 625 and unlikely after c. 810 and explicitly allows mixed classical/reformed orthography.

## Comparative data

### Ogham Data v1 readings
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ogi-ogham/ogham-datav1/main/csv/ogham/og_readings.csv

**Seen as 1,017-line public CSV.** Used as a control corpus. `COLORRS`, `PIBAN`, and `SNAV` do not occur as exact substrings in this older corpus. It does contain `DUGENNGG[I] MAQI RODDOS`, relevant to the reverse side-1 family.

### `RODDOS` discussion
David Stifter, 1998, linguistic discussion surfaced via Maynooth repository search: `RODDOS` is an attested ogham name/form and has been analysed in connection with the red-colour word family. McManus, *A Guide to Ogam*, also lists `?RODDOS` as a personal-name form.

### Period-correct personal name Pípán
Dictionary of Irish Biography, `Fáilbe`:
https://www.dib.ie/biography/failbe-a2991

**Seen.** Fáilbe (d. 679), eighth abbot of Iona, is explicitly described as son of **Pípán**. This demonstrates that Pípán is not merely a hypothetical lexical match: it was a real Irish personal name in precisely the seventh-century horizon relevant to Moynagh.

McAlary 2022, *Early mechanisms of abbatial succession: the case of Iona (563–704)*, independently discusses Fáilbe and the genealogical material around Pípán.

### Reformed-ogham chronology
OG(H)AM, *The Periods of Ogam Usage*:
https://ogham.glasgow.ac.uk/index.php/2023/12/20/the-periods-of-ogam-usage/

**Seen.** Defines the eighth century onward as the reformed-ogham phase, with manuscript spelling influence, word spacing and discourse/feather marks becoming characteristic.

## Internet hypothesis checked and rejected as evidence

A July 2026 r/HistoricalLinguistics post proposed connecting Old Irish `snob` 'bark' with English/Scots `snob/snab` 'cobbler', yielding a leatherworker interpretation.

This bridge is **not established**. Oxford's current word-origin note dates English `snob` in the cobbler sense to the late eighteenth century and calls its origin unknown; Scots material is similarly late. It cannot presently serve as medieval evidence.
