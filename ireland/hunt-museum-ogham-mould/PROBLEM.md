# Hunt Museum soapstone mould — HCA 686 / I-XXX-001

## Named unknown

What do the five incised signs on the edge of Hunt Museum soapstone mould HCA 686 actually read, encode, or represent?

## Object

- Hunt Museum accession: HCA 686
- OG(H)AM corpus identifier: I-XXX-001 (findspot unknown)
- Material: soapstone
- Dimensions: approximately 6.1 × 5.6–5.9 × 1.8 cm
- Function: two-sided mould/valve for penannular ring-brooches; specialist discussion argues the artefact is probably much later than the museum's old 5th-century label and likely belongs in a Viking/Norse-connected context.
- Provenance: unrecorded before the Hunt collection; probably art-market acquisition.

## Why this is crackable

The inscription is only five marks long but the raw object is digitally accessible. The Hunt Museum publishes a CC0 Sketchfab model, and the OG(H)AM project has published object photographs and an expert sign audit. The inscription therefore permits direct sign/orientation reconstruction rather than reliance on an old drawing alone.

## Current state of the question

Published specialist analysis (David Stifter, OG(H)AM, June 2025) treats the first three signs as standard ogham, the fourth as possibly a Younger Futhark rune, and the fifth as anomalous. Suggested branches include `ALU`, `MLGʷ/MLNG`, `ALGʷ/ALNG`, and reversed/upended equivalents such as `UDA`; no accepted meaningful Irish reading results. The fourth sign has been compared with the inverted Younger Futhark algiz/ýr rune; the fifth with a manuscript-ogham `P` form or a possible feather-mark. These are hypotheses to be audited, not starting truths.

## Crack criteria

A substantive advance is any reproducible result that materially narrows the inscription, for example:

- a defensible physical orientation or reading direction;
- a sign inventory that eliminates serious published alternatives;
- identification of one sign as securely ogham, runic, composite, decorative, or non-linguistic;
- a candidate personal name, maker/owner mark, label, formula, or notation that beats matched alternatives;
- evidence that mixed-script interpretation is materially stronger than pure ogham/runic/non-linguistic models;
- a quantified demonstration that the surviving length has too little discriminating power for lexical fitting, together with the exact additional image/comparative evidence needed to break the tie.

3. Hunt Museum online catalogue object page for HCA 686 (linked by OG(H)AM; catalogue UUID `7d99dca4-fd93-39f1-b7de-aa0c18a1778d`).

## Required method

Freeze the physical sign geometry first. Branch uncertainty explicitly. Test ordinary/reformed/scholastic ogham, Irish names/formulae, pure runic, mixed ogham-rune, Norse/Norse-Gaelic readings, maker/owner marks, amuletic formulae, technical notation, pseudo-writing/practice, later addition and non-linguistic explanations. Use matched nulls because a five-sign sequence is extremely vulnerable to overfitting.
