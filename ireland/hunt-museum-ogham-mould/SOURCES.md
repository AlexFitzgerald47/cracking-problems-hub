## C. Ogham chronology / structure

### Unicode Standard 17.0 — Ogham

- **Unicode 17.0 Core Specification, Chapter 8.15 Ogham**
- URL: https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-8/
- Used for: distinction between classical edge ogham and later/post-seventh-century scholastic inscriptions on artificial stem-lines; normal reading direction; feather/reversed-feather role.

### P-form discussion

- **The Chapel and Burial Ground**, section discussing ogham-inscribed slab and supplementary P forms.
- Indexed PDF used in this session: https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781351192224_A31870519/preview-9781351192224_A31870519.pdf
- Key point used: ninth-century Bern Burgerbibliothek MS 207 renders Latin P twice with a y-like ogham character reported as *not attested elsewhere* (citing Sims-Williams 1992); a different supplementary P occurs in later learned tradition.

## F. Lexical / chronological checks

### Old Norse *alr* / Icelandic *alur*

- Wiktionary Old Norse entry `alr`: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/alr
- Wiktionary Icelandic entry `alur`: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/alur
- Used only as lexical pointers: Old Norse `alr` 'awl'; modern Icelandic `alur` descends from it.

### Epenthetic/svarabhakti vowel chronology

- Search-accessible academic text **The unreadable poem of ...**, section 2.1: states a svarabhakti vowel appeared in Old Norse -r endings at the end of the thirteenth century and is documented in Norway before 1300.
- Search-accessible study **The Scribes of Flateyjarbók**, section 3.4: u-epenthesis begins toward end of 13th / beginning 14th century (`armr > armur`, `akr > akur`).
- Use: rejection of `ALUʀ = alur 'awl'` as an ordinary Viking-age spelling.
