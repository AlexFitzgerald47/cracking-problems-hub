# Failed readings and dead ends

## `ALU` = Old Germanic magical formula

**Why tempting:** signs 1–3 have a minimum-assumption ogham reading `ALU`, identical in Latin transliteration to the famous early Germanic runic formula `alu`.

**Why rejected:** known `alu` evidence belongs to the early/Older-Futhark horizon (e.g. Migration-period bracteates and early runic monuments). HCA 686 is materially/Norse-contextually much later. The hypothesis additionally requires a Germanic runic formula to be transliterated into ogham and then followed by a Younger-Futhark-like rune plus a fifth unexplained mark. Too many independent exceptions.

**What could revive it:** an early-medieval Irish/Irish-Sea parallel where `alu` is deliberately written in ogham in an amuletic context, or physical dating showing the inscription is dramatically earlier than the current archaeological expectation.

---

## `ALUʀ` = Old Norse/Icelandic *alur* ‘awl’

**Why tempting:** modern Icelandic `alur` means ‘awl’; a craft word on a craft object would be unusually satisfying.

**Why rejected:** Old Norse has `alr`, not the later `alur`. The epenthetic/svarabhakti vowel producing forms like `alur` develops near the end of the thirteenth century / around 1300, while the Younger-Futhark ʀ sign used in the proposed reading belongs to an earlier value history and was re-used for `y` after about 1050. The orthographic/phonological features do not share a credible normal window. The object is also a mould, not an awl.

**Lesson:** exact-looking four-sign matches must be checked against historical morphology, not modern descendant spellings.

---

## Fifth sign = ordinary phonetic `P`

**Why tempting:** Stifter noted resemblance to the y-like `P` in Bern Burgerbibliothek MS 207.

**Why downgraded/rejected as default:** published specialist discussion of ogham `P` says the Bern y-like form is not attested elsewhere; later learned `P` traditions use different supplementary shapes. On HCA 686 the sign is also smaller, more widely separated, and crosses the stem-line. The evidence is better explained by a secondary/terminal mark unless direct imaging shows otherwise.

**What could revive it:** a second practical inscription using the same y-like `P`, or clear stroke-order/context evidence on the mould tying sign 5 into the same phonetic sequence.

---

## Ordinary Primitive-Irish / classical ogham word or personal name

**Why tempting:** first three signs are recognizably ogham.

**Why downgraded:** the artificial full stem-line is characteristic of later/scholastic ogham; sign 4 is not ordinary ogham; core searches in the public Ogham-in-3D/CISP-derived readings dataset do not produce `ALU`, `ALNG`, `MLNG`, `UDA`, `NGDA`, `NGDM`, `UDM`, or `ALUR`. `MLU` appears only as a substring of Macalister's disputed `ATUCMLU` reading of the ambiguous Ennis bead, which is not independent lexical support.

---

## Pure runic reading

**Rejected:** signs 1–3 are structurally stem-line ogham and are independently classed as standard ogham signs. Treating all of them as runes creates more shape exceptions than the mixed-script explanation.

---

## Pure ordinary-ogham reading

**Rejected:** sign 4 is not a normal ogham fid. A scholastic/cryptic reinterpretation is possible in principle, but no specific ogham value has a better sign-form match than the Younger-Futhark hypothesis.

---

## Mould-use technical/numerical code as the entire sequence

**Why considered:** the inscription is on a casting tool, so workshop notation is a real prior.

**Why currently weak:** the first three signs organize cleanly as ogham and sign 4 has a strong rune-shaped parallel. No known metrological, batch, cavity, alloy or sizing notation has been found that maps naturally to the sequence. The fifth sign alone could still be technical.

---

## Alphabet fragment / literal teaching sequence

**Why considered:** Maughold deliberately juxtaposes a Younger Futhark and an ogham alphabet, proving learned rune-ogham comparison in the Irish Sea world.

**Why not accepted:** `ALU` (or its consonantal variants) is not an alphabetic run in standard ogham. The broader category of learned script practice/display remains viable, but a literal alphabet exercise is not supported.
