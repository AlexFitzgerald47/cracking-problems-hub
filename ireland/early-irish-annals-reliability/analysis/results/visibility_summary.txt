== discriminating power: Ireland against the Mediterranean ==
  deep (>=0.80) over Ireland:              70
  deep (>=0.80) over the Mediterranean:    90
  deep in both (the test is blind here):   28  = 40% of the Irish set
  deep in Ireland, <0.40 in the Med:       11  <- diagnostic of observation
  deep in the Med, <0.20 in Ireland:       17  <- diagnostic of borrowing

  Of the 132 eclipses that were deep enough somewhere for a chronicler
  on either side to notice, the test can decide 28 (11 + 17) and is blind
  on the 28 that were large over Ireland *and* over the Mediterranean.
  A notice of one of those decides nothing, whichever way it reads.

== can the annals locate their own observatory? ==
  The Chronicle of Ireland hypothesis has the common source kept at
  Iona until the middle of the eighth century and in Ireland after it.
  Iona and Clonmacnoise are 3 degrees of latitude apart, which is enough
  to put one of them inside a penumbra and the other outside it. Where
  that happens, a notice that reports depth *is* evidence of where it
  was written.
  |mag(Iona) - mag(Clonmacnoise)| >= 0.10, deeper site >= 0.50:   13 (10 favour Iona)
  |mag(Iona) - mag(Clonmacnoise)| >= 0.15, deeper site >= 0.50:    9 (7 favour Iona)
  |mag(Iona) - mag(Clonmacnoise)| >= 0.25, deeper site >= 0.50:    3 (2 favour Iona)
  |mag(Iona) - mag(Clonmacnoise)| >= 0.40, deeper site >= 0.50:    1 (1 favour Iona)
  central at Iona but NOT at Clonmacnoise:        2
  central at Clonmacnoise but NOT at Iona:        1
  (AU 885, 16 June 885, is one of the first group: 1.077 at Iona,
   0.960 at Clonmacnoise, 0.972 at Armagh.)

  VERDICT ON THIS TEST: weak. Over 810 years only 12 eclipses split the
  two sites usefully, and the annals record a small fraction of eclipses
  at all. Expect a handful of usable cases, not a distribution. Treat it
  as corroboration for a hypothesis argued on other grounds, never as the
  primary evidence.
