# Early Irish Annals – Reliability & Chronology

## Statement
Critically evaluate the reliability of the early entries in the major Irish annals (particularly the Annals of Ulster, Annals of Tigernach, Chronicon Scotorum, and related texts) for the period before c. 700 CE. Clarify the chronological framework, identify layers of later redaction, and assess what can be securely known about early medieval Irish history from these sources.

## Why it belongs on the board
The Irish annals are among the most important primary sources for early medieval Ireland and, to some extent, Britain. Their early sections contain a mixture of contemporary recording, retrospective construction, synchronisms, and later interpolation. Establishing what is historically reliable remains a central and contested task in early Irish studies.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- Major modern editions and translations exist (Mac Airt & Mac Niocaill, Stokes, etc.).
- Scholars such as Kathleen Hughes, Gearóid Mac Niocaill, Daniel McCarthy, and others have worked extensively on chronology and textual history.
- The relationship between the “Chronicle of Ireland” and the surviving annals is still debated.
- Synchronisms with external events (eclipses, etc.) provide important anchors but also points of contention.

## Success criteria
Clearer separation of contemporary, near-contemporary, and retrospective material; improved understanding of the chronological scheme(s) in use; and a more precise statement of what can (and cannot) be securely claimed for the 5th–7th centuries on the basis of the annals.

## Key sources & starting points
- Critical editions of the major annals
- McCarthy’s chronological work
- Studies on the “Chronicle of Ireland”
- Contemporary external sources (Bede, continental chronicles, etc.) for cross-checking
