# The provenance-knowledge constraint — a candidate frame, not a candidate list

*Opened 2026-09-07. Conditional on H1/H-COLLISION surviving the tests in
`churchill-date-collision.md`. **Do not use this file before those tests are run.** Every
row is `[INFERRED]` from `[SEARCH]`-grade context; nobody named here is being accused of
anything, and several are named only to be excluded.*

## The argument

Most espionage identity problems are constrained by *access to information*. This one, if
H1 holds, is constrained by something much rarer: **access to the knowledge that a
particular stream of intelligence was produced by breaking a cipher machine.**

In April 1941 those two sets were deliberately kept apart by design. Ultra-derived
intelligence was distributed disguised as agent reporting — Churchill's own 3 April message
to Stalin invented "a trusted agent" for precisely this purpose. The number of people in
London who both (a) saw the product and (b) knew where it came from was a small fraction of
the number who saw the product.

**That asymmetry is the crack.** A candidate does not need to have been at Bletchley. A
candidate needs to have been on the short list of people who knew the disguise was a
disguise.

## The four access routes to provenance knowledge, spring 1941

| Route | Who is in it | Fit to B5 (GRU, not NKVD) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R1 — SIGINT production** | GC&CS/Bletchley staff; Hut 3 | Weak by default | The "traitor inside wartime GCHQ" framing that circulates around BARON. Note the framing is *asserted* in the secondary literature, not established |
| **R2 — SIS distribution** | "C" (Menzies) and the SIS handling chain that dressed Ultra as agent product | Weak | The people who *built* the disguise necessarily knew it was one |
| **R3 — Political/staff circle** | Churchill's private office and staff; Eden and the FO side; the JIC; Desmond Morton | Unknown | The 3 April decision was taken here. This is the circle H-COLLISION points at |
| **R4 — Allied liaison** | Czechoslovak, Polish, Free French military-intelligence staffs in London receiving British-sourced material | Unknown | The annotator's "Military Intelligence" note fits an allied MI staff as readily as a British one. This is where West's Sedláček identification lives |

**R1 and R2 are the historically explosive readings. R3 and R4 are the more probable ones,
and R4 is the one with a standing published candidate.** A session that goes hunting in R1
because it is the exciting answer is doing the thing this board exists to avoid.

Those three exclusions matter because they are the names a non-specialist reaches for first,
and all three fail on the GRU/NKVD distinction — which is the most under-used discriminator
in this whole problem.

## What would make this frame operational

1. **Settle B7** (does BARON supply the provenance, or does someone else?). Until then this
   frame may be answering a question the documents do not ask.
2. **Enumerate R3 and R4 by name from Hinsley vol. 1 and the Czech/Polish liaison
   literature**, with dates of access — not from memory, and not from a search summary.
3. **Only then** score candidates, and score every one of them against the same constraint
   set, recording how many survive. `PRACTICES.md`: *count the competitors, do not score
   one.* A frame this narrow will produce a small survivor set, and the size of that set is
   the result — not the top name in it.

## The result this frame is aiming at

Not "BARON was X". Realistically: **"the BARON documents require provenance knowledge, only
these N people in London had it in April 1941, and the standing Sedláček identification is
or is not among them."** That is a defensible, falsifiable, publishable-shaped result, and
it does not require naming a traitor to be worth having.
