# Mesha Stele line 31: what do the damaged traces actually read?

## Statement
The end of line 31 of the ninth-century BCE Mesha Stele is damaged. Competing readings include `BT[D]WD` / `BTDWD` (commonly interpreted as “House of David”), a three-consonant royal name beginning with `B` (Finkelstein, Na’aman & Römer cautiously proposed Balak), and more agnostic reconstructions. The named unknown is the most defensible sequence of signs between the clear context `... WHWRNN YŠB BH ...` and the surviving following text.

## Why it belongs on the board
This is a bounded, image-grounded epigraphic crack with unusually high historical consequence. The stone itself, the pre-break squeeze, RTI photographs, backlit squeeze photographs, and multiple explicit competing reconstructions are available remotely. A useful result can be falsified mark by mark.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- André Lemaire proposed `BT[D]WD` in 1994 after the Tel Dan `BYTDWD` discovery.
- Michael Langlois (Semitica 61, 2019) combined RTI of the stone and squeezes and argued the best reading is `BTDWD`; he considered B, W, following D and terminal divider strong, the central T much less secure.
- Israel Finkelstein, Nadav Na’aman & Thomas Römer (Tel Aviv 46, 2019) argued the T does not exist and that a vertical sentence divider occurs before W; they inferred a three-consonant personal name `B??` and cautiously proposed Balak.
- Matthieu Richelle & Andrew Burlingame (2021/2023) argue that `BTDWD` remains possible but unconfirmed: the alleged T and first D are not securely traceable on the available squeeze/RTI evidence, and the terminal divider is disputed.
- The lower stone was broken after the 1869 squeeze, so modern plaster restoration must never be treated as original incision evidence.

## Success criteria
A full crack would freeze each surviving stroke independently of lexical expectation and show that one reading explains the stone + independent squeeze evidence substantially better than its competitors. A strong partial result would kill one major branch (e.g. the Balak/sentence-divider model or the claimed-confirmed `BTDWD` model) by an image-independent physical or syntactic contradiction.

## Notes
Treat this as five separate questions: which marks are original; which belong to a letter; which letter; where word/sentence boundaries are; only then what the sequence means. Do not let `DWD`, Balak, or biblical history choose the strokes.
