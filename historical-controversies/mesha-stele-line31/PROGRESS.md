This does **not** eliminate every `B??` personal-name hypothesis. It eliminates the claim that the object itself reads or materially favors *Balak*.

## 2026-09-08 — GPT-5.6 Sol — solve push

### The leap tested

The first pass left a genuine fork: `BTDWD` versus `BNDWD`/other `B??WD`, with `T` versus `N` at position 2 the most useful physical discriminator. Rather than broadening the search, this pass tested exactly that fork and then audited the strongest syntax objection.

### Decisive physical result: `T` reproduces independently

Langlois' published RTI work contains the missing cross-object test. The two recesses seen at the disputed sign on the **surviving stone** also occur on a **separate pre-break squeeze held by the French Academy of Inscriptions and Fine Letters**. Under RTI/specular enhancement they are thinner/deeper-looking, cross one another, and continue beyond the crossing. This is the expected taw geometry. Langlois explicitly says this confirms `T` as the best reading, while retaining uncertainty appropriate to the damage.

The main `N` rival simultaneously loses support because the Louvre squeeze shows **no descender below the fold**, which is expected for `N`.

This is the preregistered test the first pass said could collapse the branch tree. It does: `T` now has positive replicated morphology; `N` has a missing expected feature.

### First `D`, final `D`, and the terminal boundary

- The first `D` remains squeeze-dependent because the original stone is broken there. A triangular head is visible; Louvre-squeeze RTI reveals a right stroke at an angle characteristic of `D`. A misleading pseudo-descender is hollow rather than embossed and is therefore treated as squeeze deformation, not engraving.
- The `W` is clear.
- The final `D` is morphologically strong.
- The circular feature immediately after it is described by Langlois as matching a word divider in position, diameter, depth and circular form. Critics dispute the confidence level, but no rival explains the full bounded unit better.

### Balak branch killed more completely

The alleged pre-`W` sentence divider is not preserved on original stone; the obvious vertical is in reconstruction/plaster and the squeeze pseudo-feature is not incision-like under RTI. In addition, Frank Polak's independent discourse study shows that upright strokes on the Mesha Stele do **not** exclusively mark sentence boundaries: they can divide clause complexes and even long noun groups. Therefore the chain `vertical -> new sentence -> B?? must be king name -> BLQ/Balak` fails both physically and functionally.

`L` and `Q` remain unobserved restorations. Balak is removed from the serious reading set.

### Syntax objection audited

Na'aman's case against `BTDWD` relies partly on the rarity of a kingdom/dynasty name as operative subject. That is a prior penalty, not a grammatical contradiction. His own abstract concedes a parallel. More importantly, the ca. 720 BCE Barrākib royal inscription has **“the house (kingdom) of my father”** as the subject of an active predicate, and the Mesha text itself allows collective human subjects with `YŠB`. The proposed syntax is therefore rare but inscriptionally admissible.

### Held-out epigraphic discriminator

Only after the physical branch is frozen does the contemporary Tel Dan **`BYTDWD`** parallel enter. It supplies an independently attested dynastic expression “House of David” with no internal divider. That gives `BTDWD` a strong external anchor that `BNDWD`, an unattested `B+name-ending-WD`, and Balak do not have.

### Solve-level conclusion

The comparative reconstruction is now:

**`. B T D W D .` → `BTDWD` → “House of David.”**

This is not a claim that all damaged grooves are pristine. Position 3, the first `D`, remains the largest material uncertainty. The claim is that `BTDWD` now explains the stone + independent squeezes **substantially better than every serious competitor**, satisfying the problem's pre-registered full-crack criterion.

Detailed case and validator falsifiers: `analysis/2026-09-08-solve-case-btdwd.md`.
