# Primary texts — verbatim, with sources

Every text below was retrieved this session and confirmed by at least two routes where a
claim depends on it. The corpora in `data/CORPUS.md` are not redistributed; these short
passages are the load-bearing ones and are quoted here so the analysis is self-contained.

## Suetonius, *Divus Gaius* (Caligula) 44–47

Source: Latin Library `suetonius/suet.cal.txt`, cross-checked verbatim against LacusCurtius
(`penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/L/Roman/Texts/Suetonius/12Caesars/Caligula*.html`). The two
agree character-for-character in ch. 46.

**44** (context — the campaign that frames the episode): "...Nihil autem amplius quam
Adminio Cynobellini Britannorum regis filio, qui pulsus a patre cum exigua manu
transfugerat, in deditionem recepto, quasi universa tradita insula, magnificas Romam
litteras misit..."

**45** (context — the staged German "ambush" and the astral mock-triumph): "...comites autem
et participes victoriae novo genere ac nomine coronarum donavit, quas distinctas solis ac
lunae siderumque specie exploratorias appellavit..."

**46** (the episode itself):
> Postremo quasi perpetraturus bellum, derecta acie in litore Oceani ac ballistis
> machinisque dispositis, nemine gnaro aut opinante quidnam coepturus esset, **repente ut
> conchas legerent galeasque et sinus replerent imperavit**, "spolia Oceani" vocans
> "Capitolio Palatioque debita," et in indicium victoriae altissimam turrem excitavit...

Rolfe (Loeb): "...he suddenly bade them **gather shells and fill their helmets and the folds
of their gowns**, calling them 'spoils from the Ocean, due to the Capitol and Palatine.'"

## Cassius Dio 59.25.1–3

English: Cary (Loeb), LacusCurtius `.../Cassius_Dio/59*.html` (VERIFIED).
Greek: two independent routes agree on the operative word —
(a) remacle.org `historiens/Dion/livre59.htm` (running Greek text);
(b) OCR of the Cary Loeb bilingual, archive.org `diosromanhistory0007earn` vol. 7 (1924).

> ...εἶτ᾽ ἐξαίφνης **ἐκέλευσέ σφισι τὰ κογχύλια συλλέξασθαι**. λαβών τε τὰ σκῦλα ταῦτα (καὶ γὰρ
> λαφύρων δῆλον ὅτι πρὸς τὴν τῶν ἐπινικίων πομπὴν ἐδεῖτο) μέγα τε ἐφρόνησεν... καὶ ὁ μὲν ἐς τὴν
> Ῥώμην **τὰ κογχύλια** ἀνεκόμισεν, ἵνα καὶ ἐκείνοις τὰ λάφυρα δείξῃ.

**The word is τὰ κογχύλια** (*konchylia*, the direct cognate of Latin *conchylium*), used
twice, with the gathering verb συλλέγω. **Dio does not use μῦς** — the Greek word that, like
*musculus*, means both "mouse" and "mussel." (remacle.org has συλλέξασθαι; the Loeb OCR reads
συλλέξαι — a middle/active variant of the same verb, immaterial to the point.)

## Caesar, *Bellum Civile* 2.10 — the *musculus* at Massilia

Latin Library `caesar/bc2.txt` (VERIFIED). Opening: "...**musculum pedes LX longum ex
materia bipedali**, quem a turri latericia ad hostium turrim murumque perducerent, facere
instituerunt; cuius musculi haec erat forma..." A roofed gallery **sixty Roman feet long**,
built, moved on rollers ("machinatione navali"), and defended — men work *sub musculo*.

## Vegetius, *Epitoma rei militaris* 4.16 — definition

Latin Library `vegetius4.txt` (VERIFIED):
> **Musculos dicunt minores machinas**, quibus protecti bellatores sudatum auferunt
> ciuitatis; fossatum etiam ... conplent ... ut turres ambulatoriae sine inpedimento
> iungantur ad murum. **Vocantur autem a marinis beluis musculi**; nam quemadmodum illi, cum
> minores sint, tamen ballenis auxilium adminiculumque iugiter exhibent...

## Isidore, *Etymologiae* — two military/technical senses, neither shellfish

18.11 (siege engine): "**Musculus cuniculo similis fit, quo murus perfoditur; ex quo et
appellatus, quasi murusculus.**" — a mining gallery, "little wall".
19.1.14 (ship): "...cuius contrarius **musculus, curtum navigium**." — a short boat.

## Vitruvius — negative result

*Musculus* occurs **nowhere** in *De architectura*. Confirmed independently: (a) Latin
Library `vitruvius1.txt`–`vitruvius10.txt`, zero; (b) Perseus
`phi1056.phi001.perseus-lat2.xml`, zero; (c) LacusCurtius Book 10, grepped chapter by
chapter, zero. Vitruvius's siege shed is the *testudo* (*testudo arietaria*, book 10).

## The idiom, a century before Caligula

Cicero, *De Oratore* 2.22 (`cicero/oratore2.txt`, VERIFIED): Scaevola's anecdote of Scipio
and Laelius — "**conchas eos et umbilicos ad Caietam et ad Laurentum legere consuesse**."

Valerius Maximus 8.8.1 (`valmax8.txt`, VERIFIED), same anecdote, same transmission: "eos
Caietae et Laurenti uagos litoribus **conchulas et umbilicos lectitasse**."

Aurelius Victor, *De Caesaribus* 3.11 (`victor.caes.txt`, VERIFIED) — Caligula: "**conchas
umbilicosque in ora maris Oceani legi iussit**" — reproduces the Ciceronian collocation, and
is therefore assimilation to the topos, not independent testimony.
