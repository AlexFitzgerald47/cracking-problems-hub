## P1 — Dio's Greek word is a κόγχ- word, not μῦς. (FULLY PROSPECTIVE)

**Status at freeze:** I have read Cary's English ("ordered them to gather up the shells")
from LacusCurtius. I have **not** seen Dio's Greek and both my attempts to fetch it failed.

**The test.** Greek μῦς, like Latin *musculus*, means both "mouse" and "mussel" — the same
double sense Woods's argument turns on. So Dio's choice of word is informative:

- If Dio wrote **κόγχας / κόγχους** (or another shell word: ὄστρεα, κοχλίας): he is
  reporting shells, and his vocabulary gives no foothold for a *musculus* original.
- If Dio wrote **μῦς**: that is a serious point *for* Woods. It would show the tradition's
  underlying word was the mouse/mussel word — exactly what his reconstruction needs — and
  would make the Latin *conchae* look like one translator's disambiguation of it.

**Prediction: κόγχ-.** **Fails if** Dio's word is μῦς or a cognate of it.

## P2 — Aurelius Victor's *umbilici* is a real shellfish word with independent attestation. (FULLY PROSPECTIVE)

I have found Victor's "conchas umbilicosque ... legi iussit" in the corpus but have not
checked whether *umbilicus* is independently attested as a marine creature.

**Prediction:** *umbilicus* is attested elsewhere in Latin as a sea creature (I expect in
Pliny), i.e. Victor is using a genuine second shellfish term rather than coining one.
**Fails if** no non-Victor attestation of a marine *umbilicus* exists in either corpus.

## P3 — Military *musculus* does not occur in narrative-historical Latin. (PARTLY EXPLORATORY)

**Honest labelling.** My concordance has already run over the whole corpus, so I *already
know* that Ammianus Marcellinus and Livy contribute zero `musculus` attestations. That half
is not a prediction. What is genuinely unmeasured, and what I am freezing, is the
**comparator**: how often those same narrative authors use the other siege terms.

**Prediction:** in Ammianus — the most technically detailed siege narrator in Latin —
*testudo*, *aries* and *vinea* each occur at least five times, against *musculus*'s zero.
That contrast is what makes the zero mean "this word belongs to technical manuals" rather
than "these authors never describe sieges."
**Fails if** the comparators are also near zero (in which case the zero is uninformative).

## P4 — *concha* and *musculus* are never interchangeable for one referent. (PROSPECTIVE)

I have seen Celsus group *musculi* under *conchulae*, and Plautus list *conchas* and
*musculos* as separate items. I have not searched for a case where the *same* creature is
called *concha* by one author and *musculus* by another.

**Prediction:** no such equation exists; the ancient lexicographers who define *musculus*
(Isidore) relate it to *cochleae*, *ostreae* and *masculus*, never to *concha*.
**Fails if** a Latin text glosses *musculus* by *concha* or vice versa.
