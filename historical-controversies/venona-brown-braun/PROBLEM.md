# VENONA BROWN/BRAUN — identify the London GRU intermediary/source

## Statement

Identify the real person behind the Soviet military-intelligence covername **BROWN / BRAUN** in the 1940 London GRU VENONA traffic, or narrow the identity to a small, defensible candidate set.

Three recovered London→Moscow messages constrain BROWN. In July 1940 he was involved in arranging clandestine wireless infrastructure and served as a conduit between the Soviet side and STENLI/STANLEY. In August a Communist Party area secretary knew about a wireless set obtained through BROWN; POULTRY-DEALER described BROWN as devoted to the Soviet cause but talkative. On 4 September BROWN signed a report conveying information obtained “direct from the production line” at Henry Hughes & Son concerning a horizontal echo-sounder / ASDIC-related instrument.

The central ambiguity is **role**: the cables do not yet prove BROWN personally worked at Henry Hughes. He may have been (H1) the Henry Hughes insider, (H2) a handler/intermediary receiving from an insider, or (H3) a Communist-network collector with a Henry Hughes source.

## Why it belongs on the board

This is a named historical identity problem with primary evidence, hard occupational/network constraints and a falsifiable endpoint. A successful identification would connect an unresolved VENONA covername to a real person and clarify a small but concrete Soviet technical-intelligence network in wartime Britain.

It also fits the Hub's crack test better than a general espionage survey: candidates can lose if they cannot satisfy the 1940 chronology, Communist-network, clandestine-radio, geography and Henry Hughes access constraints.

As of 2026-09-05, the NSA/Wilson Center VENONA transcription still annotates BROWN as an unidentified covername. Targeted current searches did not locate a credible published identification. This remains subject to archival re-checking.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts

### Primary-cable constraints

1. **22 Jul 1940, London No. 798**
   - STENLI was being set up for clandestine W/T work.
   - A flat was to be found for BROWN with a wireless set.
   - STENLI was receiving Soviet material through BROWN.
   - BROWN was to be put in touch with MINISTR or another Soviet contact.

2. **13 Aug 1940, London No. 876**
   - POULTRY-DEALER reported that an Area Secretary of the Communist Party knew about a W/T set obtained from BROWN.
   - POULTRY-DEALER considered BROWN devoted to the Soviet cause but too talkative.
   - BROWN was to move back to his old flat as part of a security re-arrangement involving STANLEY.

3. **4 Sep 1940, London No. 976**
   - Information obtained “direct from the production line” at the Henry Hughes works concerned an instrument for determining underwater distance between submarines and surface vessels by reflection — described as a horizontal echo-sounder.
   - The message ends “No. 256 BROWN [BRAUN]”. The VENONA annotators note this is the only known London GRU message in which an agent outside the Embassy signs a message passed using Embassy cipher/facilities.

### First technical-source narrowing

Patent and technical-history evidence sharply narrows the relevant Henry Hughes circle:

- **Donald Orr Sproule** was a Henry Hughes engineer/inventor in Ilford/London and, before the cable, worked directly on echo-sounding and compressional/directional sound-wave apparatus.
- A 1939 British patent for **directional sound-wave apparatus** names D. O. Sproule, A. J. Hughes and Henry Hughes & Son and describes a rotatable acoustic reflector around a transmitting/receiving element.
- An earlier Henry Hughes patent by Albert Beaumont Wood, Frederick Daniel Smith and James Andrew McGeachy covers **measurement of distances by echo reception** using ultrasonic/magnetostrictive components.
- A 1998 peer-reviewed history of ultrasound states that Sproule was later forced to resign from Kelvin Hughes because of an alleged association with Russian spies. That sentence appears without an immediately visible source citation in the article, so it is a lead to verify, **not evidence sufficient to identify him as BROWN**.
- An online index to the UK National Archives SOE personnel-file series lists Donald Orr Sproule (born 26 May 1903) within **HS 9/1401**. The exact sub-file and its contents still require verification against the National Archives catalogue/file.

### Important traps

- Do **not** equate “information received direct from the production line” with “BROWN was employed on the production line.” The grammar permits an intermediary.
- Do **not** infer anything from the literal surname Brown. A later Kelvin Hughes engineer named Tom Brown replaced Sproule; that is only a surname coincidence unless independent evidence connects him to the 1940 network.
- “No. 256” is almost certainly a local outgoing-message sequence rather than an agent number: the immediately preceding London cable ends “No. 255 BARCh.”
- A Vassiliev-notebook “Braun” found in search is from a US/NKGB context and is not this London GRU BROWN.

## Success criteria

### Full crack
A real individual is identified who independently satisfies the primary-cable constraints, with documentary evidence tying that person to BROWN's Soviet/Communist network and explaining the Henry Hughes information channel.

### Strong partial crack
Reduce the search to a small candidate set and demonstrate which role model (Henry Hughes insider vs intermediary/handler) best explains all three cables, with at least one new archival/documentary link.

### Falsifiable candidate standard
A candidate should be tested against:
- alive/present in Britain in summer 1940;
- plausible London/Essex geography;
- access to or a direct contact inside Henry Hughes's echo-sounder work;
- Communist/Soviet trust-network evidence consistent with No. 876;
- ability to participate in clandestine W/T logistics / use of a flat;
- chronology consistent with all three messages;
- no contradiction from occupation, military service, known residence or security files.

## Notes

The next decisive move is not generic biography hunting. It is to identify the **small Henry Hughes echo-sounder personnel/source network in 1939–40**, then intersect it with **Ilford/Hainault-area Communist Party and security/intelligence records**, while separately testing whether Sproule's later “Russian spies” allegation has a traceable MI5/SOE documentary basis.
