# The 1940 CPGB “Area Secretary” — identity target

**Date:** 2026-09-06  
**Purpose:** turn No. 876's vague-sounding Communist Party clue into a specific organisational identity problem.

## Why this clue matters

London GRU No. 876 (13 Aug 1940) says POULTRY-DEALER reported that an **Area Secretary of the Communist Party** knew about a W/T set obtained from/through BROWN.

This is potentially one of the strongest BROWN constraints because **Area Secretary was a real CPGB organisational title**, not simply a translator's generic phrase for “local Communist official.”

If the relevant Area Secretary can be named and their files/contact network reconstructed, BROWN's candidate set may collapse rapidly.

## Organisational terminology — correction

Do **not** equate the No. 876 Area Secretary with **Ted Bramley** merely because Bramley was a major London Communist.

Evidence shows:

- Ted Bramley became **London District Secretary / organiser in 1937** and remained in that London-wide role through the war.
- Other CPGB biographies explicitly use **Area Secretary** for subordinate/geographic organisations — e.g. Bill Alexander as **Merseyside Area Secretary**.
- Later first-person CPGB material describes an **Area Conference** and named Area Secretary operating inside the London structure.

Therefore “Area Secretary” and “London District Secretary” are distinct enough that identifying Bramley as the cable's official without further evidence would be an error.

## Why Bramley still matters

Bramley remains a high-value network node:

1. Oral-history scholarship places him as a Communist **organiser in Ilford** before the Spanish Civil War.
2. By 1937 he was London District Secretary, making him organisationally senior to local/sub-district structures.
3. A declassified Security Service file on Edith Tudor-Hart records that in March 1937 Bramley wrote to W. F. R. MacCartney — recently released after an espionage conviction — asking him to receive Tudor-Hart. The same MI5 narrative connects Tudor-Hart's circle to Percy Glading's espionage investigation.

This establishes espionage-adjacent party-network context around Bramley, but it does **not** identify him as the No. 876 Area Secretary or prove he knew BROWN.

## Ilford/Plessey relevance

The two leading BROWN candidates now have direct Ilford industrial/political relevance:

- **Harry Fraser** — Communist, International Brigader, obtained a Plessey Ilford job after Spain and stayed in Ilford around the period before his Oct 1940 marriage.
- **George Alfred Barnard** — Communist, directly self-dated at Plessey by Mar 1940, active workplace representative and involved in design/production including special radio sets.

This makes the identity of the CPGB official responsible for **Ilford / East London industrial organisation in summer 1940** a high-information target.

However, the cable itself does not say the Area Secretary was geographically based at Henry Hughes or Plessey. The Area Secretary might belong to BROWN's residence, party branch, industrial group or another district. Geography must be derived, not assumed.

## Working organisational hypotheses

### A1 — Ilford / East London geographic Area Secretary
Prediction:
- the official appears in Ilford/Goodmayes/Walthamstow/Hainault party records;
- likely contacts include Plessey workers and possibly Henry Hughes workers;
- could connect directly to Fraser/Barnard.

### A2 — Industrial Area / group secretary
Prediction:
- “Area” may relate to industrial organisation rather than municipal geography;
- records may sit under engineering/electrical/trade-union CPGB organisation.

### A3 — Different London area tied to BROWN's “old flat”
Prediction:
- if BROWN moved between East London/Ilford and an older London address, the Area Secretary may belong to the older location rather than the factory locality.
- Fraser's known pre-Spain Hackney address makes this testable if his 1940 residence can be resolved.

## Archive targets

### CPGB archives / People's History Museum / Labour History Archive
Prioritise:
- London District Congress / conference records for **1939 and 1940**;
- delegate lists and officer lists;
- London District organisational reports;
- area/sub-district reports;
- industrial department / engineering-group records;
- Ilford, East London, Hackney, Walthamstow and relevant works-branch material;
- Plessey works branch or factory bulletin records if present.

### Security Service files
Search known senior/network figures' files for:
- “Area Secretary”;
- Ilford / Plessey / Henry Hughes / Hainault;
- Fraser / Barnard;
- W/T, wireless, transmitter, radio set;
- industrial source / factory / shop steward.

High-value file families include Dave Springhall and other CPGB London organisers.

### Newspapers / Daily Worker
A mundane route may work: public CPGB meetings, conferences and candidate notices often name local organisers/secretaries. Search 1939–40 Ilford / East London Communist notices for the formal title **Area Secretary**.

## Falsifiable outputs

A useful result is not “Ted Bramley was important.” It is one of:

1. **named 1940 Area Secretary** for the relevant CPGB area;
2. documentary evidence that the title refers to a specific London sub-area and its boundaries;
3. a direct contact chain from that official to Fraser, Barnard, Plessey or Henry Hughes;
4. evidence that the assumed Ilford geography is wrong, redirecting the candidate search.

## Current state

**Open, high priority.**

The continuation pass establishes that “Area Secretary” should be treated as a **specific office to identify**, and that Bramley's London District role is too senior/different to fill it automatically.

This clue now ranks alongside the Henry Hughes project roster as the most likely archival route to a crack.
