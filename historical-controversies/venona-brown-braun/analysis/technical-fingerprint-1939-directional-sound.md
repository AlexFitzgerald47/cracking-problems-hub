# Technical fingerprint — does No. 976 match the Sproule/Hughes 1939 directional-sound project?

**Date:** 2026-09-05 continuation pass.

## Question

Can the 4 Sep 1940 VENONA description of a Henry Hughes "horizontal echo-sounder" be narrowed beyond generic echo sounding to a specific pre-war Henry Hughes project?

## Primary cable wording

London GRU No. 976 reports that details "received direct from the production line" at Henry Hughes works compromised an instrument:

- for determining distance through water between submarines and surface vessels;
- based on reflection;
- described as a "horizontal echo-sounder."

The word **horizontal** is the discriminating clue. Ordinary depth sounders point downward; horizontal ranging requires directional/scanning acoustic apparatus.

## Strongest patent match found

### GB591533A / GB908039A

**Title:** *Improvements in and relating to directional sound wave apparatus*  
**Applicants/inventors in the abstract:** Henry Hughes & Son Ltd, D. O. Sproule, A. J. Hughes  
**Filed:** 22 Mar 1939  
**Published:** 21 Aug 1947

Google Patents' abstract describes:

- an electro-acoustic **transmitting or receiving** element;
- located at the focus/focal axis of an acoustic reflector;
- reflector mounted to **rotate** around the element;
- a motor driving the reflector;
- a modification in which a paraboloidal reflector rotates independently about **two axes**.

The patent is classified as sound focusing/directing/scanning using **mechanical steering** by moving a reflector.

## Why this is materially stronger than "Sproule worked on echo sounders"

1. **Directional / steerable geometry.** A rotatable acoustic beam is exactly the kind of architecture needed to turn vertical depth sounding into horizontal target ranging.
2. **Transmit + receive.** The patent explicitly allows the focal element to be a transmitter or receiver, consistent with pulse/echo ranging architecture.
3. **Vehicle/structure mounting.** The claimed apparatus is designed to be fixed to a structure or vehicle while the reflector scans.
4. **Timing.** Filed in March 1939, it existed well before the Sep 1940 leak.
5. **Secrecy/publication timing.** The British application was not published until **1947**. Therefore a Sep 1940 report matching its directional architecture cannot be dismissed as simply repeating the published GB591533A specification.

This does **not** prove No. 976 describes GB591533A. It establishes a high-information prediction: the compromised "horizontal echo-sounder" should be compared against this project and its production/test team before broader Henry Hughes searches.

## Important nuance: public echo-sounder knowledge versus unpublished directional detail

Henry Hughes had older patents on depth/distance measurement by echo reception, and generic echo-ranging was not secret in 1940. This matters because the intelligence value of No. 976 cannot rest merely on "Henry Hughes uses reflected sound to measure distance."

The potentially sensitive novelty is the **horizontal/directional implementation** and whatever production detail the cable omitted.

Thus future archive work should ask:

> Was the 1940 Henry Hughes production item a steerable/rotating-reflector horizontal ranging set derived from the Sproule–A. J. Hughes 1939 application?

If yes, source searches can be restricted sharply to personnel with access to that project's drawings, assembly, testing, Admiralty liaison or production line.

## Result

**Advance:** No. 976 can now be attacked as a likely **directional-acoustic project leak**, not a generic Henry Hughes factory leak. GB591533A/GB908039A is the strongest exact technical match found so far because it predates the cable, provides steerable directional acoustics, and remained unpublished until 1947.

**Not established:** that the leaked device was literally this patent implementation; that Sproule was the source; or that Sproule was BROWN.
