# Candidate dossier — George Alfred Barnard

**Status:** serious BROWN/BRAUN candidate under the CPGB industrial-intermediary model; **not identified**.  
**Date:** 2026-09-06 continuation pass.

## Why Barnard emerged

The BROWN cables require an unusual blend of roles:

- trusted Communist/Soviet-network intermediary;
- involved in clandestine W/T logistics, though STANLEY rather than BROWN is explicitly the radio technician;
- technically literate enough to pass industrial/military information;
- by 4 Sep 1940 able to channel details described as received direct from Henry Hughes production.

George Alfred Barnard is the first candidate found who is independently placeable **inside a major Ilford defence-electronics works before the BROWN traffic**, was a longstanding Communist, had a real workplace political role, and by his own account worked across design/production including special radio sets.

He is therefore complementary to Harry Fraser: Barnard has stronger independently dated industrial/W/T relevance; Fraser has stronger independently documented International Brigade/Springhall/Soviet-wireless-network adjacency.

## Primary BROWN constraints

### London No. 798 — 22 Jul 1940
- STANLEY receives Soviet material through BROWN.
- BROWN is to be put in touch with MINISTR / another Soviet contact.
- A flat and W/T set are part of the arrangement.
- STANLEY, not BROWN, is the person described as a radio technician.

**Implication:** BROWN need not be a radio operator; an industrially connected Communist conduit with enough technical competence to handle radio/logistics is compatible.

### London No. 876 — 13 Aug 1940
- a Communist Party Area Secretary knows about a W/T set obtained through/from BROWN;
- POULTRY-DEALER describes BROWN as devoted to the Soviet cause but talkative;
- BROWN is told to move back to an old flat.

### London No. 976 — 4 Sep 1940
- BROWN signs a report carrying Henry Hughes information about a horizontal echo-sounder;
- details are described as received direct from the production line.

## The strongest primary/self-authored evidence

### 1. Barnard explicitly dates himself at Plessey before the VENONA traffic

In a later correction published by the *London Review of Books*, Barnard corrects a wartime anecdote and states that he was working for Plessey in March 1941, adding that **in March 1940 they were doing the same things**.

This matters because many biographies merely say "in 1940". Barnard's own dating places him inside the company before BROWN appears in July 1940.

### 2. Barnard's own 1993 memoir describes his Plessey role

In "During the War: Personal Recollections" (Statistical Society of Canada, *Liaison*, May 1993), Barnard says he had the good fortune to be employed as a **roving mathematician by Plessey**. He lists wartime products including:

- bomb cases;
- aircraft fuel systems;
- anti-aircraft gun predictors;
- **special types of radio sets**;
- other items;

and says he was able to contribute to **design and production with many of these**.

This is unusually close to the profile expected for a technically literate industrial intermediary: broad cross-project access rather than one narrow production job.

### 3. Shop-steward / MI5 detail must be handled carefully

The same memoir says that by May 1942 Barnard had been elected **Shop Stewards' Convenor for the Plessey Company**. When he was recruited to the Ministry of Supply, he says MI5 held up the appointment because he was regarded as a **disruptive trade-union militant**.

Do **not** turn this into "MI5 suspected Barnard of spying." The memoir gives a political/industrial-vetting reason, not an espionage allegation. Its value here is that it confirms Barnard sat at the intersection of technical production and organised workplace politics.

## Possible Fraser–Barnard workplace overlap

Facts:
- Fraser returned from Spain in Dec 1938 and subsequently obtained a job at Plessey, Ilford.
- Barnard's own statement places him at Plessey by Mar 1940.
- Fraser later worked for Cossor during the war, but the exact transition date is unknown.

Prediction:
> If Fraser remained at Plessey after March 1940, Fraser and Barnard were co-present in the same Ilford defence-electronics works during the period immediately preceding BROWN's July traffic.

This is a high-value falsifiable proposition. Do **not** state they knew each other until the employer dates/department records establish overlap.

## Candidate interpretation

### Best-fit model: H3 industrial/CPGB intermediary

Barnard's strongest fit is not as a Henry Hughes insider. It is as an industrially connected Communist with:

- broad access to defence production;
- technical literacy;
- radio-set work;
- workplace political standing;
- an Ilford industrial base.

Under H3, he could plausibly know industrial Communist contacts outside Plessey, including a source at Henry Hughes. That last step remains entirely unproven.

### Why H1 is currently weak

No evidence places Barnard at Henry Hughes. No direct business/project link between Plessey and the relevant Henry Hughes horizontal-acoustics project has been found. The phrase "direct from the production line" therefore cannot presently be mapped to Barnard himself.

## Predictions / kill tests

### B1 — 1940 residence
Recover Barnard's 1939 Register / 1940 electoral-register address.

**Strong update:** a summer-1940 move between a new Ilford location and an earlier London flat consistent with No. 876.

**Weakening condition:** continuous residence pattern incompatible with the cable's security move.

### B2 — shop-steward chronology
Determine when Barnard became a shop steward, not merely when he became convenor in 1942.

**Strong update:** he already held workplace political office by summer 1940 and can be linked to the CPGB London/Ilford area structure.

### B3 — Plessey CPGB structure
Identify the Plessey works branch, industrial group, district organiser or Area Secretary in 1940.

**Strong update:** Barnard appears in the same local Party reporting chain as the Area Secretary implicated in No. 876.

### B4 — Fraser overlap
Resolve Harry Fraser's Plessey→Cossor date.

**Strong update:** Fraser remains at Plessey after Mar 1940, creating a real co-worker intersection between the two strongest BROWN-network candidates.

### B5 — Henry Hughes bridge
Search Plessey/AEU/ETU/CPGB workplace records and Henry Hughes staff/union records for shared names.

**Required for strong identification:** some independent route from Barnard's network into the Henry Hughes horizontal-acoustics production/test chain.

### B6 — Soviet/security files
Search Security Service/CPGB/Comintern records for Barnard.

**Strong update:** direct Soviet-contact, GRU, W/T, Springhall, industrial-intelligence or Henry Hughes reference.

**Important negative:** the 1942 MI5 employment delay in Barnard's memoir is not itself such evidence.

## Current verdict

**PROMOTE TO SERIOUS CANDIDATE, NO SOLVE CLAIM.**

Barnard is currently a better fit than Fraser on the **independently dated Ilford industrial + radio-production** constraints, but worse on the **Soviet-intelligence-adjacent personal network** constraints. Neither candidate has the load-bearing Henry Hughes bridge or direct GRU evidence.

The key new research object is therefore no longer one man. It is the **1940 Plessey Communist/industrial network**, especially shop stewards and the local Party area structure, intersected with Henry Hughes personnel.
