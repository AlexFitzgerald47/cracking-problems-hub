# STANLEY role separation — BROWN is not required to be the radio technician

**Date:** 2026-09-06

## Why this matters

The first BROWN candidate searches gave substantial weight to radio/electronics competence because the July–August 1940 messages revolve around clandestine W/T arrangements.

Re-reading the surrounding STANLEY traffic shows that this over-constrained BROWN.

## Primary evidence

### London No. 798 — 22 Jul 1940
The network intends to arrange a flat / W/T infrastructure involving BROWN and STANLEY. STANLEY receives Soviet material through BROWN.

The structure already suggests different functions:
- **BROWN:** conduit / intermediary / logistics;
- **STANLEY:** radio-side operator.

### London No. 816 — 26 Jul 1940
The later message makes the division much clearer. Soviet personnel are using **STANLEY's W/T set** and consulting him about technical receiver/current/converter problems.

VENONA commentary describes STANLEY as:
- Canadian;
- working-class;
- a radio technician.

### London No. 876 — 13 Aug 1940
After the security leak through the Communist Party Area Secretary:
- **BROWN** is told to move back to his old flat;
- **STANLEY** is told to stop radio work for a fortnight and move to a flat in another area.

Again, the radio operation is explicitly STANLEY's work.

## Result

**Constraint correction:** BROWN does **not** need to be a trained radio operator or professional wireless engineer.

A plausible BROWN can instead be:
- a politically trusted Communist/Soviet intermediary;
- a collector/conduit for technical or industrial information;
- involved in arranging flats/contacts/material;
- technically literate enough to interact with the radio network without being the operator.

This materially affects candidate scoring.

## Candidate consequences

### Harry Fraser
His electronics background remains useful, but failure to find direct clandestine-radio operation is no longer a major negative.

### George Barnard
His work on special radio sets remains relevant industrial competence, but he does not need to be the transmitter operator.

### Wilfrid Vernon
His lack of identified 1940 W/T operating activity is likewise not fatal. His proven Soviet technical-document conduit role becomes relatively more important.

## New scoring weights

Increase weight on:
1. Communist/Soviet trust network;
2. intermediary / document-handling role;
3. ability to reach industrial technical sources;
4. residence / flat chronology;
5. contacts with Soviet mission / GRU handlers;
6. connection to POULTRY-DEALER / Area Secretary.

Reduce weight on:
- amateur-radio licence;
- professional radio servicing;
- transmitter construction;
- direct W/T operating skill.

Those are primarily **STANLEY** constraints.

## Falsifiable implication

If BROWN is ultimately identified, the biography does **not** have to explain the technical receiver/converter work. Instead it should explain why Soviet material and contacts passed **through BROWN to STANLEY** and why BROWN's accommodation/security arrangements mattered.
