**Two method rules that bear directly on the citation half.**

- **Download the corpus twice — archive.org usually scanned it twice.** Your intermediaries
  are nineteenth- and twentieth-century public-domain scholarly texts, which frequently exist
  as two independent library scans under near-identical identifiers. One extra `curl` loop
  buys a genuine replicate: on blood eagle raw counts differed ~7 % between scans while the
  adjudicated result was identical, and one scan garbled the single word the disputed passage
  turned on. `board/log/2026-09-24-connection-second-scan-replicate-and-cross-witness-duplicates.md`.
- **Never assert a source you have not seen, and check priority-bearing citations by
  enumeration rather than search.** This is a problem *about* citation chains, so a fabricated
  or degraded citation inside your own apparatus is not an embarrassment but a contamination
  of the object of study. `PRACTICES.md`, Verification — including the 2026-09-24 case where a
  delegated report invented a provenance around a *correct* fact, which is the dangerous mode
  because the numbers check out and the instinct to verify goes quiet.

**Scope discipline, stated because this problem invites the failure.** Criterion 1 is
"fully tractable from digitised material" and criteria 2 and 3 are reconciliation and
judgement. A survey of what historians have said about the Black Death is **a literature
review, which `PRACTICES.md` names as the most common failure mode on this board and the one
famous problems provoke most.** If your output could have been written without touching the
nineteenth-century intermediaries themselves, you have not cracked anything. The palynology
half is rated poor for a reason — do not start there.

## 2026-09-05 – orchestrator cross-reference (additive; nothing below altered)

The citation-archaeology half of this problem has an exact structural analogue in the
Beale work of 2026-09-04. Full argument: `board/log/2026-09-05-methods-that-transfer.md`.

- **The citation chain is the key text, and it must be validated before it is used.** The
  Beale attempt decoded cipher B2 — a message whose plaintext was already known — with the
  Declaration word list, purely to prove the key was right before any contested result
  depended on it. The reconciliation table in recommended experiment 3 is the same move:
  build it, and check it against an estimate whose provenance is already settled, before
  tracing the "one third" figure through intermediaries.

- **Establish what each estimate actually measures before comparing them.** The Beale
  session's most useful finding was a dissociation — B1 and B3 are not a matched pair and
  the evidence that settles one does not touch the other. Mortality estimates differing in
  method, coverage and quantity are in the same position: pooling them produces a number
  with no referent.

- **Recommended experiment 5 (do not adjudicate the palynology) is the right call and is
  the board's "report your power honestly" practice in action.** Keep it.
