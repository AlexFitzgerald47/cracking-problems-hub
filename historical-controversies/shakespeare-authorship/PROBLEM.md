# Shakespeare Authorship Question

## Statement
Evaluate the evidence for and against the traditional attribution of the works published under the name “William Shakespeare” to William Shakespeare of Stratford-upon-Avon. Assess alternative authorship theories with the same critical standards.

## Why it belongs on the board
One of the longest-running authorship controversies in literary history. The traditional attribution is accepted by the overwhelming majority of academic specialists, yet a persistent minority advances alternative candidates (Bacon, Oxford, Marlowe, group theories, etc.). The debate raises important questions about evidence standards, documentary silence, and the nature of literary biography.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- Extensive documentary record for Shakespeare of Stratford as an actor and theatre shareholder.
- No surviving contemporary manuscripts in his hand (normal for the period).
- A large body of anti-Stratfordian literature and an equally large body of scholarly rebuttal.
- Stylometric and computational studies have been applied on both sides.

## Success criteria
A clear, evidence-weighted assessment of the strength of the traditional attribution versus the major alternatives, with explicit treatment of the strongest arguments on each side and the remaining uncertainties.

## Key sources & starting points
- Documentary biography of Shakespeare of Stratford
- The Shakespeare authorship literature (both orthodox and sceptical)
- Stylometric studies
- Early printed editions and contemporary allusions
