### Evidence-limited watchlist

**Oweynagat / Rathcroghan second ogham inscription** — keep visible, but do not spend a top cracking slot on it yet. The surviving text is conventionally represented only as a fragment such as `QR G SMU` and is described as too incomplete for a confident reading. Reopen if older drawings, squeezes, archival photography or new imaging preserve strokes no longer legible in situ.

### Immediate working order

3. **Ennis bead** — excellent sign-reading problem, but the tiny object would benefit from higher-resolution multi-angle data.

### Cracker rule for this cluster

Before claiming one, freeze the exact glyph transcription from the best available imagery and branch every ambiguous stroke rather than silently choosing a reading. The first experiment must compete ordinary Irish/ogham, personal-name formulae, scribal exercise, cryptic/learned ogham, magical notation, and non-Irish/script-switching explanations where the object warrants them.

## Irish / British intelligence ciphers

These are the highest-interest intelligence-code seams located so far. Do not confuse a document that merely contains code with a genuinely unresolved cipher: the first task is always to prove the plaintext/key has not already been recovered elsewhere in the archive or literature.

| Priority | Target | Why it belongs | Evidence access | Current blocker |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| **C-1** | **Michael Collins Papers — mine for orphan British/RIC ciphertexts** | The digitised Collins intelligence correspondence contains intercepted Crown Forces code material and explicit codebreaking activity. A surviving ciphertext with no surviving key/plaintext would be an excellent original crack. | **Excellent for discovery.** Military Archives provides a digitised, downloadable Collins corpus. | **Discovery-first:** must classify every candidate as solved/keyed/unsolved before promotion to a standalone cipher. |
| **C-2** | **IRA Director of Intelligence `VORFYDCGT` message, 25 Oct 1923** | Publicly catalogued coded token embedded in a real IRA intelligence memo; no published decipherment located in the current verification pass. The phrase around it may constrain meaning. | **Good enough to begin contextual attack.** Catalogue text is public; surrounding O'Malley/IRA intelligence material may supply cribs. | **Short-text power:** nine letters alone are underdetermined; success depends on reconstructing the internal code context, especially what “100's methods” means. |
| **C-3** | **IRA Army Council ↔ Clan-na-Gael coded correspondence, NLI MS 50,046/1–143 (1929–31)** | 143-item archive with many coded passages and at least one dispatch accompanied by decoded coded sections — potentially ideal crib/key material for other messages. | **Metadata excellent; primary images uncertain.** NLI catalogue is public. | **Major access blocker:** manuscript images are not confirmed online; first move is reproduction/digitisation access plus proof that remaining coded passages were not already solved by Mahon/Gillogly or others. |
| **C-4** | **WWII British carrier-pigeon cipher (`AOAKN HVPKD FNFJW YIDDC ...`)** | GCHQ publicly regards the 27-group message as unresolved after examining many proposed solutions. Historical identification of sender/unit/destination could recover the missing cryptographic context. | **Excellent ciphertext access.** Ciphertext and official discussion are public. | **Potentially structural:** if one-time material/codebook is truly lost, ciphertext-only solution may be impossible; prioritize identity/unit/codebook reconstruction over brute-force pattern fitting. |

### Immediate working order

1. **Mine the Collins Papers** — best chance of discovering a fresh, attackable cipher with complete digital evidence.
2. **`VORFYDCGT`** — tiny but unusually contextual; attack the intelligence-network semantics rather than the nine letters in isolation.
3. **NLI MS 50,046/1–143** — potentially the richest seam, but only after primary-image access is secured and prior decryption coverage is audited.
4. **British pigeon cipher** — genuine unsolved cipher, but lower expected value unless archival work identifies sender/unit/system or parallel traffic.

### Source anchors

- Irish Military Archives, *The Collins Papers 1917–1922*, digitised collection and codebreaking/intelligence correspondence: https://www.militaryarchives.ie/en/online-collections/the-collins-papers-1917-1922
- National Library of Ireland, Ernie O'Malley material, IRA Director of Intelligence memo dated 25 October 1923 containing the coded token `VORFYDCGT` in the sentence “Can any of 100's methods be used now that no VORFYDCGT?”.
- National Library of Ireland, **MS 50,046/1–143**, IRA Army Council / Clan-na-Gael correspondence, 1929–1931; catalogue notes multiple cipher/code passages and a June 1931 dispatch with two coded sections fully decoded on an accompanying slip.
- Hugh Mahon & James J. Gillogly, *Decoding the IRA*; use as the mandatory prior-solution check before claiming any 1920s/30s IRA ciphertext.
- GCHQ, *Pigeon takes secret message to grave*: official discussion of the WWII carrier-pigeon ciphertext and why submitted solutions have not been accepted.

### Cracker rule for this cluster

For each intelligence cipher, first build a **solution-status ledger**: exact ciphertext, date, sender/recipient if known, archive reference, known codebook/key material, known plaintext fragments, prior published attacks, and whether a solution already exists elsewhere in the archive. Do not spend compute on a ciphertext until that ledger is complete. Short messages should be attacked through network, chronology, codebook, vocabulary, unit and parallel-traffic context before brute-force language scoring.
