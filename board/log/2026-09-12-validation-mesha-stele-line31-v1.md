## What the source supports

Langlois does report another squeeze at the French Academy, superimposed on the Louvre squeeze in Figures 12–13. He describes crossing recesses consistent with taw, but explicitly retains uncertainty. His reported lack of a descender weakens nun; it does not exclude it. Printed page 45 still includes both taw/nun alternatives in its lexical discussion. The published composite is therefore real, but the claimed decisive closure is stronger than its source. The inspected passage supplies neither a dated provenance nor an independently documented pre-break acquisition for the Academy squeeze. That pedigree is unverified here, not disproved.

Richelle and Burlingame specifically challenge whether the ambiguous marks are scribal strokes, including a diagonal coinciding with a squeeze wrinkle. Their objection concerns identifying physical evidence, not merely demanding pristine letters. They report examining both squeeze faces and RTI and retain `BTDWD` as a possible reconstruction. These are competing primary observations requiring adjudication.

## Why the full criterion is not met

1. **No reproducible stroke freeze.** The repository supplies letter-level judgments, but no coordinates, untraced crops, acquisition metadata, registration transforms, or groove/damage classifications. A figure containing an overlay cannot independently establish the alignment or provenance asserted by the claim. The rendered image shows ambiguous relief; I cannot identify the claimed crossing channels with sufficient confidence to reproduce the stroke classification.
2. **Replication of relief is not automatically replication of engraving.** Even independently made impressions may reproduce damage already present on the stone. The claim needs a discriminator between incised channels and shared non-letter surface relief, not merely recurrence across representations.
3. **Unresolved dalet and boundary.** The claimant acknowledges uncertainty at the first dalet but upgrades the sequence to a frozen five-letter reading. No new raw test resolves the wrinkle objection or classifies the terminal depression against secure dividers. Those missing decisions affect both the letters and segmentation.
4. **The burden shifts improperly.** The solve case asks validators to find a better complete reading. The registered criterion instead requires the claimant to demonstrate a substantial comparative advantage after a defensible physical freeze. An unidentified damaged sign need not supply a fully named rival to prevent that demonstration.
5. **The proposed held-out test is not demonstrated as held out.** The branch-adjudication file already discusses the Tel Dan parallel and the preferred phrase. Consulting an independent inscription is useful comparison, but no documented blindness makes it an independent validation of the claimant's stroke selection.
