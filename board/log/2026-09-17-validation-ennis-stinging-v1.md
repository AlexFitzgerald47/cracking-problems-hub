verdict: PARTIAL

reasoning:

**1. The cryptographic core is correct, and the alphabet checks out against a source `SOURCES.md` does not cite.**

I reimplemented the alphabet and shift from scratch without importing repo code. `D`=6, `M`=10, `V`/Fearn=2, `A`=15; minus 3 mod 20 gives 3, 7, 19, 12 = `S`, `T`, `I`, `NG`. `DMVAVA` → `STINGING`. The mapping holds exactly as stated.

`SOURCES.md` cites **no source at all** for the 20-letter order, which is load-bearing for the entire claim. I verified it independently against the Unicode Ogham block (U+1681–U+1694), whose canonical code-point order carries the traditional sequence in its character names: BEITH, LUIS, FEARN, SAIL, NION, UATH, DAIR, TINNE, COLL, CEIRT, MUIN, GORT, NGEADAL, STRAIF, RUIS, AILM, ONN, UR, EADHADH, IODHADH. DAIR is index 6, MUIN 10, FEARN 2, AILM 15; −3 lands on SAIL, TINNE, IODHADH, NGEADAL. The claimed order is right and the arithmetic is right. One presentational inaccuracy: `PROBLEM.md`, `SOLUTION.md` and `PROGRESS.md` write the alphabet as `B L V S N`, substituting the epigraphic transliteration `V` for the letter Fearn (`F`). Only `analysis/stinging-candidate-2026-09-07.md` states this openly. It does not affect the result, since only position matters, but the headline alphabet as printed is not the learned-ogham alphabet as normally written.

**2. I verified the raw transcription against the live primary source, and it does not say `DMVAVA`.**

I fetched `lguariento/og-h-am` `XML/I-CLA/I-CLA-003.xml` directly (HTTP 200, 10,888 bytes). Confirmed first-hand: transliteration `?DMVA?VA`, ogham line `?ᚇᚋᚃᚐ?ᚃᚐ`, editor Nora White, both edition divs tagged `xml:lang="ga"`; Macalister's `ATUCMLU` carried as `<rdg source="MAC1945">`; photogrammetry and RTI both captured 2023-12-05 (Metashape / Relight, Megan Kasten); and a **commented-out** `<media>` element reading `3d model by Megan on 3dhop`, which independently confirms the repo's statement that no public 3D model is exposed. The apparatus reads: "an unidentifed symbol to the left, not meeting the stemline and which doesn't match any known ogham character… Following this are some relatively clear ogham characters: DMVA (or perhaps DMLO). At this point things become a little strange as we have what appears to be a split in the stemline (possibly to avoid running into the perforation), one branch goes to the right at an angle (roughly 45º) and **appears to have the ogham letters VA repeated**, although the final A is quite short and looks like an X at the end of the stemline. The other branch, if that's what this is, goes to the left at a similar angle and length with just an oblique stroke to the right at the end."

Every source claim the repo makes about this record is accurate. But two things follow that the claim does not confront.

(a) "the ogham letters VA repeated" on the right branch is genuinely ambiguous between "VA, repeating the VA just read" (→ payload `DMVAVA`, the claim) and "VA occurring twice on that branch" (→ payload `DMVAVAVA`). The claimants adopt the first without noting the second exists. I tested the second: `DMVAVAVA` yields **zero** English or Irish hits across both directions and all 20 shifts. So one of the two natural readings of the single sentence the whole payload rests on destroys the solution outright, and it was never tested.

(b) The editors transliterate **seven** signs, with the second `?` sitting exactly between the core and the `VA`. The claim reaches six by deleting both `?`s. That is a decision, not an observation.

**3. The claim cannot absorb the anomalous marks as characters at all — this is where the Crack target bites.**

I ran the editors' actual seven-sign string `?DMVA?VA` with both `?` free over all 20 ogham values, in both reading directions, at all 20 shifts: **16,000 combinations, 0 English hits.** There is no assignment of the two anomalous marks under which this cipher produces an English word. The claim therefore does not *explain* the anomalous marks; it *excludes* them, and the exclusion is load-bearing rather than incidental. `SOLUTION.md` calls the detached `<` "non-phonetic" and the oblique stroke "secondary/aborted/structural", while `evidence-ledger.md` honestly lists five live alternatives for the latter ("structural, corrective, delimiter-like, aborted, or phonetic") and picks none. Clause (2) of the Crack target is not met.

Clause (3), direction: I implemented the correct other-direction transform (aicme side-flip B↔H, L↔D, F↔T, S↔C, N↔Q with vowel and M-aicme signs invariant, then reversal) — the claimants' `ATATML` / `ATODML` are derived correctly, which is a genuinely competent piece of work that I confirm rather than dispute. But the reverse direction yields nothing, so the reading direction is selected *by* the plaintext rather than explained *to* the plaintext. That is circular with respect to a criterion that asks direction to be explained.

Clause (4), circular/closure geometry: unresolved by the claimants' own 2026-09-08 audit, which explicitly downgrades this from "resolved fork" to "unresolved loop/cycle traversal". Not met.

So the claim satisfies at most a conditional version of clause (1) and fails clauses (2), (3) and (4). Under the pre-registered standard as written, this is not a solve.

**4. Nothing survives if the transcription is a rival — and that is the claim's real exposure.**

Tested `MTBCML`, Macalister's `ATUCMLU`, `LMCBDV` (Glenfahan comparator) and `LMCBTM` (Westropp's Fahan form), each in both directions across the full 160-map affine family: **zero English hits for all four**. `DMLOVA` and `DMLOVAVA`: zero. The claimants' assertion that only `DMVAVA` works is confirmed, and confirmed more broadly than they tested it. The right way to read that result cuts both ways: the transform is highly specific, *and* 100% of the claim's weight rests on one sentence of one 2023 examination report whose own editors flag the alternative `DMLO`, mark two of seven signs unread, and have not upgraded their transliteration in the two years since.

One sub-result the repo does not report and which a reader could easily over-credit: the **uncontested** core `DMVA` alone gives `STING` at the identical −3 key. That looks like corroboration but is not — P(a random 4-sign sequence reaches some English word under some shift) is **41.98%** (CMUdict). The 4-token sub-hit carries essentially no information; only the joint 6-token fit does.

**5. The null runs, but it is under-powered in two specific ways, and I ran the missing tests.**

*Missing test A — the matched null.* `SOLUTION.md` §2 and `PROGRESS.md` present "exact repeated morphology `VA VA → I-NG I-NG`" and `HANDOVER.md` lists "the token structure is exact" as independent support. **This is mathematically forced and carries zero evidential weight.** A monoalphabetic shift is a bijection applied uniformly, so any ciphertext of form ABCDCD *must* decrypt to a plaintext of form ABCDCD, at every one of the 20 shifts. The claimants' null never conditions on this. I ran the matched null: among six-token dictionary forms whose ogham shape is ABCDCD, CMUdict gives 35 orbits out of 8,000 = **0.4375%**, TextBlob 11/8,000 = 0.1375% — i.e. *at or slightly above* the unconditional rates of 0.4203% / 0.0898%. Conditioning on the repeat does not make the hit rarer. The hits in that class are dominated by exactly the family you would expect (`bringing`, `clinging`, `cringing`, `flinging`, `slinging`, `stinging`), which is the point: `STINGING` is one member of a small but real `-INGING` family that the ciphertext's shape selects for.

*Missing test B — language power.* The null tests English only. The EpiDoc tags this text `xml:lang="ga"`, and the repo's own caveat (`analysis/…`, point 2) concedes an English ogham inscription before Windele's 1840 drawing would be "unusual". A null with power must charge for the language the milieu actually favours. I fetched a 120k-entry Irish hunspell dictionary (`wooorm/dictionaries` `ga`), accent-stripped to 108,664 headwords, and computed: P(random six-sign sequence reaches an **Irish** word under some shift) = **0.2464%**; the English∪Irish union rate = **0.6526%**, versus the 0.406% the repo advertises. Charged against the claimants' own 4- and 8-branch union bounds that is **2.6% to 5.2%**, before charging anything for the 160-map affine family they also searched, for the decision to delete two signs, or for the loop's undetermined start point. That is suggestive; it is not a decisive p-value, and `SOLUTION.md` is right to say so ("supporting evidence, not a formal post-selection p-value"). Separately: **zero** Irish hits across 480 loop-model combinations. The only language that yields a word is the one the period makes least likely.

**6. Honest search budget, including the one the corrected physical model actually implies.**

The 2026-09-08 audit re-modelled the inscription as a **cycle with no established start point**. By the claimants' own physical model every rotation of the payload is then an admissible reading, which multiplies the budget by 6 per path — and that expanded null was never run. I ran it: 2 payloads (`DMVAVA`, `DMLOVA`) × 6 rotations × 2 directions × 20 shifts = **480 combinations, 1 English hit** (`stinging`). Across everything I tested — roughly 19,000 (transcription × direction × shift/map) combinations spanning the claim payload, both cores, all loop rotations, both directions, wildcarded anomalous signs, four rival inherited readings, the full affine family, and English plus Irish lexicons — the total English-word yield was three: `STINGING` (the claim), `STING` (its own uninformative 4-token prefix), and `HAIM` from `DMLO` reversed under an affine map. **The claim's uniqueness assertion survives a considerably harder test than the one it was given.** That is the strongest thing I can say for it, and I say it without reservation: this is not a plausible reading fitted to a corpus small enough to fit anything. It is a narrow, specific, reproducible hit.

It is just not a hit against the criterion that was pre-registered.

**7. Governance finding: the claimants' own adversarial analysis said not to file this.**

`analysis/stinging-candidate-2026-09-07.md` concludes verbatim: "**PARTIAL / high-value candidate. Do not post a solve claim yet.**" and "it currently fails the pre-registered physical criterion: the object evidence does not yet independently force the `DMVAVA` path, and the exact -3 ogham displacement remains unattested." It also fixes falsifiers in advance, including #1 (surface evidence shows the right `VA` branch is not continuous) and #5 (English before 1840 implausible), **none of which were subsequently discharged**. `board/log/2026-09-07-ennis-stinging-solve.md` was filed the same day anyway, and `PROGRESS.md`'s third pass records the reason: "User explicitly asked to stop accumulating low-value tests, make the inferential leap… and either bridge the historical mechanism or kill it." The solve decision was taken against the project's own pre-registered stop rule, on instruction to leap. To the claimants' real credit, the 2026-09-08 audit walked the physical claim back unprompted, `SOLUTION.md` and `HANDOVER.md` now state the residual gaps plainly, and `STATUS.md` already carries the problem as "Open — STINGING candidate, validation pending". The current documents do not oversell. The board-log solve claim, taken on its own, does.

**Summary.** `DMVAVA` → `STINGING` under −3 in the canonical 20-sign order is arithmetically exact, reproducible from scratch, robust to a search budget several times larger than the one it was tested against, and semantically apt for an object independently documented since 1856 as a sore-eye amulet. That is a real and unusually specific result. But `DMVAVA` is not the transcription — `?DMVA?VA` is; the six-sign payload is produced by deleting the two marks the Crack target specifically demands be explained; no assignment of those marks yields a word; direction is inferred from the plaintext rather than from the geometry; the closure geometry is unresolved by the claimants' own admission; the −3 key is an acknowledged nonce; the plaintext language is the one the period makes least likely; and the headline null statistic is quoted inconsistently across the repo and does not reproduce. Clause (1) of the Crack target is met conditionally on a contested transcription. Clauses (2), (3) and (4) are not met at all. **PARTIAL.**
