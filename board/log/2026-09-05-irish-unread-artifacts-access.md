# Irish unread artefacts — access audit and priority decision

A focused access check was run before promoting the Irish portable-ogham cluster.

## Cleared for top interest

### Moynagh Lough antler tine (I-MEA-003)
- OG(H)AM directly examined/recorded the object in November 2023.
- Public April 2025 treatment includes RTI/3D-derived imagery and the current readings `COLORRS` and `XIBANSNAVQẸ` / `PIBANSNAVQẸ`.
- The interpretation is explicitly still obscure.
- David Stifter's 2026 preprint is publicly downloadable.
- **Access verdict:** workable now. Raw RTI/3D files would improve stroke adjudication but are not a start blocker.

### Ennis amber bead (I-CLA-003 / BM 1888,0719.119)
- British Museum catalogue record is public.
- OG(H)AM photographed the bead during direct examination in December 2023 and published the images.
- Current tentative reading `?DMVA?VA` differs from Macalister's `ATUCMLU`; direction and unusual signs remain live.
- **Access verdict:** workable now. No public RTI/3D model located; higher-resolution multi-angle imagery would strengthen a solve claim.

### Hunt Museum soapstone mould (HCA 686 / I-XXX-001)
- Current specialist treatment is public.
- Hunt Museum publishes a downloadable 3D model under CC0.
- The mixed ogham/probable-rune sequence remains puzzling and none of the proposed orientations yields meaningful Irish.
- **Access verdict:** strongest digital access in the cluster. Provenance is weak, but content access is not blocked.

### Tullycommon Bone (CIIC 52 / NMI E4:248)
- Historical drawings/transcriptions and derived imagery are publicly accessible.
- No modern RTI/3D or fresh high-resolution object photography was located during this pass.
- **Access verdict:** workable but image-limited. First serious session should audit whether the National Museum or OG(H)AM team can supply modern photography before treating any disputed stroke as settled.

## Not promoted: Oweynagat second ogham

The second inscription is too fragmentary for a confident reading (often represented only as `QR G SMU`). This is an intrinsic information-loss problem rather than a mere access problem. Keep it on the evidence-limited watchlist and revisit only if older drawings, squeezes, archival photographs or new imaging recover additional strokes.
