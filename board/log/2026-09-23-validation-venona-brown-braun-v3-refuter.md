## Attack 1 — the primary text contains a constraint the project has never recorded

No. 798 §3–4, verbatim from the transcription:

> "I have seen STENLI and we have come to the following agreement. We shall find a flat at once for
> BRAUN with a MUSIC. STENLI has already learnt how to use the code and will come to work on the days
> [B% indicated by you]. He will receive £20 a month.
>
> STENLI is looking for work on an **agricultural farm near the MUSIC** so as to obtain deferment of
> his call-up to the Army and to regain his health."

The set is installed in BRAUN's new flat, and STANLEY intends to take farm work *near that set* as
his cover and his call-up deferment. **BROWN's July 1940 radio flat was therefore sited within
working distance of agricultural land** — the outer fringe, not inner London. Neither `PROBLEM.md`
nor `analysis/constraint-ledger.md` records this; C1 renders it only as "a London-area accommodation
footprint."

This is the single most useful thing I found, and it cuts against the claim. It points the search at
the semi-rural edge of the London area. It is worth noting what sat on that edge: Henry Hughes & Son's
works were at **Hainault**, and Hainault in 1940 was farmland — 92–96% of the old forest had been
converted to farmland after the 1851 enclosure, and the housing that now covers it was **built by the
LCC between 1947 and 1953** (Wikipedia, *Hainault, London* / *Hainault Forest*). A flat near farmland,
near a clandestine set, within reach of the Hughes production line, in a locality with a CPGB Area
Secretary, is a coherent single object. It requires no corporate shareholding to explain anything.

Meredith is the wrong shape for this. He was 45 in 1940, married since 1935 with two children, and a
salaried chief designer. The residency does not "find a flat at once" for such a man, then move him
back to "his old flat" a fortnight later. The claim grades the residence test "compatible but not
passed"; on the raw text the correct grade is *unevidenced and pointing the other way*.

## Attack 2 — count the competitors

The claim says "most likely" and never states a denominator. Here is one.

**On the Henry Hughes side.** The bridge as argued is *"Smiths held a controlling interest in Henry
Hughes from 1935, and Meredith was senior technical staff at Smiths."* I verified both facts
(Grace's Guide, *Henry Hughes and Son*: "1935: Controlling interest in the company was acquired by
Smiths"; Wikipedia, *Frederick William Meredith*: RAE to April 1938, then Smiths Aircraft Instrument,
Cricklewood, as Chief Designer, Physics and Instruments). They are true. They are also not evidence
about a person. The bridge is satisfied by *every employee of the Smiths group in 1940* — thousands —
and it is **anti-selective**: it is satisfied *better* by every fitter, tester and draughtsman at
Hainault, who are exactly the people No. 976's own words ("received direct from the production line")
point at. A criterion that ranks the named candidate below the population it is supposed to beat is
not a narrowing.

The claim's own comparison table shows the failure clearly. It scores Meredith "Strong institutional"
on Henry Hughes access while scoring the project's earlier Ilford/Plessey candidates "None found" and
"Indirect Plessey only" — although those men lived and worked in the same Essex borough as the Hughes
works, inside the same local CPGB and the same engineering-union milieu. The scoring inverts actual
proximity. That is the signature of an argument run backwards from a name.

I checked the whole of Meredith's technical output that I could reach for any marine or acoustic edge
and found none: US2286561, *Automatic Control Apparatus for Aircraft*, filed 7 July 1939, GB priority
13 July 1938, "**Frederick William Meredith, London, England, assignor to S. Smith & Sons (Motor
Accessories) Limited**" — gyroscopic autopilot work. The Gazette's own index returns four 1941 notices
naming him, all in the same aircraft-autopilot patent action with Philip Andrew Cooke *of the Ministry
of Aircraft Production*, and the address line appears in the **16 May 1941** notice (issue 35165,
p.2859), not the 13 May one the claim file cites: "Frederick William Meredith of Cheltenham Road
Bishops Cleeve near Cheltenham". His documented professional world in this window is aviation
instruments and MAP, at Cricklewood and then Gloucestershire. There is no Meredith–Hughes,
Meredith–Sproule, Meredith–Hainault or Meredith–acoustics edge of any kind. **The channel is entirely
an inference from a 1935 shareholding.** Said plainly: Smiths owning Henry Hughes is not Meredith
knowing anyone there, and nothing in the file closes that gap.

**On the network side.** BROWN's network constraints are: devoted Communist, indiscreet enough that a
CPGB Area Secretary heard about the set, trusted as a conduit passing Soviet material to STANLEY,
movable between flats, and by September able to relay shop-floor technical information. The pool that
satisfies those is not one man. CPGB membership in 1940 ran to tens of thousands on any published
series (the party peaked at 60,000 in 1945). Narrow to the London Communist/International-Brigade
layer that Soviet intelligence actually recruited from and you are still in the hundreds. The claim
never performs this step, so its "most likely" has no denominator at all.

## Attack 3 — a rival candidate who fits as well or better

I was asked to construct one. **Oliver Green** (1904–1971), from the International Brigade Memorial
Trust's volunteer record:

- printer; joined the **Communist Party in 1935**; International Brigade, Spain, Feb 1937 – early 1938;
- **"recruited by Soviet Intelligence in Spain"**;
- by **1939 living at Falkland Terrace, St Pancras, London**;
- arrested January 1942 for forging petrol coupons; the police search turned up **"films of classified
  information and a diary containing cryptic references, apparently to meetings"**;
- **"refused to identify sub-agents, though he claimed to have run some"** — MI5's reconstruction has
  his network reaching into British factories and military establishments.

Set him against the PROBLEM.md falsifiable standard beside Meredith:

| alive/present in Britain, summer 1940 | yes | yes |
| plausible London/Essex geography | workplace Cricklewood; residence unknown, trending to Gloucestershire | London residence attested 1939 |

| Communist/Soviet trust-network evidence consistent with No. 876 | GRU source 1936–39, but a fellow-traveller, not an attested branch member known to local party officers | CPGB member from 1935 *and* Soviet-recruited — satisfies both halves of No. 876 |
| clandestine W/T logistics / use of a flat | no evidence | no evidence |

| conduit relaying material from a production floor | inferred from a shareholding | attested method — ran factory sub-agents and handled films of classified documents |

**I am not claiming Green was BROWN.** His documented activity is 1941–42 and I have nothing placing
him in the 1940 residency traffic. The point is narrower and fatal to the claim as stated: on the

the two clues the claim admits it cannot match — party embedding and indiscretion. Meredith's entire
margin is the Henry Hughes bridge, and the bridge is empty. Remove it and the ordering is not merely
uncertain, it is arguably reversed. One rival found in a single search, with no archive access, is a
sufficient demonstration that the candidate set was never bounded.

## Attack 4 — the Vernon leg, which is weaker than the file says

`analysis/wilfrid-vernon-poultry-dealer.md` rests on "Direct Osterley/Wintringham fit — **very
strong**." I verified that Vernon really was at Osterley: the 9th Earl let Tom Wintringham open the
first Home Guard training school there in May–June 1940, and "Maj. Wilfred Vernon taught the art of
mixing home-made explosives," his explosives store still visible at the rear of the house (Wikipedia,
*Osterley Park*). **That component survives.** So does the primary fingerprint: 882 §6 and 1112/1141
come straight off the cables as the file describes.

But the inference built on it does not. Here is what No. 882 §6 actually says:

> "POULTRY-DEALER has reported that [unrecovered] has proposed to TOM WINTRINGHAM, a former battalion
> commander in the International Brigade, that he should help them to organize a Republican group with
> a view to a rising against FRANCO. TOM was expelled from the CORPORATION after his arrival from the
> south. He is now working in a special school on introducing Spanish methods of anti-tank warfare into
> the Home Guard."

Everything in that paragraph except the unrecovered proposer was **public in 1940**. The Osterley
school was financed by Edward Hulton of *Picture Post*; Wintringham wrote a series for the *Daily
Mirror* from May 1940 and articles for *Picture Post* republished that year as the Penguin Special
*New Ways of War*; the school "won world fame in newsreels and newspaper articles" and was disapproved
of by the War Office and Churchill. Wintringham's expulsion from the CPGB was common knowledge in
party circles. **Reporting that Wintringham was teaching Spanish anti-tank methods at a special school
required no access whatsoever.** The one privileged item is Spanish-exile politics — a proposal about
a rising against Franco — which was current across the whole International Brigade milieu in London.
The Vernon file treats a mass-publicity fact as evidence of proximity. Deflate that and Vernon's fit
to 882 is: he was in the Spanish-veteran/CPGB world. So were hundreds, including every other Osterley
instructor.

The Hounslow argument deflates the same way. POULTRY-DEALER's October reporting is not local: in one
paragraph it carries Eastern Command's move out of Hounslow, 70–80 river craft landed at **Hull**, and
casualty figures from the **Bristol** aircraft factory raid. That is an aggregator with a wide network,
not a man reporting from his own doorstep, and it removes the force of "a person working at Osterley is
geographically much better placed than a generic London Communist."

Two further things weigh against Vernon that the file does not price:

- His documented espionage is **pre-war**. Wikipedia (*Wilfrid Vernon*) has exactly one sentence on it:
  "In February 1952, Vernon was interrogated by MI5 officer Jim Skardon and admitted having been part of
  a **pre-war** Soviet espionage ring." The Weiss network ran 1935–39 with Germaine Schneider as courier
  1936–39.
- He was, since **1937**, a publicly convicted security risk — dismissed from the RAE for taking home
  official documents in breach of the Official Secrets Act (Wikipedia, *Ernest David Weiss*). A legal
  residency choosing him in 1940 as a regular reporting sub-source is possible but is a cost the file
  never books.

And the Popov lunch and the "Meredith recruited him" statement — the file's two strongest specifics and
the "most important person-to-person bridge in the whole identification case" — both come from a single
secondary article I could not open by any route. Wikipedia's account of the same network says only that
"Harry II" introduced Meredith to *both* Vernon and Weiss, i.e. it does not support the recruitment
direction the claim leans on. I record the Bowie article as **UNVERIFIED** and note that the claim's
central bridge currently rests on one inaccessible source.

## Attack 5 — negative evidence from the annotators

The translations are dated: 798 is "3/PPDT/T30 (of 3/2/1967)", reissued 2/10/1973. So the cables could
not have informed Vernon's February 1952 confession. But from 1967 onward, GCHQ and MI5 held these
decrypts *and* a complete Meredith file (opened 30 January 1948 from the Robinson papers; Skardon
interview 6 January 1949) *and* a complete Vernon file, with both men's Smiths and RAE employment on
record — and still annotated **both** BROWN and POULTRY-DEALER "Unidentified covername." The
Smiths/Hughes corporate link the claim calls its "hard institutional pathway" was far more visible to
them than to us. VENONA identification failed often, so this is weak evidence rather than refutation;
but it is evidence against, it is cheap, and the claim does not mention it.

## What survived my attack

A refuter who cannot break something has produced evidence for it, so, explicitly:

1. **The STANLEY/BROWN role separation holds.** 798 §3–6 gives STANLEY as the Canadian, non-party
   radio technician on £20 a month, avoiding CP contact; 876 §1B stops *his* transmissions and moves
   *him*. BROWN and STANLEY are different people with different functions. This is a real, reproducible
   result off the primary text and it is the strongest thing the project has produced.
2. **The "No. 256" reading holds, and is stronger than PROBLEM.md states.** I confirmed the serials
   directly: 798 ends "No. 196 BARCh", 876 ends "No. 221 BARCh", 975 ends "No. 255 BARCh", 976 ends
   "No. 256 BROWN", 987 (6 Sep) ends "No. 259 BARCh", and the intervening numbers alternate between BARCh and
   DICK. It is a single shared residency outgoing sequence, not an agent number — and BROWN's name is
   substituted into the residency's own numbering, which sharpens rather than weakens the annotators'
   point about an agent signing through Embassy facilities.
3. **The Smiths 1935 controlling interest is a true fact,** correctly reported. So is Meredith's 1938
   move to Smiths Cricklewood, so is his 1936–39 GRU role, so is Vernon's Osterley presence with
   Wintringham from July 1940. The claim's factual reporting is honest throughout; what fails is the
   inference laid on top of it.
4. **Meredith is not refuted.** I found no contradiction — no evidence he was out of the country, no
   incompatible posting, nothing. He is an unevidenced candidate, not a killed one, and the 2026-09-06
   log was right to label the work "provisional identification / validation required" and to publish a
   proof-debt list. That self-labelling is good practice and should be said so.

verdict: **PARTIAL**

reasoning:

**Full crack: not met.** The bar requires "documentary evidence tying that person to BROWN's
Soviet/Communist network **and explaining the Henry Hughes information channel**." Neither exists. The
first is conceded by the claim itself ("No located archival source yet states 'BROWN = Meredith'"). The
second is the part I was sent to break, and it broke: the channel is an inference from a 1935
shareholding with no personal, site, project, committee or document edge beneath it; it admits thousands
of Smiths employees; and it is satisfied strictly better by the Hainault production-line staff the cable
itself points at. Under the claim's own preferred role model (H2, BROWN as intermediary) the corporate
relation does no work on the information path at all — the path is insider → BROWN — and it is
unevidenced on the social path.

**Strong partial crack: one of three components met.**

- *"demonstrate which role model best explains all three cables"* — **met.** The H2/H3 intermediary
  reading, with STANLEY holding the radio role, is correct and reproducible from 798/876/816. This is
  genuine progress and should stand.
- *"reduce the search to a small candidate set"* — **not met, and this is the core failure.** No set was
  enumerated and no denominator computed. I produced a rival (Green) in one search who ties or beats
  Meredith on every dimension the claim evidences. A bounded set would have to be constructed on the
  Hainault/Ilford end — the production-line roster and the CPGB Area Secretary — which
  `constraint-ledger.md` Q2/Q3 had already proposed before the Meredith detour and which the detour
  abandoned.
- *"with at least one new archival/documentary link"* — **not met.** Everything load-bearing is
  published secondary material: Wikipedia, Grace's Guide, a patent front page, a Gazette index entry and
  one inaccessible local-history article. KV 2/2199–2202 and KV 2/992–996 remain unopened.

**Why not FAIL.** Three components survive attack (role separation, the serial-number reading, the
corporate facts as facts), Vernon at Osterley is independently confirmed, and Meredith is unevidenced
rather than contradicted. FAIL would misdescribe a body of work that is careful, honestly labelled and
has produced one result I could not break.

**What I would tell the next session to do,** given the new constraint: stop working the Cricklewood
end. No. 798 §4 puts BROWN's radio flat near farmland; Hainault in 1940 *was* farmland with the Henry
Hughes works in it; No. 876 puts a CPGB Area Secretary within conversational range of BROWN; No. 976
puts the source on the production line. Those four facts describe one small geography. Build the 1939
Register occupational roster for the Hughes works and the Hainault/Barkingside/Ilford streets around it,
name the Ilford-area CPGB Area Secretary, and intersect. That population is enumerable. The Smiths group
is not.

dissent: I read validator 1's verdict only after forming the above. We agree on the outcome (PARTIAL)
and, independently, on the central reason — the Henry Hughes bridge is a corporate relation rather than
an access edge, it admits thousands, and it favours Hughes's own staff over Meredith. I record that as
convergent rather than confirmatory: we are similar models and may share the blind spot, so the human
should weigh the *primary-text* findings above more heavily than our agreement.

I differ from validator 1 in one place and add three things. **Differ:** V1 grades the Vernon =
POULTRY-DEALER leg "a defensible reading" and "the best of a handful." I think it is weaker than that.
The plank the file calls "very strong" — Osterley/Wintringham — reports facts that were in *Picture
Post*, the *Daily Mirror* and the newsreels in 1940, so it evidences no access at all; the Hounslow
plank is undercut by the same cable carrying Hull and Bristol; and both of the file's genuinely specific
items (the Popov lunch, "Meredith recruited him") rest on one source I could not open by any route.
Vernon should be graded a *plausible* POULTRY-DEALER, not a strong one. **Add:** (1) the farmland
constraint from 798 §4, which is new to this project and which I think is the most useful thing in this
validation; (2) the named rival, Oliver Green, which converts "the denominator was never computed" from
a methodological complaint into a demonstrated failure of ranking; (3) confirmation of the full
196/221/255/256/259 serial sequence from the raw text, which strengthens the annotators' anomaly note.

I could not verify V1's two textual corrections — "illicit **link**" for "illicit ink", and the
leak-reading of "a MUSIC from BROWN" — because `media.defense.gov` 403s and `web.archive.org` is
egress-blocked for this session. I neither endorse nor contest them; whoever holds the pen should treat
the NSA image as the authority and re-check both before amending `PROBLEM.md`. If either is right, note
that the leak-reading *reinforces* my Attack 1 and Attack 3: it requires BROWN to be in ordinary
conversational contact with a local party officer, which is a branch member on the Essex fringe, not a
chief designer in NW2.
