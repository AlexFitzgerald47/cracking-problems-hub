reasoning:

**What reproduced exactly.** Every transcription and distributional statement in `PARTIAL_BIGRAPH_KERNEL.md` is faithful to the raw source. `BYBL ra` = `E4AC E4AD E41F E44D E42A E483`; `rb Var.3` = `E49A E416 E491 E4AF`; `rc Var.3` = `E4B0 E443 E429 E4AF` — all three byte-for-byte as claimed. U+E4AF has **zero** off-cylinder tokens (confirmed). The `E42A E483` digraph occurs **once**, in `ra` (confirmed). U+E4B0's single off-cylinder token is in `BYBL f` recto, is guess-marked, and its follower is a wildcard (confirmed, and the kernel states this). The merge/split history reproduces: U+E416 and U+E4AF are in one grapheme group in `search`, `syl1`–`syl5` and in different groups in `syl6`–`syl8`. `syllableMap` really does contain `ATON U+E416`.

**The stale-mapping finding is correct and I verified it from both ends independently.** The GEAS 2021 page literally contains "the logo-phonograms `U+E4AF` ATON and `U+E42A U+E483` AMUN"; Schmutz & Mäder 2024 segment the names `anch-es-en-pa-a-mun / me-ʕ-ke(t)-ATON / me-ri-t-ATON`, which puts U+E416 at the `ʕ` slot, not the ATON slot. So `ATON U+E416` in the public tool's `syllableMap` is wrong against the project's own published statement. This matters more than the kernel says: U+E416 has **17 off-cylinder tokens across 6 Dunand objects**, so any user of the public tool is being shown ATON at 17 core positions where the project's own reading is `ʕ`. This is a real, useful, reproducible contribution and it stands.

**Criterion 1 — NOT met.** There is no power analysis anywhere in the folder. `PARTIAL_BIGRAPH_KERNEL.md` §6 gives a qualitative remark ("not enough degrees of freedom to decode a 60–120-type system"); `ME_ANCHOR_TRANSFER.md` §5 explicitly declines one ("no post-hoc p-value is presented"). Declining a power analysis is not performing one. There are also no positional statistics in any artefact, and the "sign inventory with documented variant-merging decisions" is one adjudicated merge, not an inventory. I supplied the missing analysis (`v1_power_and_ngrams.py`): de-duplicated corpus N = 1284 sign tokens, V = 213 attested types (238 declared in `rawTokens`), N/V = 6.0, 78 hapax (37% of V), **111 of 213 types (52%) occur ≤ 2 times**, 245 unreadable wildcard slots (16% of all slots), 137 guess-marked tokens (11% of signs), longest uninterrupted certain-sign run 19, median 5. The honest ceiling statement the criterion asks for is therefore: for a **majority** of the signary there is no distributional evidence at all at any sample size, because the tokens do not exist. One further number the folder should carry: U+E402 → U+E48F, the bigram the ME result turns on, has 2 tokens in the whole corpus, and both are inside the claimed cluster — so there is no independent evidence that the pair is a unit.
Also not reproducible: the "18 `BYBL` / 14 `BYBL?`" figure repeated from OCBI's own description. By `source` I get 31/12 entries; by `group` 29/14; distinct objects 24/11; collapsing cylinder `ra–rd` to one object, 21. No counting rule I can construct yields 18.

**Criterion 2 — met, but by prior art, and the kernel says so.** I read the Schmutz & Mäder 2024 PDF directly. Its abstract states that "a linguistic application of the phonetic values to previously unexamined inscriptions (the bigraphic 'daughter's names' on the cylinder seal of Byblos and the Berlin Familienstele respectively) can be used to prove that the translations advocated by Woudhuizen and Best are attestedly incorrect"; a footnote extends this to Mendenhall (1985) and dismisses Colless's rescue hypotheses. The kernel's §5 characterises this accurately and explicitly disclaims novelty. Credit the folder for correct prior-art handling. But nothing on disk independently reproduces either exclusion — no Woudhuizen/Best or Mendenhall value table is applied to the cylinder anywhere in the folder — so the Hub has imported the criterion, not satisfied it.

**Criterion 3 — substantially met at the level of argument, not executed.** I verified all four DEAPS dates exactly as tabulated (KAI 3 = −1000 to −975; KAI 4 = −950 to −920; KAI 5 = −940 to −930; KAI 6 = −920 to −900), and DEAPS's own notes confirm the Phoenician-over-pseudohieroglyphic relation for KAI 3 and KAI 4. The three-parameter reframing (genesis / terminal phase / replacement) is a genuine and well-argued advance, and §4 correctly refuses to declare Dunand's dating victorious. Two qualifications the document should carry. (a) **The Yehimilk filiation in KAI 6 is a restoration.** DEAPS renders it "son of [Yehimilk king of Byblos]" — in square brackets. `PALIMPSEST_CHRONOLOGY.md` §2 point 4 uses that filiation as one of five links in a cumulative chain without noting it is restored text; the chain is correspondingly weaker. **Criterion 4 — NOT met, and the one attempt inverts.** This is the serious finding.

The folder's only criterion-4 artefact is `ME_ANCHOR_TRANSFER.md`: "every one of the three clear non-cylinder occurrences of the exact `U+E49A` form is followed by the same sign `U+E402`", yielding a claimed "three-sign lexical/morphemic family `U+E49A U+E402 {U+E48F, U+E412}`" with "low variation inside, high variation outside". I reproduce the three loci exactly. But I then went to the GEAS font that ships the signs and read what the project calls U+E402:

> **U+E402 = "b I 7 kurzer Worttrenner oben"** — *short word divider, upper.*
> (U+E404 = "kurzer Worttrenner Mitte" is the only other such sign in the font.)

U+E402 is a **word divider** by the naming of the same project that supplies the corpus and the ATON/AMUN labels the kernel relies on. `Byblos.elm` sets `seperatorChars = ""`, so the app never flags it — which is presumably how the Hub pass missed it. My name-free distributional check agrees with the name: U+E402 has 13 tokens and **zero** at a line edge (P(0 edges | ordinary sign) = 0.07), which is what a divider does and a syllabogram does not.

Re-reading the three loci with U+E402 as a divider:

```
BYBL i IX : ... E442 | E49A | E48F E40D? ...   -> E49A is a ONE-SIGN word, flanked by dividers
BYBL k IV : E45A E497 E439 E49A | E412 E45A    -> E49A word-final; E412 begins the NEXT word
BYBL m II : [frac][x] E410 E49A | E48F E46B    -> E49A word-final
```

So: the claimed three-sign unit does not exist — it straddles a word boundary. The "invariant first internal transition `U+E49A → U+E402`" **is** the boundary, and carries no lexical information. The stated asymmetry reverses: the only invariant element is the boundary marker, while every genuinely lexical neighbour varies. The conditional `ME–?–T(?)` skeleton for `BYBL k` is void, because U+E412 is not the third sign of a word beginning with ME. And the §1 statement that "the exact bigrams `U+E402 U+E48F` found here are not just the ordinary fate of every `U+E402`" is true but empty: those two tokens are the only two in the corpus.

Two further independent problems with the same artefact. First, **table 3 misreports the data in its own favour's direction.** It lists BYBL i's left neighbour as U+E442. The line is `E45A? E413 E442 E402 E49A E402 E48F E40D? ...` — the left neighbour is U+E402. U+E402 sits on **both** sides of U+E49A in BYBL i, which is the single most diagnostic fact in the whole locus and is the one the table drops. Second, **BYBL i and BYBL m are not independent witnesses.** They share *two* distinct trigraphs (`E498 E46B E45A` and `E49A E402 E48F`) out of only **11** cross-object trigraphs in the entire de-duplicated corpus; BYBL m has 12 certain signs in total. Two independent objects do not share two trigraphs. The "crosses medium (bronze vs stone), object, and publication cohort" independence argument does not survive that.

What does survive, and I want it on the record because it is still interesting: all three clear off-cylinder U+E49A tokens sit immediately before a word divider, and family-wise U+E49A is the **only** sign with ≥3 tokens in divider-using objects that is always divider-followed (restricted-null p ≈ 6e-4). That is a real positional fact about a possibly word-final-only sign. It is not evidence for the value *me*, and it is not the lexical unit claimed. (My budget-matched null — 0 of 13 three-token signs have a constant follower — converges with the 2026-09-25 session's independently-computed 1-of-21 figure; we differ only in de-duplication rule.)

Separately, criterion 4 is **unreachable for three of the four frozen anchors by construction**: U+E4AF has 0 off-cylinder tokens, the `U+E42A U+E483` digraph has 0, and U+E4B0 has 1 which is guess-marked with an unreadable follower. Only U+E49A (3 tokens) and U+E44D (11 tokens) can be probed at all, and U+E44D is never probed anywhere in the folder. Meanwhile the published source the kernel cites supplies two far better probes it never freezes: **U+E429 = `t`** (26 off-cylinder tokens on 8 objects) and **U+E416 = `ʕ`** (17 on 6). Those are the obvious next test and they are sitting unused.

**The inventory adjudication — the kernel's self-identified new contribution — is conditional in a way the document never states.** Two problems.

(a) *Variant dependence.* `rb` has three OCBI transcription variants and `rc` has three, of the same physical columns. U+E416 appears in **exactly one of the six**: `rb Var.3`. `rb Var.1`/`Var.2` read `E4FF E49A E4AE E4AF` — no U+E416, and the ME sign is not even name-initial. `rc Var.1` ends U+E4AE, `rc Var.2` ends U+E4FD. So the two properties §2 offers as *external constraints* supporting the identification — "the two `-Aton` daughters have … a `me` onset … and the exact same terminal sign" — hold in **1 of the 9 variant combinations**, and that selection budget is never charged. More damaging for the Hub's own result: run the kernel's own argument on `rb Var.1`/`Var.2` and the same-grapheme collision inside the name is `U+E4AE ~ U+E4AF`, which is merged in **every** OCBI syllabary including Syl6–Syl8. So the claimed "later OCBI inventories independently move in exactly the direction demanded by the external control" is an artefact of the variant chosen; under the other reading of the same column the external control would demand a split OCBI never makes. The variant selection is Mäder's, not the Hub's invention — Schmutz & Mäder do segment `me-ʕ-ke(t)-ATON`, four units — so this is an unstated dependency, not a fabrication. But the Hub's one new result rests on it and the document should say so.

(b) *The split has a cheaper explanation than the bigraph.* From the GEAS glyph names: U+E416 = "offener Ring", U+E495 = "offener Ring eingedreht" — an open-ring family. U+E4AE = "Sonnenaufgang", U+E4AF = "Sonnenaufgang / Rechts-Schlaufe" — a sunrise family. Syl2–Syl5 put a ring and a sunrise in one group; Syl6–Syl8 separate the rings from the sunrises. That is OCBI re-grouping along its own shape taxonomy, already encoded in the sign names. The Amarna alignment *coincides* with it. The kernel reads the coincidence as independent corroboration; I cannot distinguish that from OCBI simply fixing a graphic mis-grouping, and the kernel offers nothing that would.

(c) *The adjudication rule is applied in two directions.* The kernel promotes the **later** OCBI decision (Syl6+ split) where it supports the kernel. `ME_ANCHOR_TRANSFER.md` §4 relies on the **earlier** decision (`U+E491 ~ U+E412` merged) where that supports the transfer. `syl9` — the last inventory in the file, which the folder never mentions — **splits** `U+E491` from `U+E412`, killing the `ME–?–T(?)` bridge, and also **splits** `U+E49A` from `U+E4B0`, i.e. splits the ME allograph pair the kernel's "both daughters begin with *me*" argument needs. `syl9` contains neither U+E4AF nor U+E4AE nor U+E4FD at all, so Syl6–Syl8 is not the end state of the series. Any rule of the form "promote the later OCBI split" cuts against the kernel at least as hard as for it.

It does not: 8 of its 21 distinct signs (38%) are cylinder-only, against a corpus-wide single-fragment-type rate of 43%. The cylinder is slightly *below* base rate. That is a point **in favour** of the kernel and I record it as such.
