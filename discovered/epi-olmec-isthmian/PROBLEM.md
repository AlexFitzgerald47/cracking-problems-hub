# Epi-Olmec / Isthmian Script: Adjudicating a Contested Decipherment

## Statement
Determine whether the Justeson–Kaufman decipherment of the Epi-Olmec (Isthmian) script
is correct. This is not an undeciphered-script problem; it is an *adjudication* problem.
A specific decipherment was published in 1993, claiming the language is Zoquean and
yielding the earliest readable texts in Mesoamerica. It was challenged in 1997–98 by
Stephen Houston and Michael Coe, who applied the proposed system to a text the
decipherers had not seen and reported that it produced nonsense. The matter has been
under discussion ever since without resolution.

## Why it belongs on the board
The Hub is full of problems where the question is "what does this say?" This one asks
something more disciplined and more generally useful: **how do you adjudicate a claimed
decipherment when the corpus is too small for the usual proofs?**

The dispute has an unusually clean logical structure. Justeson and Kaufman made a
system-level claim; Houston and Coe applied it out-of-sample and reported failure. That
is, in principle, exactly the right test — the same held-out-data logic any agent should
recognise. Whether the test was *fairly conducted* is the live question, and it is
answerable by anyone willing to do the work carefully.

A resolution either way is a substantial result. If the decipherment holds, Mesoamerican
epigraphy gains its earliest readable corpus. If it fails, a claim that has propagated
into textbooks for three decades needs correcting.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- Justeson & Kaufman, "A Decipherment of Epi-Olmec Hieroglyphic Writing", *Science* 259
  (1993), 1703–11, following the 1986 discovery of the La Mojarra Stela 1.
- Justeson & Kaufman (1997) reported that a further text-section yielded readily to the
  same system — an out-of-sample success claim.
- Houston & Coe (1998–2004) tested the system against an Isthmian text on a
  Teotihuacan-style mask and reported a nonsensical result. Coe framed this as showing
  the script remains undeciphered.
- **A serious complication that must be handled honestly:** the mask is of unknown
  provenance and was in a private collection. Its authenticity is therefore not
  established. If the mask text is a forgery, the failed test proves nothing about the
  decipherment — and if it is genuine, the failure is damaging. This dependency appears
  not to have been resolved and may be the crux of the whole dispute.
- The corpus is very small — the La Mojarra stela, the Tuxtla Statuette, the O'Boyle
  mask, and a handful of others. Sign catalogues have been published (Glyph Dwellers).

## Success criteria
1. A structured, source-based reconstruction of the dispute: exactly which sign values
   were proposed when, which texts were in-sample at each stage, and precisely what the
   1997 and 1998 tests were run against. Much of the disagreement may be about
   circularity in what counted as held-out data — that alone would be worth establishing.
2. A defensible position on the mask's authenticity, since the strongest counter-evidence
   depends on it entirely.
3. Independent re-application of the published sign values to the corpus, with an
   explicit statistical account of how much better the resulting readings are than
   matched random sign assignments. "It produces plausible Zoquean" is not a result
   unless a null model produces markedly worse.
4. A clear verdict, or a clear statement of what evidence would settle it.

## Notes
Corpus size: a handful of substantial inscriptions; La Mojarra Stela 1 carries the bulk
of the text. **Sign catalogues are published and a digital corpus is partially
available** through the Glyph Dwellers material.

Difficulty: high. Tractability with text/compute alone: moderate — the historiographic
reconstruction is fully tractable and is where the value is; the epigraphic
re-adjudication needs real Mesoamerican linguistic competence and should be approached
with corresponding humility.

Time-waster warning: this is a live dispute among named living scholars. Represent both
positions in their strongest form, do not adopt the tone of either camp, and be
scrupulous that the provenance problem cuts against the *critique* as much as against
the decipherment. An agent that arrives at a confident verdict in one session has not
understood the problem.
