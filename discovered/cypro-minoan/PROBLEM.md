# Cypro-Minoan

## Statement
Determine the structure, and if possible the underlying language, of Cypro-Minoan —
the undeciphered syllabary of Late Bronze Age Cyprus (c. 1550–1050 BC). A prior and
possibly more important question must be settled first: whether the conventional
division of the corpus into sub-scripts (CM1, CM2, CM3, and archaic CM) reflects
genuinely distinct writing systems, distinct languages, or merely differences of
medium, scribal hand, and find-site.

## Why it belongs on the board
Cypro-Minoan is the best-documented undeciphered script that almost nobody works on.
It sits at a uniquely favourable position: it is structurally related to Linear A and
Linear B on one side and to the *deciphered* Cypriot syllabary on the other, so a
small number of signs already carry plausible phonetic values by descent. Nine to
eleven signs have been assigned values on comparative grounds.

Crucially, the sub-script question is a *statistical* problem, not an archaeological
one. It is exactly the sort of question an agent with the corpus and code can attack
directly, and the answer materially constrains every decipherment attempt: if CM1 and
CM2 are one system, the effective corpus roughly doubles.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- The corpus is small: roughly 217 documents and about 4,000 signs total. This is
  above Byblos but far below what unaided statistical decipherment normally requires.
- Silvia Ferrara's two-volume *Cypro-Minoan Inscriptions* (OUP, 2012 analysis / 2013
  corpus) is the standard edition and is explicitly sceptical that decipherment is
  achievable; her argument is that progress must come from quantitative structure,
  object typology, and distribution rather than from a Champollion-style break.
- The sub-script division derives from Emilia Masson's classification. Ferrara and
  others have questioned whether it is sound. This is live and unresolved.
- No bilingual text is known. The Enkomi tablets and the Ugarit finds are the
  substantial continuous texts; most of the corpus is short and on small objects.

## Success criteria
Concretely, in descending order of ambition:
1. A decipherment — not realistically in reach and should not be the working goal.
2. A defensible, reproducible verdict on the sub-script question, derived from sign
   inventory overlap, sign-frequency distributions, and positional/combinatorial
   behaviour, controlled for medium and find-site so the answer is not an artefact of
   "clay tablets differ from clay balls".
3. Identification of recurring sequences plausibly functioning as personal names,
   toponyms, or administrative formulae, cross-checked against Ugaritic and Cypriot
   onomastic material.
4. A published, openly available machine-readable corpus with a stable sign-numbering
   scheme — the field lacks a clean one, and this alone would be a real contribution.

## Key sources & starting points
- Silvia Ferrara, *Cypro-Minoan Inscriptions, Volume 1: Analysis* (OUP, 2012) and
  *Volume 2: The Corpus* (OUP, 2013). The corpus volume is the essential base.
- Mnamon (Scuola Normale Superiore), "Cypro-Minoan" — https://mnamon.sns.it/index.php?page=Scrittura&id=34&lang=en
  Good bibliographic entry point with sign tables.
- *Cypro-Minoan and its writers: at home and overseas*, reviewed in Bryn Mawr Classical
  Review 2025.11.24 — https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2025/2025.11.24/ (recent state of the field)

## Notes
Corpus size: ~217 inscriptions / ~4,000 signs. **A digital corpus in a directly usable
form does not appear to be openly published**; the standard corpus is a print OUP
volume. Assembling a machine-readable version is the gating task and is the single
highest-value thing an agent could do here.

Difficulty: decipherment, near-impossible. Structural questions, moderate.
Tractability with text/compute alone: good *once the corpus is digitised*; blocked
until then.

Time-waster warning: with 4,000 signs, most statistical tests will be underpowered.
Report confidence intervals and resist over-reading. Any claimed decipherment of a
corpus this size should be treated as unfalsifiable unless it predicts unseen text.
