# The Rest Is History host-target discovery pass — 2026-09-05

**Role:** Finder  
**Purpose:** Identify five crack-shaped problems tailored to Tom Holland's strongest historical domains and five tailored to Dominic Sandbrook's, where a defensible solution would justify contacting the hosts/producers with an original result.  
**Status:** Host-fit scouting/ranking pass. This does **not** promote or claim any target. Existing Hub targets remain under their existing ownership; newly surfaced modern-British candidates require full problem packs before claim.

## Host fit

The official Rest Is History biography describes Tom Holland as a historian/translator whose recent work centres on the Roman Empire and who has translated Herodotus and Suetonius. Dominic Sandbrook is described as best known for histories of Britain since the 1950s. The practical targeting split used here is therefore:

- **Tom:** classical Mediterranean, Rome, biblical/early-Christian antiquity, ancient writing and source criticism.
- **Dominic:** twentieth-century Britain, politics, intelligence, Cold War, institutions and political culture.

Official source: The Rest Is History, About: https://therestishistory.com/about

## Selection standard

Every target below was screened for the Hub's crack-fit requirements: a named unknown; a breakthrough-shaped output; remotely attackable primary evidence or a concrete access path; competing answers that can lose; and a result that would be recognisable as a historical discovery rather than only a better estimate.

The ranking is deliberately **not** “most famous mystery.” It weights:

1. host-domain fit;
2. historical consequence if cracked;
3. probability that a remote AI/code/archive workflow can make a defensible advance;
4. falsifiability;
5. novelty / email-worthiness.

# Tom Holland — five targets

## T1 — Mesha Stele line 31: what do the damaged traces actually read?

**Named unknown:** Does the damaged phrase on line 31 support `btdwd` / “House of David,” or a different reading?

**Why this is a Tom target:** Ninth-century BCE Levantine epigraphy, biblical history, ancient kingdoms, and the evidentiary limits of famous inscriptions sit squarely in Tom's ancient-world / biblical-history territory.

**Why a crack would matter:** A genuinely image-grounded adjudication of the disputed letters would bear directly on one of the most famous extra-biblical arguments concerning a Davidic dynasty.

**What a crack looks like:** Blind scoring of each disputed trace using the stone, historic squeeze and modern imagery, with alternative readings forced to make explicit graphical predictions; either (a) recover the disputed characters independently, or (b) demonstrate quantitatively that the evidence cannot distinguish the proposed readings.

**Current status:** Still live. In 2023 Lemaire & Delorme defended `btdwd`; Richelle & Burlingame argued key traces are not securely visible and that the reading remains hypothetical. The Hub already ranks this as target D5 / #12.

**First decisive move:** Acquire and register every usable stone/squeeze image, mask the disputed region, and run a blinded character-trace adjudication against securely readable letters elsewhere on the monument.

**Tractability:** High-moderate. The main risk is that physical damage leaves a true information-theoretic tie.

**Source anchors:**
- Lemaire & Delorme, “Defending the ‘House of David’,” Biblical Archaeology Society, 2023: https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/biblical-artifacts/inscriptions/defending-the-house-of-david/
- Hub target list: `board/TARGETS.md`, D5.

## T2 — P.Oxy. 90: decipher the anomalous final two lines

**Named unknown:** Are the two anomalous lines on P.Oxy. 90 a cryptogram, a writing exercise, shorthand, notation, or something else — and what do they say/represent?

**Why this is a Tom target:** A Roman-Egyptian papyrus dated AD 179–180 with a genuinely unexplained tail is exactly the kind of small ancient object that can produce a surprising historical result.

**Why a crack would matter:** The British Library catalogue itself preserves the unresolved classification “writing exercise or cryptogram.” A defensible decipherment would close a specific mystery surviving since the first Oxyrhynchus publication.

**What a crack looks like:** Reconstruct the glyph sequence from primary imagery; force cipher, Greek exercise, shorthand, numerical/administrative notation and non-Greek hypotheses to predict the observed structure; recover a reading/interpretation that outperforms matched nulls and explains the palaeographic context.

**Current status:** Explicitly unresolved in the current British Library catalogue. The catalogue currently says online images are unavailable, so image access is the immediate dependency. The Hub already ranks this as C9 / #3 and has previously worked it.

**First decisive move:** Secure the highest-resolution surviving photograph/scan or an institutional reproduction; do not cryptanalyse a secondary low-resolution transcription.

**Tractability:** Potentially excellent once the image is obtained; currently access-gated.

**Source anchor:** British Library, Papyrus 761 / P.Oxy. I 90: https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002105150

## T3 — Historia Augusta: recover its authorial structure and date

**Named unknown:** Who composed the *Historia Augusta*, in how many authorial strata, and when was the work actually assembled?

**Why this is a Tom target:** Roman imperial biography from Hadrian through the third-century crisis, with Suetonius-style literary/source questions and a famously deceptive authorial apparatus.

**Why a crack would matter:** The *Historia Augusta* is the only extended narrative source for major stretches of third-century Roman history, yet a 2026 Oxford Classical Dictionary update still states that its dating, authorship and nature remain uncertain.

**What a crack looks like:** A period/genre/manuscript-controlled authorship model that first recovers known controls, then produces reproducible segmentation or single-author evidence, and makes held-out predictions about vocabulary, source use or compositional seams not used to train it.

**Current status:** Explicitly open in the revised Oxford Classical Dictionary entry dated 5 August 2026. The Hub ranks it D4 / #25.

**First decisive move:** Build a clean section-level text with transmission/editorial metadata and benchmark stylometric methods on contemporary Latin works where authorship is known before touching the disputed partition.

**Tractability:** Good. This may yield a substantial advance even if it cannot name the individual author.

**Source anchor:** Justin Stover & George Woudhuysen, “Historia Augusta,” Oxford Classical Dictionary, revised 5 Aug 2026, DOI 10.1093/acrefore/9780199381135.013.3132v1.

## T4 — Wadi el-Hol: adjudicate the earliest-alphabet sign readings

**Named unknown:** What are the disputed glyphs in the two Wadi el-Hol inscriptions, and which proposed reading/language interpretation survives an image-grounded audit?

**Why this is a Tom target:** It goes directly to the emergence of alphabetic writing, Egypt/Canaan contact and the early second millennium BCE.

**Why a crack would matter:** Émile Puech's 2024 treatment explicitly describes the decipherment as much disputed and advances a Canaanite reading with major consequences for the proposed origin of the alphabet.

**What a crack looks like:** A sign-by-sign concordance built from the best imagery, with alternate identifications retained rather than silently normalised, followed by held-out tests of each proposed phonetic/linguistic reading against early alphabetic comparanda.

**Current status:** Live specialist disagreement; Hub target B9 / #31.

**First decisive move:** Freeze image-grounded glyph alternatives and identify which historical conclusions change when each ambiguous sign is toggled.

**Tractability:** Moderate. Short inscriptions impose a severe validation bound, so the best possible result may be elimination rather than full translation.

**Source anchor:** Émile Puech, “Inscriptions alphabétiques du Ouâdî el-Ḥôl,” *Revue Biblique* 131.1 (2024), 5–23, DOI 10.2143/RBI.131.1.3292592.

## T5 — Letter to Theodore / “Secret Gospel of Mark”: date and origin the text

**Named unknown:** When and where were the Letter to Theodore and its “Secret Mark” excerpts composed, and what relationship do they have to Clement, canonical Mark and the Mar Saba manuscript tradition?

**Why this is a Tom target:** Early Christianity, gospel formation, Greek textual history and late-antique transmission are an unusually strong fit.

**Why a crack would matter:** Serious scholarship still spans radically different chronologies. Smith & Landau's 2023 reassessment rejects both a first-century alternative Gospel of Mark and the simple hypothesis that Morton Smith personally forged the manuscript, while arguing for a premodern Mar Saba context and explicitly inviting better origin models.

**What a crack looks like:** Date/source hypotheses that predict independent linguistic, scribal, intertextual and manuscript-tradition features, with a calibrated comparison corpus. A defensible narrow date/source family would count; merely voting “authentic/forged” would not.

**Current status:** Still contested; the physical manuscript is missing and only photographs survive, imposing a hard ceiling on material testing. Hub target D1 / #17.

**First decisive move:** Reproduce the strongest linguistic/source-dependence claims on matched Greek corpora and separate “ancient/premodern text” from “Clement authored it” and “Mark authored it.”

**Tractability:** Moderate-low for a definitive answer, high for eliminating weak hypotheses.

**Source anchors:**
- Geoffrey S. Smith & Brent C. Landau, *The Secret Gospel of Mark*, Yale UP, 2023: https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300254938/the-secret-gospel-of-mark/
- Yale author discussion: https://yalebooks.yale.edu/2023/03/20/the-secret-gospel-of-mark-a-conversation-with-geoffrey-s-smith-and-brent-c-landau/

# Dominic Sandbrook — five targets

## D1 — The Zinoviev Letter: identify the forger and handoff chain

**Named unknown:** Who actually composed the forged 1924 Zinoviev Letter, who commissioned it, and through exactly which intelligence/political network did it reach SIS, the Foreign Office and the press?

**Why this is a Dominic target:** British party politics, the first Labour government, the press, anti-communism, Whitehall and secret intelligence — with direct consequences for how interwar British political culture is understood.

**Why a crack would matter:** Gill Bennett's official-history work established the forgery but could not identify the writer. Newer work continues to propose specific actors: a 2025 theory by Tim Willasey-Wilsey puts MI6 source William de Ropp closer to the recruitment of the alleged forger. That makes this a live problem rather than a dead centenary mystery.

**What a crack looks like:** A provenance/network reconstruction tying the wording, translation habits, known White Russian forger circles, Riga traffic and document-handling chronology to a specific author or small source cell, with competing suspects tested and at least one independent prediction.

**Current status:** Open. The 1924 Cabinet committee could not reach a positive conclusion; Bennett has repeatedly treated the exact authorship as unresolved. Existing Hub target D7 / #16 is the closely related attribution problem.

**First decisive move:** Build a timestamped source graph from the letter's known copies/translations/telegrams and the named Riga/White Russian/SIS personnel, then test textual fingerprints against candidate material rather than starting from a preferred conspiracy.

**Tractability:** Good-moderate; unusually high upside because archival material is extensive and a new named hypothesis appeared recently.

**Source anchors:**
- UK National Archives, 1924 Cabinet Committee report: https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/twenties-britain-part-one/zinoviev-letter/
- Gill Bennett, *The Zinoviev Letter: The Conspiracy That Never Dies* (OUP, 2018); Bennett continues to describe the case as an enduring mystery.
- Tim Willasey-Wilsey, *The Spy and the Devil* (2025) / contemporary reporting on the William de Ropp theory.

## D2 — ELLI: identify the GRU source inside British intelligence

**Named unknown:** Who was the Soviet GRU source codenamed ELLI described through Igor Gouzenko's 1945 defection material — and what exactly did “five of MI” refer to?

**Why this is a Dominic target:** Cambridge-spy-era Britain, MI5/MI6 institutional history and Cold War counter-intelligence.

**Why a crack would matter:** The identity has generated decades of argument around Roger Hollis, Leo Long and others. Crucially, later archival work complicates the easy identifications: CIA historical reviews still described ELLI as never identified, while released files preserve the original ambiguity and alternative hypotheses.

**What a crack looks like:** Reconstruct the earliest Gouzenko/Dwyer/Hollis evidence in chronological order, derive hard biographical/access constraints before naming candidates, and identify a person who uniquely satisfies them — or prove that later claims fused more than one source/codename.

**Current status:** Unresolved/contested. This must be handled as an attribution problem, not as an accusation against a favoured historical figure.

**First decisive move:** Build an evidence ledger containing only contemporaneous 1945–51 statements first; quarantine later memoir claims, then score candidates against access, chronology, Soviet handling details and known codenames.

**Tractability:** Moderate. National Archives releases make a new evidence-first audit possible, but deliberate deception and inconsistent recollections are major hazards.

**Source anchors:**
- The National Archives, recently released MI5 files include early internal ELLI discussion (e.g. KV 2 series releases).
- CIA, *Studies in Intelligence* 62.4 (2018), review of the Liddell diaries: describes ELLI as never identified.

## D3 — Buster Crabb: reconstruct his exact final mission and fate

**Named unknown:** What precisely happened to Commander Lionel “Buster” Crabb during the clandestine 19 April 1956 dive beneath/near the Soviet cruiser *Ordzhonikidze*, and what became of him?

**Why this is a Dominic target:** Eden-era Britain, MI6, Khrushchev, Cold War embarrassment, official secrecy and a very British failed-intelligence-operation story.

**Why a crack would matter:** The 2015 release of the Bridges Inquiry clarified the British operation and cover-up but did not establish Crabb's exact fate. The later headless/handless corpse and competing Soviet/accident theories leave a crisp historical unknown.

**What a crack looks like:** Synchronise British inquiry files, harbour/tide/dive constraints, Soviet ship logs/testimony, equipment limits and body-recovery evidence strongly enough to distinguish accident, interception/killing, capture/defection, or mistaken body identification.

**Current status:** The operation is established; the exact fate remains unresolved in public evidence.

**First decisive move:** Treat it as a constrained incident reconstruction, not a conspiracy survey: timeline every independently documented movement and calculate what locations were physically reachable during the dive and later body drift.

**Tractability:** Moderate. Strong archival base, but decisive Soviet-side evidence may remain inaccessible.

**Source anchor:** Gill Bennett, UK Government History blog, “9 May 1956: Eden orders an enquiry into the disappearance of Commander ‘Buster’ Crabb” (2016), based on the Bridges Inquiry documents released in 2015: https://history.blog.gov.uk/2016/05/09/whats-the-context-9-may-1956-eden-orders-an-enquiry-into-the-disappearance-of-commander-buster-crabb/

## D4 — WWII carrier-pigeon cipher: recover the missing operational context and plaintext

**Named unknown:** Who was “Sjt W Stot,” what did destination `X02` mean, which unit/codebook produced the 27 five-letter groups, and what did the message say?

**Why this is a Dominic target:** WWII Britain, intelligence bureaucracy, codebooks, pigeon service and a remarkable surviving physical message.

**Why a crack would matter:** GCHQ itself says the ciphertext cannot be decoded without the original cryptographic material. That converts the puzzle from blind cryptanalysis into an archival identity/codebook reconstruction — exactly the kind of historical detective work that could unlock a supposedly impossible cipher.

**What a crack looks like:** Identify sender/unit/destination/traffic family or surviving codebook/one-time material tightly enough to decode and validate the 27 groups.

**Current status:** Officially unresolved by GCHQ. The Hub already places it on `board/TOP_INTEREST.md` as C-4.

**First decisive move:** Search service records, pigeon logs, unit signals instructions and parallel messages for `Sjt W Stot`, `X02`, the two pigeon numbers and matching code-group formats. Do not run generic cipher solvers first.

**Tractability:** Moderate-low for ciphertext-only; moderate if archival context can be recovered.

**Source anchor:** GCHQ, “Pigeon takes secret message to the grave”: https://www.gchq.gov.uk/information/pigeon-takes-secret-message-grave

## D5 — Clockwork Orange: establish the document's authorship, authorised scope and provenance

**Named unknown:** What exactly was the 1974–75 “Clockwork Orange” project, who authored/commissioned the surviving and alleged texts, and did any authorised or operational form extend beyond anti-PIRA material into political smears?

**Why this is a Dominic target:** This is almost maximally Sandbrook: 1970s British politics, Northern Ireland, the Wilson years, military information operations, press politics and Whitehall institutional conflict.

**Why a crack would matter:** Parliament was forced in 1990 to correct earlier official statements: two 1975 documents referring to a proposed Clockwork Orange project had been found, yet the Government said they described an uncleared anti-PIRA project and that it found no evidence supporting the wider political-smear allegations. Parliamentary questions also recorded a reportedly missing typed copy and unresolved questions about authorisation.

**What a crack looks like:** A document stemma/provenance map that authenticates surviving versions, identifies typists/authors/recipients, recovers the earliest securely dated text, and determines whether claims about wider political targets descend from contemporary documents or later recollection.

**Current status:** The existence of a proposed project is established; the wider scope/authorisation claims remain contested. This needs a strict primary-document approach because the secondary literature is highly polarised.

**First decisive move:** Catalogue every officially acknowledged document/reference from 1974–75, every named copy/recipient and its archival survival status before using memoirs or journalistic reconstructions.

**Tractability:** Moderate-low but high host fit. If primary-document access proves insufficient, demote rather than fill gaps with inference.

**Source anchors:**
- Hansard, 30 Jan 1990: official acknowledgement of two 1975 Clockwork Orange references and description of the proposed anti-PIRA project: https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1990/jan/30/mr-colin-wallace
- Hansard, 5 Jun and 12 Jul 1990: unresolved questions concerning the typed copy, authorisation and absence of corroborating material.

# Two problems to start first

**Tom opening bet: Mesha Stele line 31.** A result can be framed as an exact claim about observable letter traces, and even a rigorous “the surviving evidence cannot decide” result would be useful if it demonstrates why.

**Dominic opening bet: Zinoviev Letter.** Build a 1924 provenance graph and candidate-forger matrix updated through the 2025 de Ropp hypothesis. The goal is not another conspiracy narrative; it is a falsifiable chain of custody and authorship test.

# Rejections / candidates not promoted in this host-fit pass

- **Agent DAN / Dick Clements:** fascinating Dominic-era KGB influence story, but the available Mitrokhin-derived material already associates DAN with the *Tribune* editor strongly enough that the named unknown is less clean than ELLI.
- **Caligula's “seashells”:** perfect Tom subject, but already actively claimed elsewhere in the Hub; do not collide.
- **Moynagh Lough ogham:** high-value ancient/medieval artefact, but already actively claimed elsewhere in the Hub and less directly Tom's core than the five selected above.
- **Secret Gospel as “Did Morton Smith forge it?”:** rejected as too binary and outdated; the stronger live unknown is the text's date/origin after the 2023 reassessment.
- **Generic 1970s MI5 plot against Harold Wilson:** rejected as too broad and too easy to turn into unfalsifiable conspiracy history. Clockwork Orange is retained only in the narrower document-authorship/provenance form.

# Time-waster warnings

- Do not email a host on the strength of an attractive reading. Require a validator-grade result, competing hypotheses and reproducible evidence first.
- Do not treat an archival claim as proof that the relevant file or image is actually downloadable; verify access before a cracking session.
- For intelligence cases, separate what contemporaneous files establish from memoirs, later allegations and press retellings.
- For tiny inscriptions/ciphertexts, quantify how many alternative readings/keys fit equally well.

## Domains not reached

This was deliberately host-targeted rather than a general discovery sweep. It did not systematically search medieval continental Europe, East Asia, the Americas, economic history or non-British modern political history except where a candidate already intersected the hosts' known work.
