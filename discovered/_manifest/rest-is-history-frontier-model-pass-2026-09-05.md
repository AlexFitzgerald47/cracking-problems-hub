# The Rest Is History — frontier-model target pass — 2026-09-05

**Role:** Finder / host-fit scouting  
**Purpose:** Re-run the Tom Holland / Dominic Sandbrook target search under a much harder constraint: only retain problems a frontier model can plausibly attack end-to-end from digitally reachable evidence, with a reproducible crack-shaped result.

This is a scouting/ranking pass, not a claim or promotion. Existing Hub targets keep their current ownership. Newly surfaced targets need a full `discovered/<slug>/` problem pack and a fresh solution-status audit before a cracker claims them.

## Hard frontier-model gate

A target survives only if all are substantially true:

1. The named unknown is finite and specific.
2. The primary evidence is online now, or the corpus can realistically be assembled from public digital sources.
3. The decisive work can be done with reasoning, code, corpus analysis, image analysis, stylometry, graph/entity resolution or archival cross-linking.
4. Competing answers can lose against held-out evidence, controls, chronology or explicit constraints.
5. A successful output could be stated as a discovery, not merely a probability ranking.
6. The decisive fact does not fundamentally depend on an unreleased archive, physical object inspection, laboratory testing or a future witness.

# Tom Holland — strict top 5

## T1 — Cretan Hieroglyphic vs Linear A: independently reproduce or break the 2026 same-language claim

**Astra fit: 9.5/10**  
**Named unknown:** Do Cretan Hieroglyphic and Linear A actually encode the same language, as Brent Davis's 2026 Cambridge analysis argues?

**Why this survives:** This is unusually fresh, exact and computational. Davis explicitly treats the Cretan Hieroglyphic corpus as small and uses matched Cypriot Syllabic/Linear B controls. His published result says the two undeciphered scripts have an overwhelming probability of encoding the same language. That is a strong claim which can be independently reimplemented, stress-tested under sign-value uncertainty, corpus-size matching and held-out evaluation.

**Crack:** Reproduce the claimed signal under an independently coded pipeline and then either (a) validate it under stricter controls, or (b) identify a confound/null under which the signal disappears. A robust failure would be as publishable as a confirmation.

**First move:** Reconstruct Davis's corpus partitions and control experiment from primary inscription datasets; recover the known Linear B/Cypriot control before touching the disputed comparison.

**Source:** Brent Davis, *The Undeciphered Aegean Scripts*, ch. 9, Cambridge UP, online 20 Mar 2026, DOI 10.1017/9781009562331.010.

## T2 — Cypro-Minoan ↔ Eteocypriot: test the proposed language continuity

**Astra fit: 9.5/10**  
**Named unknown:** Is Eteocypriot a descendant/continuation of the language encoded by Cypro-Minoan?

**Why this survives:** Eteocypriot is written in a readable syllabary but remains an unknown indigenous language, making it a rare bridge between a partly constrained language and an undeciphered predecessor. Davis's 2026 chapter reports a strong same-language signal between Eteocypriot and Cypro-Minoan. The claim is fresh enough for a genuine independent replication rather than a literature review.

**Crack:** Reproduce or falsify the language-continuity signal using matched-size controls, alternative segmentation, sign-value uncertainty and held-out sequences. If it survives, derive concrete phonotactic/morphological predictions for Cypro-Minoan inscriptions not used in the test.

**First move:** Rebuild the Cypro-Minoan/Eteocypriot sequence corpora and repeat the known Greek control with Cypriot Syllabic/Linear B.

**Source:** Brent Davis, *The Undeciphered Aegean Scripts*, chs. 10–11, Cambridge UP, online 20 Mar 2026.

## T3 — Sidetic residual sign values after the 2025 corpus revision

**Astra fit: 9/10**  
**Named unknown:** What phonetic values should be assigned to the remaining doubtful Sidetic signs, especially signs 15 and 24, and what does the revised vowel inventory imply for Sidetic morphology?

**Why this survives:** This is a bounded decipherment problem with a newly revised open corpus. Gem Ferrer's 2025 work separates a previously conflated vowel sign and explicitly says other examples remain obscure and signs 15 and 24 remain very doubtful. New inscriptions from 2020–23 also changed the evidence base.

**Crack:** Infer candidate values from bilingual/proper-name/loanword/formula constraints and require the values to predict forms across inscriptions withheld from derivation. A successful value must beat competing assignments under matched nulls, not merely produce plausible words.

**First move:** Encode the 2025 revised corpus, freeze sign uncertainty, and validate the pipeline by recovering already-secure values from withheld bilingual/proper-name anchors.

**Sources:** Gem Ferrer, “A new Sidetic vowel. Splitting a misread sign into two,” *Kadmos* 64 (2025), 149–174, DOI 10.1515/kadmos-2025-0009; Zinko & Rizza, “Neue Inschriften aus Side,” *Kadmos* 64 (2025), 117–148.

## T4 — Camunic: identify the language relationship, or prove the current candidates fail

**Astra fit: 8.5/10**  
**Named unknown:** Is the Camunic language genetically related to any known neighbouring language family, and which repeated forms are genuine linguistic structure rather than unstable sign readings?

**Why this survives:** The Vienna Thesaurus states directly that Camunic has not been deciphered or convincingly connected to surrounding languages and that letter identification itself is a major problem. The same digital infrastructure provides inscriptions, sign/epigraphic metadata and comparative North Italic material.

**Crack:** Build a provenance-aware Camunic corpus, branch disputed sign readings, derive repeated morphology on one partition, and test candidate relationships (Raetic/Tyrsenian, Celtic/IE, other) on held-out inscriptions against contact- and length-aware nulls. A strong elimination result is acceptable if it closes major proposed relationships.

**First move:** Corpus audit and image-to-sign uncertainty ledger before any lexical matching.

**Source:** Thesaurus Inscriptionum Raeticarum, University of Vienna, `Script` / Camunic section and digital inscription database.

## T5 — Phaistos Disc vs Linear A: pressure-test the new 2026 same-language result

**Astra fit: 8/10**  
**Named unknown:** Does the Phaistos Disc text genuinely show the same language-level syllabotactic signal as Linear A, or is the 2026 result an artefact of a tiny single-object corpus?

**Why this survives:** It is not “decipher the Phaistos Disc by vibes.” The target is one explicit new statistical claim from Davis's 2026 Cambridge work. The claim is falsifiable and the severe short-text validation problem can itself be quantified.

**Crack:** Reproduce the claimed same-language signal, then quantify its power under matched pseudo-discs, segmentation variants, sign-frequency preserving nulls and equal search budgets. Either a robust independent replication or a demonstrated low-power/confounded result would materially change how the claim should be interpreted.

**First move:** Recover the known positive/negative control behaviour using exactly matched text lengths before scoring the Disc.

**Source:** Brent Davis, *The Undeciphered Aegean Scripts*, ch. 8, Cambridge UP, online 20 Mar 2026, DOI 10.1017/9781009562331.009.

# Dominic Sandbrook — strict top 5

## Why the list changed

The strongest model-native seam is not Buster Crabb / ELLI / Clockwork Orange. It is the **public VENONA London GRU corpus**: roughly 260 exploitable London↔Moscow GRU messages, with multiple source codenames still explicitly labelled unidentified. This gives a frontier model a finite primary corpus, network chronology, hard job/access clues, and independently searchable biographical records.

The goal is not to accuse living people or infer guilt from weak resemblance. These are deceased historical actors; any solve must be identity-by-constraint with independent corroboration.

## D1 — BARON: identify/adjudicate the British source of Enigma-derived intelligence

**Astra fit: 9.5/10**  
**Named unknown:** Who was the London GRU source codenamed BARON, described in VENONA as supplying material connected with British decryption of German Enigma traffic?

**Why this survives:** The NSA's VENONA history still describes BARON as unidentified. A 3 April 1941 cable says the information came solely from BARON, references ENIGMA and mentions the source's possible connection with Military Intelligence. Later writers have proposed Karel Sedlacek, but the identification is not treated as securely closed.

**Crack:** Translate every BARON mention into a constraint set (access, dates, contacts, locations, institutional pathway), enumerate plausible candidates, and show that one candidate uniquely predicts independent facts not used to select them — or decisively eliminate the Sedlacek hypothesis.

**First move:** Build the complete BARON message graph and distinguish direct access to decrypts from second-hand military-intelligence access.

**Sources:** NSA, *The VENONA Story*; Wilson Center, London GRU cable 649, 3 Apr 1941.

## D2 — BROWN/BRAUN: identify the source who leaked ASDIC information

**Astra fit: 9.5/10**  
**Named unknown:** Who was BROWN/BRAUN, the London GRU source who reported information obtained directly from the production line at Henry Hughes & Son in London concerning an ASDIC/echo-sounding instrument?

**Why this survives:** This is unusually constrained. The 4 September 1940 VENONA message explicitly labels BROWN unidentified, ties the source to Henry Hughes production-line information, and notes that BROWN is the only known agent outside the Embassy to have signed a London GRU message passed in the Embassy cipher by Embassy facilities.

**Crack:** Link company employment, engineering roles, Communist/GRU contacts, residence/network clues and the other BROWN messages to identify one historical person; validate against an independent personnel, security or contemporary intelligence record.

**First move:** Build the Henry Hughes 1939–41 employee/engineer candidate set before stylistic or network scoring.

**Source:** Wilson Center/NSA VENONA, London GRU No. 976, 4 Sep 1940, “BROWN'S REPORT TO SLANT ON ASDIC.”

## D3 — THERAPEUTIST: identify the airfield source reporting Battle of Britain intelligence

**Astra fit: 9/10**  
**Named unknown:** Who was THERAPEUTIST, the source reporting repeatedly from/about a British aerodrome during the Battle of Britain?

**Why this survives:** VENONA explicitly labels the covername unidentified. The messages contain unusually concrete operational clues: “his aerodrome,” aircraft trials, bombing damage, pilot morale, specific aircraft counts, and later documentary material on fighter-pilot training. That can be intersected with RAF station chronology and personnel/access records.

**Crack:** Infer the aerodrome and role from the reports, derive the small set of people with repeated access to the observed information, and identify the source using an independent network/biographical record.

**First move:** Geolocate the aerodrome from the Dutch-guard detail, aircraft types/trials, bombing chronology and other dated observations before searching names.

**Source:** Wilson Center/NSA VENONA London GRU corpus, including London Nos. 882, 903 and 1107 (1940); translator notes explicitly say “THERAPEUTIST: Unidentified covername.”

## D4 — MINISTER: identify the government/diplomatic GRU source and his network

**Astra fit: 9/10**  
**Named unknown:** Who was MINISTER/MINISTR, the London GRU source whose Russian covername is specifically noted as governmental/diplomatic and who appears to have led or participated in a recruitment group?

**Why this survives:** The primary corpus explicitly says MINISTER is unidentified and notes that the Russian word is used only in a governmental or diplomatic sense. Moscow instructed London to increase MINISTER's group by recruiting staff of the military department; later traffic links MINISTER with other source/radio-network activity.

**Crack:** Convert the entire MINISTER trail into biographical/institutional constraints and identify a unique official/diplomatic candidate whose job, contacts and chronology predict the later messages.

**First move:** Extract every MINISTR occurrence and build a temporal network before consulting candidate biographies.

**Source:** Wilson Center/NSA VENONA London GRU corpus; e.g. London No. 766, 15 Jul 1940 and associated translator comments.

## D5 — POULTRY-DEALER/KURNIK: identify the political-network source

**Astra fit: 8.5/10**  
**Named unknown:** Who was POULTRY-DEALER/KURNIK, an unidentified GRU source embedded in British Communist/left political networks and reporting both clandestine-radio security and military/political information?

**Why this survives:** Four public London messages repeatedly label the source unidentified. The source knew an Area Secretary of the Communist Party, assessed BROWN's reliability, discussed operational security around STANLEY and a clandestine radio, and reported a proposal involving Tom Wintringham and a Republican anti-Franco group. Those social-network constraints are far richer than a bare cryptonym.

**Crack:** Reconstruct the CPGB/International Brigade/GRU contact graph in 1940 and identify the one person fitting the dated contacts, geography and intelligence access; demand independent corroboration outside VENONA.

**First move:** Enumerate people in the relevant CPGB area / Wintringham / International Brigade networks and score them only after all message-derived constraints are frozen.

**Source:** Wilson Center/NSA VENONA London GRU Nos. 876, 882, 1112 and 1141 (1940); translator comments explicitly mark POULTRY-DEALER unidentified.

# Recommended opening bets

## Tom: Cretan Hieroglyphic ↔ Linear A

This is the cleanest frontier-model target of the whole pass: a major **2026** scholarly claim, explicit control experiment, finite corpus, and a result that can be independently confirmed or broken. An email saying “we independently replicated the 2026 Cambridge claim and found it fails/survives under these stronger held-out controls” is substantially stronger than another speculative decipherment.

## Dominic: BARON first; BROWN second

BARON has the highest historical upside because of the possible Enigma connection. BROWN may be the more tractable identity problem because the Henry Hughes production-line clue sharply narrows the candidate universe. A successful result would be a genuine identification of a previously unresolved Soviet source in wartime Britain, not merely a new theory about an already famous mystery.

## Meta-observation

The frontier-model filter changes target selection dramatically. The best targets are often **recent claims or unresolved identities embedded in structured public corpora**, not the most famous historical mysteries. They offer the model something it is unusually good at: exhaustively joining clues that were previously scattered across hundreds of pages and then testing the resulting hypothesis against evidence withheld from the search.
