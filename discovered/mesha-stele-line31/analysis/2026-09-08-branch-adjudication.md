# Mesha line 31 — branch adjudication

**Session:** 2026-09-08, GPT-5.6 Sol  
**Question:** What can the damaged sequence after `WHWRNN YŠB BH` actually support?

## Result in one line

Freeze the physical evidence as **`B ? ? W D`**, with a probable but disputed divider after the final `D` and **no secure original divider before `W`**. On that frozen object, **`BTDWD` is the strongest reconstruction**, but the middle `T` and first `D` are not secure enough to call the whole word an observed reading. The specific **Balak (`BLQ`) identification should be rejected as an epigraphic decipherment**: neither `L` nor `Q` is observed, and its branch depends on a disputed divider plus contextual selection from several possible `B??` names.

This is a branch-level crack, not a full solve claim.

That is more constrained than the generic “unreadable lacuna,” but less certain than printing `BTDWD` without qualification.

## The decisive branch error: the divider before W

Finkelstein, Na'aman & Römer's 2019 branch requires a vertical sentence divider immediately before `W`. That divider does **not** survive as an unambiguous stroke on the original stone at that position. Langlois notes that the conspicuous full vertical is in the plaster reconstruction; its claimed ancient counterpart must therefore come from the damaged squeeze/small original fragment. His RTI inspection further identifies the apparent pseudo-descender near this area as hollow rather than embossed in the manner expected from an original incision.

This matters because their entire geometry is:

`B ? ? | W D ...`

rather than:

`B ? ? W D`

The first geometry is not established by the object. It is an interpretation of damaged secondary evidence.

I do **not** elevate the opposite claim (“there definitely was no divider”) to fact. The correct result is narrower: **the divider cannot carry the burden of splitting the five-position sequence.**

## Why “Balak” specifically falls

Finkelstein, Na'aman & Römer themselves list several possible `B??` personal names before selecting Balak. The two letters required for `BLQ` are not read from surviving strokes; the paper explicitly says those letters are eroded. Balak is then preferred because of a proposed historical memory preserved in Numbers 22–24 and the geography of Horonaim.

That makes **Balak a contextual restoration, not a decipherment**. It has four stacked assumptions:

1. the disputed divider before `W` is real;
2. the erased two positions form a three-consonant royal name with initial `B`;
3. those unseen consonants are specifically `LQ` rather than another `B??` name;
4. the much later Balaam narrative preserves this ruler's name.

The object independently supplies only the initial `B` for that identification. The specific `BLQ` claim therefore does not survive an OBSERVED/INFERRED audit and should leave the serious epigraphic candidate set unless new strokes appear.

**Important:** this rejects *Balak specifically*, not every possible personal-name reconstruction.

## The leap: rank the remaining single-unit readings only after the freeze

Once the unsupported split before `W` is removed, the natural search object is a five-position unit `B??WD`.

`BTDWD` gains three independent advantages:

1. **Glyph compatibility.** T is Langlois' best account of the crossing traces at position 2; D is his best account of the triangular trace at position 3. Neither is secure, but both point in the same direction.
2. **Boundary compatibility.** No word divider is visible between `BT` and `DWD`; a probable divider follows the final D. This is exactly the shape expected for one bounded expression.
3. **Held-out external anchor.** The ninth-century BCE Tel Dan inscription independently attests the cognate dynastic expression `BYTDWD` (“House of David”) without an internal divider. This parallel was not used to decide which physical strokes exist; it is applied only after the image freeze.

The strongest live alternative inside the same geometry is **`BNDWD`**, because N was among the shape possibilities and could yield something like “son of DWD.” But the missing N descender weakens it, and as a standalone subject “son of David” without an individual's name is less naturally motivated than the independently attested dynastic formula. Other `B??WD` lexical/name solutions remain logically possible but currently lack a comparable external anchor.

So the ranking after this pass is:

1. **`BTDWD` — “House of David”**: best reconstruction; not fully observed.
2. **other single-unit `B??WD`**, especially `BNDWD`: live but weaker.
3. **a `B??` personal name followed by a new `W...` clause**: physically possible only if the disputed divider is genuine; disfavored.
4. **`BLQ` / Balak specifically**: unsupported as an epigraphic reading; reject pending new evidence.

## Syntax does not rescue either camp

The inscription itself uses `YŠB` elsewhere with an explicit subject (`Omri`, the men of Gad, the king of Israel). Line 31's topicalized Horonaim construction is unusual enough that word order cannot safely identify the damaged subject. Na'aman's wider corpus argument—that a kingdom name is rarely an operative subject—counts against the dynastic reading, but rarity is not a physical contradiction. Conversely, biblical geography cannot supply two invisible consonants for Balak.

Syntax is therefore a secondary weight, not the deciding instrument here.

## Preregistered outward falsifiers

A future raw RTI / 3D / photometric-stereo pass should test these without lexical labels visible to the adjudicator:

1. **Position 2 (T):** two crossing incision channels should persist under independent illumination directions and show channel geometry comparable to unquestioned taws elsewhere on the stele. If not, demote T.
2. **Position 3 (D):** the alleged triangular strokes must be separable from the squeeze fold/wrinkle and reproduce on the contact face or another independent squeeze. If the long diagonal is entirely the wrinkle, demote D.
3. **Pre-W divider:** a real sentence divider must exhibit original-incision morphology independent of the plaster restoration and squeeze damage. If it does, reopen the `B?? | W...` branch.
4. **Post-D divider:** its local topography should cluster with unquestioned circular word dividers rather than cracks/irregular depressions. Confirmation would strongly favor one bounded `B??WD` word.

## Bottom line

There is not enough material evidence to announce that line 31 has been *read* as `BTDWD` with five secure letters. There **is** enough to improve the state of the problem:

- stop treating Balak as a rival reading—it is a conjectural historical identification;
- stop treating the pre-W sentence divider as an observed fact;
- freeze the recoverable sequence as **`B??WD`** unless/until that divider is independently demonstrated;
- within that sequence, carry **`BTDWD` / “House of David” as the leading reconstruction**, explicitly marking T and the first D as uncertain.

That is the frontier this session can defend from the available evidence without converting wishful tracing into inscription.
