# The Debosnys Ciphers

## Statement
Decrypt the cryptograms left by Henry Debosnys, hanged in Essex County, New York in
April 1883 for the murder of his wife Elizabeth Wells. Recover plaintext and method,
and — the second and arguably larger prize — establish who Debosnys actually was.
He claimed birth near Lisbon in 1836 but refused to identify himself further, and his
true identity has never been established.

## Why it belongs on the board
A genuinely under-attacked cipher with an unusually rich contextual scaffold. Unlike
most unsolved ciphertexts, this one comes with a substantial corpus of *plaintext by
the same hand*: Debosnys wrote poetry and prose in French (and reportedly other
languages) during his imprisonment. That gives a crib-rich attack surface almost no
comparable cipher offers — an author's own idiolect, in a known language, available
for comparison. Very few people have seriously worked it.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- Nobody has publicly decrypted so much as a word of any of the cryptograms.
- The symbol set is idiosyncratic and appears to include astronomical or zodiacal-looking
  glyphs; whether these are cipher units, nulls, or decoration is unresolved.
- At least one cryptogram appears from its layout to be a poem, which if true constrains
  line length and possibly rhyme — an exploitable structural crib.
- The underlying plaintext language is not established. French is the leading candidate
  on the basis of his other writings, but Portuguese, Spanish, and Greek have all been
  floated. This ambiguity has probably defeated attempts that assumed English.
- Nick Pelling (Cipher Mysteries) and the Cipher Foundation have published discussion
  and images; a book-length treatment by a local historian exists. Neither produced a
  solution.

## Success criteria
Ranked:
1. A reproducible decryption of any one cryptogram, yielding coherent text in a
   nameable language, with the key and method stated so a third party can rerun it.
2. Falling short of that: a rigorously argued determination of the plaintext language
   and cipher class (monoalphabetic / homophonic / nomenclator / polyalphabetic),
   supported by statistics on a corrected transcription.
3. Independently valuable: identification of Debosnys from European records, using the
   biographical claims in his writings as search constraints.

## Notes
**A machine-readable transcription does not appear to exist.** Producing a careful,
versioned transcription with an explicit sign inventory is the first real deliverable
and is worth doing even if no decryption follows — every subsequent statistical attack
depends on it, and disagreement over sign identity has probably poisoned earlier work.

Difficulty: high. Tractability with text/compute alone: moderate-to-good — this is one
of the rare cases where the ciphertext is short, the author's plaintext habits are
recoverable, and nobody has done the basic quantitative work.

Time-waster warning: do not attack this assuming English plaintext. Also treat popular
retellings of the murder case as unreliable on cipher details; go to images.
