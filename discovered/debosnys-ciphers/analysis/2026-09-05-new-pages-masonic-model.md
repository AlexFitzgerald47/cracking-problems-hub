## Primary-image correction: the famous `516` is actually `5/6`

On cryptogram #2a / manuscript page “No. 9”, the token in the second cipher line has sometimes been discussed online as **516**. The new scan resolves the middle stroke unambiguously as a diagonal slash: the token is **`5/6`**, not `516`.

This matters because a 2015 Cipher Mysteries comment explicitly proposed “516” as a starting point. Any interpretation that depends on the integer 516 should therefore be retired.

Source for the prior 516 reading:
- https://ciphermysteries.com/2015/11/07/thoughts-on-the-debosnys-ciphers

Immediately above this area is a wire-frame cube-like drawing carrying visible `6` and `5` face labels (and a top-face stroke that may be `1`, but that reading is less secure), beside a pitcher and small glass. The cube and `5/6` may be related, but the image does **not** establish how; this should remain an open structural clue rather than be converted into numerology.

## `H.D.D.L.M.F.` is a genuine internal crib-like construction

The same #2a scan sharply confirms the plaintext initials embedded inside the cipher:

`H. D. D. L. M. F.`

Each initial carries a run of short marks beneath it. Nick Pelling's earlier count was `H[4] D[8] D[7] L[6] M[6] F[5]`, naturally read as the first letter plus the number of omitted letters. This gives:

- H + 4 = a 5-letter word → **Henry**;
- first D + 8 = a 9-letter word → **Deletnack** fits Debosnys' own jail spelling;
- second D + 7 = an 8-letter word → **Debosnys**;
- L + 6 = unknown 7-letter word;
- M + 6 = unknown 7-letter word;
- F + 5 = unknown 6-letter word.

The high-resolution image is consistent with that construction. Therefore `LMF` is constrained to a **7 / 7 / 6** expansion if the rule stays constant.

This does *not* solve LMF. `MASONIC` (7) and `FELLOW` (6) happen to fit the final two lengths, but no defensible seven-letter `L...` completion has been established. An old web suggestion “Looking / Masonry / Friend” also fits 7/7/6 but is grammatically and evidentially weak and should not be treated as a result.

## Stronger model-class lead: a Masonic *composite* cipher, not pigpen

The new pages make a Masonic model class more useful than the vague statement “some symbols look Masonic.” There is independent pre-existing evidence:

1. Craig Bauer reports that Debosnys repeatedly drew the square-and-compasses inverted and wrote that his son “will not be initiated into the first degree.”
2. The clasped-hands drawing beside `L.M.F.` at the foot of cryptogram #2b has been identified by Bauer as the **Boaz / Entered Apprentice grip**.
3. Stephen Brent Morris — NSA cryptanalyst, specialist in fraternal cryptography, and a 33° Mason — told Bauer that the Debosnys cipher looks like a typical Masonic cipher because of its discrete mysterious symbols.
4. The **Folger Manuscript** (Robert Folger, 1827) provides a much closer structural analogue than ordinary pigpen: letters and some common words are represented by simple shapes that are then **combined/nested into larger composite hieroglyph-like word forms**, allowing multiple visual realizations. That is strikingly consonant with Sektu's independent finding that Debosnys' whitespace-bounded glyphs decompose into an ordered inventory of subglyphs.

Sources:
- Craig P. Bauer, *Unsolved!* chapter 5 (online text mirror): https://dokumen.pub/unsolved-the-history-and-mystery-of-the-worlds-greatest-ciphers-from-ancient-egypt-to-online-secret-societies-9781400884797.html
- California Freemason, “The Code Breakers”: https://californiafreemason.org/2020/08/28/the-code-breakers/
- Sektu transcription revision: https://sektu.blogspot.com/2017/08/debosnys-cipher-transcription-revision.html

**Important limit:** resemblance of *method class* does not imply Debosnys used Folger's exact key. The correct experiment is to compare composition rules and crib behavior, not visually force Folger letter values onto Debosnys.

## New specific hypothesis: cryptogram #2a may deliberately invoke Fellow Craft / Liberal-Arts material

Page #2a contains, in close proximity, several literal or near-literal motifs:

- `5/6` — unmistakably numerical / arithmetic;
- a numbered cube-like solid — naturally geometric;
- a clear sun motif — naturally astronomical;
- one clef-like / musical-looking mark elsewhere in the page — **ambiguous**, because similar shapes also occur as ordinary cipher subglyphs.

This cluster is interesting because the historical Masonic **Fellow Craft** lecture explicitly groups the Seven Liberal Arts and Sciences as Grammar, Rhetoric, Logic, **Arithmetic, Geometry, Music, Astronomy**. Albert Mackey's *Manual of the Lodge* was entered for copyright in 1868/1870, before Debosnys' 1882–83 jail writings, and its Fellow Craft section describes precisely those four mathematical/scientific arts together. It also explicitly moves Geometry from point → line → surface → solid and discusses astronomy, globes, music, and numerical symbolism.

Primary period source:
- Albert G. Mackey, *Manual of the Lodge*, Fellow Craft degree: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Manual_of_the_Lodge/System_of_Monitorial_Instruction/Fellow_Craft%27s_Degree
- title-page/copyright history: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Manual_of_the_Lodge

### Why this is not yet a finding

The motif order on Debosnys' page is not the canonical Seven-Arts order, the music identification is not secure, and sun/cube/numbers are generic enough to occur outside Masonry. Therefore “this page encodes the Fellow Craft lecture” would be an overclaim.

### Falsifiable prediction

Treat the Liberal-Arts reading as live only if one of the following can be shown from a frozen transcription/image segmentation:

1. the remaining page structure produces credible counterparts for **Grammar / Rhetoric / Logic** in addition to the apparent quadrivium motifs; or
2. the pictorial anchors occur in a sequence that aligns significantly better to a period Fellow Craft lecture than to unrelated symbolic texts; or
3. the cipher immediately surrounding one of the anchors can be decoded under an independently derived compositional key to a corresponding Masonic term.

If none of those happens, downgrade the quadrivium resemblance to coincidence.

## Cryptogram #3 / French plaintext page

The supplied page is canonical **Cryptogram #3**, not #2b. It contains four cipher lines followed by a much longer same-hand French poem beginning approximately `Oh! mes amis je vous supplie en grace...`.

This is useful as same-author linguistic control, but it is not a direct line-for-line crib: four cipher lines cannot straightforwardly encode the entire visible French poem. Sektu independently transcribed this cipher block and reported that its symbol frequency/inventory differs materially from the cipher poem, leading him to suspect the same writing system is being used for at least two different languages.

## Current model ranking after these images

1. **Composite / subglyph-based code or shorthand**, likely with more than one plaintext language — strongest structural model.
2. **Masonic/fraternal composite system or a personal system influenced by one** — substantially strengthened as a model class; exact key unknown.
3. **Phonetic/syllabic composite system** — still live from Sektu's ordered subglyph evidence and the rhyme behavior in cryptogram #4.
4. **Pasigraphic / nomenclator-like system** — still plausible; 19th-century composite pasigraphies are a real comparison class.
5. **Simple one-symbol-per-letter substitution / pigpen** — poor fit to the 425 whole-glyph types and internal compositional grammar.
