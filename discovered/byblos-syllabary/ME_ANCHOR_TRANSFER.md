# Byblos `ME` anchor transfer — first held-out core recurrence

## Result in one sentence

When the externally proposed cylinder value ` = me` is frozen **before** inspecting the Dunand/core corpus, every one of the three clear non-cylinder occurrences of the exact `` form is followed by the same sign ``, and two of the three continue with the identical third sign ``:

- BYBL i IX 5: `...  ...`
- BYBL k V 4: `...  ...`
- BYBL m II 3: `...  ...`

Thus the external `ME` anchor does not scatter randomly when transferred into the core corpus: it lands in one repeated adjacency family, `{,}`. This is the first useful leave-the-cylinder-out structural prediction found in the Hub pass.

It is **not** yet a translation.

---

## 1. Why this is a legitimate held-out test

Mäder's 2021 proposal of `me` comes entirely from the Amarna-family cylinder (`BYBL rb/rc`): the two externally identified daughter names Meketaton and Meritaton both begin with `me`, and the corresponding Byblos inscriptions begin with mirrored/allographic forms including exact ``.

Mäder then lists the exact non-cylinder occurrences of this form in a footnote: only three — BYBL i IX 5, BYBL k V 4 and BYBL m II 3 — and explicitly warns that this scarcity prevents treating the value as confirmed.

The new observation comes from returning to the public OCBI raw transcription **without assigning a language or translation** and inspecting those three held-out loci.

The three contexts are:

### BYBL i, line IX

`...  ...`

### BYBL k, line V

`... `

### BYBL m, line II

`...  ...`

All three use exact ``, not an uncertain/guessed substitute, and all three immediately share `` on the same side. Two share the full trigraph ``.

Because the OCBI source stores the inscriptions with direction metadata and some publications normalize reading direction differently, this note deliberately calls the relation **adjacency** rather than prematurely calling `` a suffix, prefix or second syllable of a word.

---

## 2. Cross-object recurrence makes it more interesting

This is not three repetitions on one damaged object.

- BYBL i is one of Dunand's bronze spatula inscriptions.
- BYBL k and BYBL m are among the later-published stone fragments/slabs added by Dunand in 1978.

So the exact `` recurrence crosses **medium** (bronze vs stone), **object**, and **publication cohort**. That reduces the chance that the pattern is merely an idiosyncratic ligature, one scribe's habit, or a local transcription artefact.

It also answers one of the concerns in Mäder 2021 in a more useful way than raw frequency. The exact `` form is rare, but its rare core uses are unusually coherent.

---

## 3. What this supports — and what it does not

### Supported

1. **Exact `` behaves as a coherent graphemic form in the core corpus.** Its three clear held-out uses all enter the same adjacency family.
2. **The cylinder-to-core transfer is no longer completely untested.** The externally proposed `ME` sign predicts a repeated structural environment in material not used to derive the value.
3. **`` is now a priority candidate word/morpheme/formula unit.** It appears intact on two different core inscriptions and `` is a close third member.
4. **Do not propagate `me` indiscriminately to every form merged with `` in older OCBI syllabaries.** The exact form has a distinctive core distribution. Mirrored `` remains externally motivated on the cylinder, but broader allograph groups need their own distributional tests.

### Not supported

- `` has **not** been given a phonetic value.
- `` and `` have **not** been shown to be variants, case endings, or a paradigm.
- the location of a word boundary has **not** been established.
- the sequence has **not** been translated.
- three tokens are too few for a strong asymptotic significance claim, and the common follower was noticed after examining the contexts, so no post-hoc p-value is presented.

---

## 4. The tempting leap: `melek` / "king" — rejected for now

A three-sign sequence beginning with externally proposed `ME`, recurring on inscriptions from Byblos, naturally tempts a Northwest-Semitic reader to see a reflex of *mlk* “king”. That would be a spectacular anchor if true.

I am **not promoting it**.

Reasons:

1. The historical Northwest-Semitic form is normally reconstructed with a consonantal skeleton *mlk* and forms such as *malku*; a surface `me-le-k` segmentation would import a later-looking vocalization without evidence.
2. We have no external value for ``, `` or ``.
3. Mäder's own methodological critique correctly warns that repeated sign patterns become fake paradigms very easily before word boundaries are known.
4. The three core contexts do not yet carry externally identified royal names or titles that would independently predict “king”.

Status: **useful hypothesis generator, zero credit as a reading until an independent context predicts the title.**

A future discovery of `` beside a securely identifiable royal name/title would immediately reopen it.

---

## 5. Stronger interpretation of the result

The important advance is not the guessed language. It is the shape of the search space.

Before this pass:

` = me` was a one-object proper-name proposal with three sparse off-cylinder sightings.

After this pass:

` = me` is a one-object external proposal whose **entire exact-form held-out core distribution collapses into one adjacency family**:

`ME – X – {Y, Z}`

with two independent objects sharing `ME – X – Y` exactly.

That is enough to justify treating `{,}` as a frontier target rather than searching the whole corpus blindly.

---

## 6. Next kill test

Do not translate the sequence yet. Test its boundaries.

For each occurrence, use the OCBI/word-boundary machinery and the surrounding repeated n-grams to ask whether boundaries repeatedly fall:

A. before `` and after `/` — supporting a three-sign lexical unit;
B. inside `...` — weakening the lexical-unit idea;
C. after `` — suggesting `ME` itself is an independent morpheme/sign;
D. nowhere consistently — suggesting the recurrence is structural but not word-level.

Only after one boundary model survives should language be spent on the unit.

A second falsifier is the proposed `ME` allograph set. The exact `` form occurs once outside the cylinder, in BYBL f recto, but the following position is unreadable/uncertain. New or better imaging that resolves that follower is disproportionately valuable: if `` truly behaves as the same phonetic sign as ``, it should eventually participate in compatible structural environments rather than an unrelated distribution.
