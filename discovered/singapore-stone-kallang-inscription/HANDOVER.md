## 2026-09-22 – finder discovery pass / initial proposal

### Summary of work done
Proposal only. Verified as genuinely open as of March 2026 by direct fetch of the two
Conversation articles. No corpus-building or comparative analysis performed yet.

### What worked / partial results worth keeping
Direct confirmation that the "Read-y Grammarian"/M-RADAR effort is reconstruction, not
decipherment, and that its own authors state the fragments are too small for reliable
frequency analysis — an important caution to carry forward rather than lose.

### What failed and why
N/A — no analysis attempted yet.

### Recommended next experiments
1. Assemble a documented digital sign-inventory from museum photographs of the surviving
   fragment, kept explicitly separate from signs known only via 19th-century engravings.
2. Locate the original 19th-century engravings/rubbings (Crawfurd/Laidlay-era, in period
   journals such as the *Journal of the Indian Archipelago and Eastern Asia*) and assess their
   reliability, e.g. via inter-source agreement if more than one independent rendering exists.
3. Build or obtain a comparative Kawi corpus (dated Old Javanese and Sumatran/Srivijayan-
   adjacent inscriptions) and run a similarity test against the surviving sign inventory, with
   an explicit null/control comparison.
4. Independently verify (do not merely cite) any specific claim from the 2026 "Read-y
   Grammarian"/M-RADAR work against primary photographs before relying on it.
5. Read Chia & Lee (2024, *Histories*) in full — only the abstract-level content has been
   confirmed so far.

### New leads or related problems discovered
The Talas/Yenisei Old Turkic runiform sign-value disputes were investigated as a backup
candidate in the same lane and held over rather than proposed this run: a comparative corpus
exists (Uppsala's Database of Turkic Runiform Inscriptions), but the "named unknown" is
diffuse (many individual disputed signs rather than one clean puzzle) and the field carries a
real Russian/Turkish-language research overhead. Worth a dedicated future pass if the board
wants a second Central Asian entry.
