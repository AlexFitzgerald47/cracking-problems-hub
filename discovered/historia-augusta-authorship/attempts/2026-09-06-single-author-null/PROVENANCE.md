# Corpus provenance and known defects

`data/segments.json` is not committed: it is 14 MB of token streams and is
fully regenerated by `code/fetch_sources.sh` followed by
`code/build_corpus.py`. `data/manifest.csv` records what it contains.

Both HA witnesses were reached through GitHub mirrors. Direct access to
perseus.tufts.edu, thelatinlibrary.com, archive.org and la.wikisource.org was
denied by this session's egress policy; `raw.githubusercontent.com` was not.

## Known defects

**The Latin Library's *Alexander Severus* is truncated.** It carries 483 tokens
and 3 chapters; the vita has 68 chapters and is the longest in the collection.
Any analysis using the Latin Library text of the HA must exclude it or will
silently lose the largest single life. It is excluded from E1 and is not used
anywhere else — the Perseus text is the primary witness throughout.

The Latin Library's *Tacitus* reports a maximum chapter number of 13 against
the expected 17. Not investigated further, since the Latin Library text is not
used for any result; flagged here for the next agent.

## Normalisation

Lowercased, accents stripped, non-alphabetic characters removed, `v`→`u` and
`j`→`i`. The fold is necessary because the two witnesses disagree on
consonantal-u orthography; anything surviving it is a feature both witnesses
agree on. TEI `<note>`, `<bibl>`, `<head>`, `<ref>`, `<cit>`, `<gap>` and
`<orig>` are dropped before tokenising, because the Perseus files carry English
editorial notes inline and leaving them in would inject English function words.

A second token stream per segment (`tokens_nq`) additionally drops `<q>`,
which the Perseus/Loeb encoding uses for quoted letters, speeches and
senatorial acclamations. This is what makes the register control in E6
possible.

## Validation performed

See `results/e1_witness_check.txt`. Across the 29 comparable lives, the same
vita in the two witnesses is 13.2× closer in 200-MFW feature space than two
different vitae in the same witness, with zero overlap between the two
distributions. Token counts agree to within 15% for every life. The features
used downstream are properties of the text, not of the digitisation.
