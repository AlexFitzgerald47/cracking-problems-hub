# Ormond–Anglesey partially ciphered correspondence (1663–64)

## Statement
Identify and, if still unread, decipher the specific ciphertext passages in this Irish-connected correspondence. A genuine crack is a transcription and reading that explain the full target passage, its cipher mechanism and independent controls in other letters or contemporaneous keys. **Discovery status only:** modern unsolved status and the exact ciphertext still require the source audit below.

## Corpus availability
No verified machine-readable ciphertext is in this pack. A digitised calendar or scholarly edition exists as indicated below; original images and full transcription have not been secured. First deliverable: an exact dated letter and a source-checked ciphertext/solution ledger.

## Why it belongs on the board
A historical cipher inventory identifies many partially enciphered letters between the Duke of Ormond, Lord Lieutenant of Ireland, and the Earl of Anglesey, Vice-Treasurer, in 1663–64. Its search-index excerpt identifies cipher assignments E=13/14 and THE=246, and flags an undeciphered ciphertext in a calendar volume 5, p. 498. Direct retrieval of that inventory and inspection of the exact calendar page failed in this pass; whether this cited passage belongs to the Ormond–Anglesey system needs verification.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
The source explicitly describes coded or undeciphered material, but a historical cataloguer's 'undeciphered' does not guarantee that a later historian or cryptanalyst has not solved it. Search adjacent letters, key sheets and editions before cryptanalysis. This candidate is separate from the hub's twentieth-century Kennedy and IRA traffic.

## Success criteria
1. Verify the exact letter(s), original or published ciphertext, source reference, and whether a key or plaintext survives anywhere else.
2. If genuinely open, decrypt at least one nontrivial passage with a consistent key or method.
3. Validate against a different passage, a parallel letter or a securely dated external document; reject context-only guesses.
