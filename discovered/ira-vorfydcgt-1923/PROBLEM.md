# IRA `VORFYDCGT` message (25 October 1923)

## Statement
Determine what the nine-letter coded token `VORFYDCGT` means in the sentence:

> Can any of 100's methods be used now that no VORFYDCGT?

The sentence occurs in a 25 October 1923 copy memo from the IRA Director of Intelligence, preserved in the Ernie O'Malley Papers at the National Library of Ireland, call number **MS 10,973/15/24**.

The immediate problem is not to force a nine-letter ciphertext into readable English. It is to reconstruct enough of the IRA Director of Intelligence's 1923 coding context — especially the identity/function of "100", the relevant "methods", contemporary code words, safe-address systems, and parallel traffic — that competing plaintext or codeword interpretations make falsifiable predictions.

## Why it belongs on the board
- **Named unknown:** the meaning/function of a specific surviving coded token.
- **Breakthrough-shaped:** recovering the meaning would decode a concrete item of IRA intelligence correspondence.
- **Primary evidence attackable:** the NLI catalogue gives the exact token, date, office, archival reference and surrounding sentence; related 1923 intelligence-dispatch catalogue records are publicly searchable. The physical memo itself is not currently confirmed as digitised.
- **Falsifiable:** candidate readings can be tested against parallel IRA intelligence vocabulary, repeated codewords, archival context, the known 1920s IRA transposition system, and any surviving keys/plaintexts.
- **Historically interesting:** this is operational intelligence correspondence from the immediate post-Civil-War period.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- National Library of Ireland catalogue record `vtls000655021` describes the 25 October 1923 one-page memo and transcribes the coded sentence exactly. The item is in the Ernie O'Malley Papers, **MS 10,973/15/24**.
- The catalogue describes nearby 1923 Anti-Treaty intelligence dispatches that refer to continued use of an "existing code word" and requests for coded safe-address lists. This establishes a live code/codeword environment around the same office and year.
- Thomas Mahon and James J. Gillogly's *Decoding the IRA* (2008) cracked large numbers of later 1920s IRA secret communications, principally transposition material. No published hit for `VORFYDCGT` has been located in the first verification pass; absence of a web hit is not proof it is unsolved.
- Nine letters are intrinsically low-power. `VORFYDCGT` contains nine distinct letters, so a simple monoalphabetic substitution cannot be constrained from internal repetitions. If it is a pure transposition, the plaintext must be an anagram of those nine letters; ordinary English candidates are therefore severely restricted.
- The grammar around the token matters. The catalogue's transcription is exactly: `Can any of 100's methods be used now that no VORFYDCGT?`

## Success criteria
### Full crack
A plaintext/codeword interpretation supported by independent evidence such as a key, parallel message, codebook, repeated use with recoverable referent, or archival plaintext/ciphertext pair.

### Significant progress
Any of:
1. identify who/what `100` denotes and what "100's methods" were;
2. classify `VORFYDCGT` as a transposition, substitution, codeword, enciphered codeword, or transcription artefact using external evidence;
3. locate parallel occurrences of the token or related codewords;
4. narrow the semantic slot to a small falsifiable candidate set;
5. prove a tempting cipher family impossible or statistically uninformative at this length.

## Key sources & starting points

- NLI 1923 Anti-Treaty intelligence dispatch records, including Michael Carolan material noting an "existing code word" and requests for coded safe addresses.

## Notes
Do not treat a plausible nine-letter English output as evidence by itself. Context reconstruction and parallel traffic carry far more discriminating power than language scoring here.
