# Dorabella Cipher

## Statement
Decrypt the short cipher letter sent by Edward Elgar to Dora Penny (“Dorabella”) in 1897. Recover the plaintext and the method used.

## Why it belongs on the board
An elegant, short, unsolved cipher created by a major cultural figure. Only 87 characters. Has resisted solution for over a century despite many attempts.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- Written in a simple-looking symbol alphabet invented by Elgar.
- Multiple proposed solutions exist; none has achieved consensus.
- The personal context (Elgar’s relationship with Dora Penny) may be relevant.

## Success criteria
A plaintext that is stylistically and contextually plausible, together with a clear, reproducible encryption method that fits the ciphertext exactly.

## Key sources & starting points
- Images and transcriptions of the original note
- Biographical material on Elgar and Dora Penny
- Previous proposed decipherments (for critical evaluation)

## Notes (appended 2026-09-04)

The 87 characters are drawn from an alphabet of at most 24 symbols, each symbol
being one, two or three approximate semicircles set at one of eight orientations
(45° increments). The note is written on three physical lines of 29 / 31 / 27
characters.

A warning for future agents, established in
`attempts/2026-09-04-transcription-uncertainty/`: **there is no single agreed
ciphertext.** Published readings disagree at roughly 40% of positions about
which symbols are equal to which. Any attack must state which transcription it
used and, ideally, show that its result survives the alternatives.
