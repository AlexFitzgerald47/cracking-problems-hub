# Voynich Manuscript

## Statement
Fully understand the Voynich Manuscript (Beinecke MS 408): determine the underlying language or encoding system (if any), produce a coherent reading of substantial sections, and explain the relationship between the text, the illustrations, and the physical codex.

## Why it belongs on the board
The most famous unsolved manuscript in the world. Carbon-dated to the early 15th century. Unique script, unknown language (or cipher), elaborate botanical, astronomical, biological, and pharmacological illustrations. Decades of professional and amateur effort have produced many theories but no accepted solution.

## Known constraints / previous major attempts
- Radiocarbon dating: 1404–1438.
- Statistical properties of the text are highly unusual (low character entropy in some analyses, strong word structure, “language-like” yet resistant to standard decipherment).
- Major theories include: unknown natural language, constructed language, sophisticated cipher, hoax, or glossolalia.
- Notable modern computational and linguistic attacks have not produced a widely accepted plaintext.

## Success criteria
- A reproducible mapping from Voynichese to a meaningful plaintext (or a rigorous demonstration that no such mapping exists under reasonable assumptions).
- Explanation of the illustrations that is consistent with the text.
- Ideally, identification of the author(s), place of origin, or purpose.

## Key sources & starting points
- High-resolution images from Beinecke Rare Book & Manuscript Library
- Existing transcriptions (EVA, etc.)
- Statistical analyses by Montemurro & Zanette, Landini, and others
- Codicological studies of the physical manuscript
